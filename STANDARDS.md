# Evidence standards anyone can apply

Version 0.1, 2026-10-08. Published research standards, open to criticism and revision.

1. **State the claim before explaining it.** Name the event, place, period, mechanism, expected trace, and observation that would count against it. Apply this to conventional and alternative accounts alike.
2. **Separate observation from interpretation.** Distinguish an object or measurement, a source's report, its authors' conclusion, and this investigation's inference. A below-ground window is an observation; its cause needs separate evidence.
3. **Keep provenance.** Record stable source URL/DOI, archive or specimen identifier, creator, creation/publication dates, accessed date, page/figure/section, and access depth. Original, transcription, translation, and catalog description remain distinct.
4. **Say what was actually inspected.** Use SCAN_INSPECTED, FULL_TEXT_PORTION, ABSTRACT, CATALOG_METADATA, SEARCH_EXCERPT, or NOT_ACCESSED. State inspected sections and failed access. Do not turn a search excerpt into a fully reviewed paper.
5. **Use original context.** Record locality, stratigraphic unit, elevation datum, excavation method, sample ID, materials, and chain of custody where available. Unknowns stay unknown; they are neither zero nor absence.
6. **Audit dates.** Record the measured object and dating method, raw result, uncertainty, calibration, timescale, and contamination/reworking/reuse risks. Do not equate a host layer's bound with an animal's exact death date.
7. **Compare specified alternatives.** Test engineered fill, subsidence, local flooding, erosion, redevelopment, map error/copying, climatic change, migration, and ordinary technological development when applicable. Both a conventional explanation and a catastrophe explanation must earn their fit.
8. **Do not count copies as independent evidence.** Build source lineages. A hundred maps descended from one template, repeated press releases, and shared dating calibrations cannot be counted as a hundred independent observations.
9. **Preserve adverse findings.** Keep failed predictions, contradictory dates, ordinary explanations, and excluded cases in the same release as supporting material. Record revisions without erasing earlier decisions.
10. **Predeclare selection.** Log search terms, inclusion/exclusion rules, control sites, and the sampling frame. This first batch is purposive and cannot estimate prevalence. A claim about 'many' needs a denominator.
11. **Check physical feasibility.** Account for water, sediment, energy, route, elevations, timing, preservation, and ecology. Illustrative equations are not simulations; an executed simulation is not validation without observations.
12. **Make missing evidence informative only when detection is justified.** An absence challenges a model only where it predicted a trace, suitable strata survive, sampling would find it, and detection limits are documented. Missing paperwork alone does not prove inheritance.
13. **Avoid conclusions about intent without evidence.** Error, simplification, renaming, reuse, appropriation, legal priority, and deliberate fabrication are different claims. Identify a mechanism from records; never invent actors or motives.
14. **Require independent challenge.** Provide the exact version of a case and its sources to a reviewer, record their role and conflicts, preserve their criticisms, and identify unresolved disagreements. Self-checking does not become independent review.
15. **Publish reproducibly and lawfully.** Release accessible methods, identifiers, permitted data/primary sources, translations with originals where rights allow, limitations, and hashes. Link restricted sources rather than falsely promising unrestricted copies.
16. **Use restrained outcomes.** Mark LEAD, SOURCED_DRAFT, READY_FOR_REVIEW, INDEPENDENTLY_REVIEWED, or RETIRED, and separately report what a case supports or challenges. Documented facts do not automatically validate a larger reconstruction.
17. **Tie promotion to evidence.** New museum or teaching copy must reflect reviewed findings and uncertainty. Correct the starting hypothesis whenever evidence warrants; no preferred story receives immunity.
18. **Protect people's actual histories.** Identify builders, workers, survivors, and cultures through sourced names and context. Do not overwrite documented communities with an inferred civilization.

## Minimum case record

Each sourced draft needs an ID, scoped question, locality, observed/reported evidence, source-linked claims with locators, documentary evidence, authors' interpretation, investigator inference, alternatives, adverse evidence, chronology limits, missing data, discriminating next action, and review status.

## Rejection and correction procedure

Record the exact version being tested and the adverse observation. Check source and measurement errors using the same standards applied to favorable observations. If the adverse result persists, narrow or reject the tested proposition; retain the earlier version and explain why it changed. A new hypothesis starts a new test, not a retroactive success.

## Review rubric

For each claim ask: Is the source authentic? Is the quoted/contextual meaning accurate? Is the measurement appropriate? Are date bounds respected? Are the alternatives actually compared? Are the observations independent? Could a different reader reproduce the inference? Does the conclusion exceed the evidence? Any failed item stays visible.
