# Golden Eagle bottle-date audit: an unresolved 1864 constraint

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York), S459. Selected scans inspected; no independent scientific review.

The [drain chronology audit](GOLDEN-EAGLE-DRAIN-CHRONOLOGY.md) retains the excavation author's reported1864 earliest-possible date for Layer56. Further inspection of the [report's glassware chapter](https://asc.sonoma.edu/historical-archaeology-golden-eagle-site) identifies a candidate bottle, but does not yet authenticate that date or the artifact-to-layer join.

## Candidate and unresolved join

Table10.2, printed10-4/PDF390, lists one D. S. & Co. soda bottle from **AreaIX**, with dates1861-1864. The descriptive entry, printed10-16/PDF402, identifies a complete cobalt-blue bottle, two-piece mold, blob top, concave base and slug-plate embossing. Plate10.9f and its caption, printed10-27/PDF418-419, depict and identify it. The entry cites **Markota and Markota1972:24**: John Delahanty, Michael Skelly and Co. operated Empire Soda Works in San Francisco from1861 until Skelly apparently left in1864.

This matches the broader excavation area of Features16/27. It does **not** establish that this is the unnamed whole bottle recovered from Layer56 in printed73/PDF105. Table10.2 gives area-level provenance, not a Layer56 identifier or accession number. The physical artifact and referenced1972publication have not been inspected independently.

Even if the join is later confirmed, the reported1861-1864 partnership interval does not by itself establish manufacture beginning in1864. An end-of-partnership year is not automatically an earliest-possible deposition year. Old bottles can remain in use or be redeposited. The discrepancy is therefore **unresolved**, not a demonstrated correction to1861, an1864 exact deposition date, or proof of a fabricated chronology. The Layer56 reported bound is preserved as a source assertion; independently accepted date remains null.

## Method and source dependence

The chapter introduction, printed10-1/PDF387, dates glass through embossing, form/color, catalogs, comparison specimens, advertisements, mold types, finishes and bases. These are artifact/historical reference methods, not direct absolute dating of flood sediment. Its distribution tables cover selected controlled features; they do not supply an exhaustive layer-level accession crosswalk. Printed10-18/PDF404 also relates assemblages to inferred business occupations. Such associations require testing separately from the identities and manufacture intervals of the artifacts.

Table10.2 lists a Hostetter bottle around1864 in **Feature15**, not AreaIX (printed10-5/PDF391). It cannot be substituted as the Layer56 bottle merely because its date agrees. Its description concerns fragments, whereas the Layer56 prose says a whole bottle. The report's separate locations and object descriptions must be retained.
