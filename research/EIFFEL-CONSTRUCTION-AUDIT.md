# Eiffel Tower: a checkable construction account

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C006 remains without independent review.

New evidence: [4 January 1889 verticality inspection](EIFFEL-VERTICALITY-REPORT.md) describes a roughly 220 m intermediate structure and an upper member not yet riveted, with survey limitations retained.

Follow-up: [archive survival and Koechlin recruitment](EIFFEL-ARCHIVE-FOLLOWUP.md) records the current archival search, a visually inspected 1879 letter, and an undated biography that must not inherit the dossier's catalog date.

Gustave Eiffel's 1900 technical monograph is accessible through [ETH-Bibliothek Zurich, Rar 9704](https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-28271). Two pages were visually inspected: the [personnel roster](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/zoom/8975936) and [printed page 225](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/zoom/8976172). This is a retrospective participant account. Its author had a direct interest in how the project was remembered.

The roster credits departments and people, including M. Koechlin for studies, Sauvestre for architecture, Letourneau for workshops, Martin and Saint-Martin for foundations/masonry, and Compagnon and Milon for metal assembly. These spellings and departmental placements were checked on the image; they are not a complete transcription or independent biographical identification. The OCR interleaves columns and should not determine roles without the image. These people are already named in an accessible historical source; this investigation has not discovered an erased workforce.

## Cost check

Page 225 reports the following principal subtotals, transcribed in [integer centimes](../data/eiffel-costs.json) to avoid rounding ambiguity:

| Printed category | Francs |
| --- | ---: |
| Infrastructure | 701,127.08 |
| Superstructure | 5,734,622.90 |
| Remaining overhead | 956,554.99 |
| Eiffel subtotal | 7,392,304.97 |
| Additional operating-company expenditure | 407,096.34 |
| Grand total | 7,799,401.31 |

The first three amounts sum to the Eiffel subtotal; adding the operating-company expenditure reproduces the grand total. The page explicitly removes 72,250 francs from overhead because that amount is already included in assembly. Counting it again would inflate the total. This checks selected arithmetic, not invoices, payment, workforce sufficiency, or whether all material was newly produced.

## What changes in the hypothesis test

There is a detailed construction account to test. An argument based on the absence of named builders or a budget fails for this source. An inherited-structure hypothesis would need positive evidence and an explanation of the documented work; general suspicion cannot substitute for that comparison.

Next checks are original supplier and payroll records, fabrication quantities, transport and assembly rates, and a dated image sequence. The museum photograph in S08 remains uninspected. Agreement among chapters of one book is internal consistency, not independent corroboration. No finding here establishes a global pattern of construction or inheritance.

## Workshop preparation and the labor denominator

The [construction chapter PDF](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/9073009.pdf) was recovered through the archive viewer. PDF pages 17–18, corresponding to book pages 100–101, were visually inspected. The [structured transcription](../data/eiffel-fabrication.json) records selected claims, source hash, exclusions and arithmetic assumptions. The chapter remains part of S17, not another independent witness.

Page 100 describes fabrication at Levallois-Perret while foundations proceeded at the Champ-de-Mars. It describes separately calculated rivet-hole positions, factory drilling and site fitting with tapered pins. Its quotation attributed to Alfred Picard gives a maximum of 250 workers **at the construction site** and explains the reduced site operations through extensive factory preparation. Picard's original report has not been independently inspected. Treating 250 as the complete workforce for producing and erecting the tower would omit the labor explicitly described upstream.

Page 101 supplies the following audit targets:

| Reported item | Quantity or attribution | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General drawings | More than 1,700; Maurice Kœchlin's office | Tower framework; specified accessories and lifts excluded |
| Execution drawings | 3,629; Pluot's office | Detail drawings |
| Combined drawing count | Approximately 5,300 in the prose | Scan reads 5,300; cached OCR incorrectly reads 3,300 |
| Different pieces detailed | 18,038 | Reported drawing content, not our physical inventory |
| Drafting staff and duration | Thirty draftspeople for eighteen months | Attendance, hours and payroll not supplied here |
| Iron supplier | Dupont and Fould, Pompey, Meurthe-et-Moselle | An attribution to trace to supplier records |
| Workshop deliveries | 6,360 tonnes | Excludes rivets installed on site and accessories delivered directly |
| Workshop output period | June 1887 to March 1889; 300–400 tonnes per month | Month-level endpoints and rounded narrative rate |
| Workshop personnel | Létourneau, chief; Pentecôte and Gagnot, foremen | Named credit, not independent biography or recovered erased identities |

The drawing figures are compatible with a rounded total near 5,300; the exact general-drawing count is not given. The workshop tonnage must not be compared with a whole-tower total without reconciling excluded components. There are 21 month boundaries between the named months, but 22 calendar months inclusive: dividing 6,360 by those alternatives yields about 303 or 289 tonnes per month. Neither is a measured monthly production series. Exact working dates, partial months and dispatches are needed before interpreting the rounded 300–400 statement as a contradiction or a validated production rate.

The source also describes checking neighboring pieces in successive flat assemblies rather than erecting the entire tower in the workshop. This identifies a specific fabrication procedure to test against original drawings, matching marks and workshop records. Eiffel's favorable assessment of workmanship remains his assessment.

This evidence makes a construction account more specific: it names a supplier, separates activities and provides quantities. It does not independently prove every piece was newly manufactured, establish total labor productivity, or exclude reuse. The next discriminating evidence is the supplier order/dispatch trail linked to Levallois part registers and site receipts, followed by payroll and dated construction images. A claim of inheritance must explain that evidence if recovered; a claim of documented new fabrication must withstand the same checks.
