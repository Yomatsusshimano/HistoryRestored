Research draft, 2026-10-11. S17; C006. No independent review.

## Where the remains are reported

The opening locates piers 1 and 4 approximately 120 metres behind the then-current quay and reports wood, pottery fragments, domestic-animal bones and stacked-block masonry in mud or variably sandy clay there. The proposed identification of the masonry as an old quay wall is an interpretation, not a demonstrated archaeological identification. Page 89 separately reports substantial masonry remains and tree trunks encountered in the pier-1 caissons, invoked to justify compressed-air work. Neither passage supplies object identifiers, specimen dates or surveyed find depths.

The author explains this setting as an older Seine channel beside the former island called Île des Cygnes, referring to Roussel's 1730 plan and a Berniquet plan dated 1789. Those exact map editions have not been inspected here. The historical island must not automatically be equated with a present feature of the same name. The account attributes the channel's final disappearance to filling associated with the Pont d'Iéna; it supplies no directly dated burial horizon. This is a specific testable competing account, not proof from an author's authority.

The locations are more specific than S355's general exhibition-pit newspaper note. They still do not demonstrate that its reported fragments are these same objects. Nor have S357's borehole numbers 20, 22 and 24 been joined to these pier investigations.

## Sounding methods and selected measurements

Page 88 names Paulin and Arrault for the earlier ordinary soundings and explicitly reports uncertainty in their results. It criticizes conclusions based on small, water-disturbed samples, then describes compressed-air investigation shafts for piers 1 and 4. This source acknowledges a method limitation; the shafts and retrospective report are not additional independent confirmations of every earlier sounding.

Retain the printed elevations as historical *cotes*, not automatically modern sea-level heights. Page 86 relates +27 to the Seine's normal Suresnes-retained level, but a modern datum conversion remains missing. Nearby opening/page89 figures +34.60 and +33.92 for pier 4 are not silently merged; different positions/stages or a printed discrepancy require the drawings and logs.

Three selected exact-decimal checks expose both consistency and a limit. The reported descent 7.43 + 7.07 = 14.50 m agrees with the total, and 34.53 − 14.50 − 1.40 = 18.63 agrees with the clay elevation. But treating the reported 7.43 m fill, 5.99 m sandy-clay/fine-sand interval and 3.37 m lower interval as successive thicknesses gives 16.79 m, whereas surface-to-clay elevation difference is 15.90 m: a 0.89 m mismatch. Also, +34.53 minus 7.43 is +27.10, while the water-stop level is described as approximately +27.00. The latter is qualified approximate; neither difference warrants an invented correction. The 0.89 m discrepancy blocks accepting a fully reconciled layer log from this passage alone. Original readings correct cached OCR's 14.20 m total and +34.33 surface to 14.50 and +34.53.

## Material moved during construction

Page 86 dates the start of pier-2 excavation to 26 January 1887 and reports cart removal to public dumps. Page 87 reports subsequent introduced backfill around the masonry, 4,170 cubic metres, after foundation work. Page 88 says pier-3 excavation material was retained centrally to raise a depression or refill around masonry. These are different operations: pre-existing fill, excavation spoil and construction backfill must not be pooled into a single catastrophe deposit. The quantities and dates remain participant reports, not independently verified cart, payroll or disposal records.

The p86 footnote reports an approximately 200 m sewer diversion by Manoury and Grousselle for 16,000 francs. This corresponds in length and one contractor to S355's earlier reported tower sewer work, but is not an independent as-built route or payment receipt.
