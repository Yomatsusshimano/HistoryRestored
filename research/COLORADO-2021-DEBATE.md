# The 2021 Colorado River chronology debate

SOURCED_DRAFT, 2026-10-08. No independent adjudication.

The later chronology must be assessed alongside its published criticism. Access is unequal: S212's author-uploaded Comment body was read as web text; S213's Reply was inspected only through its USGS introductory summary. Main paper S209 remains abstract-only. No full structural interpretation is verified here.

Dorsey and colleagues dispute the proposed younger chronology, questioning fault-driven duplication, compatibility with existing stratigraphy and fossil constraints, and whether Highwall magnetic remanence is primary. They argue that mineral formation after deposition could break the polarity-to-age link. Their critique does not itself explain away the young sanidine measurements. [Comment, Geology49:e531, DOI10.1130/G49145C.1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354306401_Redefining_the_age_of_the_lower_Colorado_River_southwestern_United_States_COMMENT).

Crow and colleagues' accessible Reply introduction defends integration around4.8–4.62Ma and argues that the criticism supplies no alternative explanation for the detrital-sanidine results. They emphasize agreement with zircon results from separate samples and correlation through magnetostratigraphy. The remainder of the Reply has not been inspected; this summary cannot stand in for its complete response. [USGS Reply record, DOI10.1130/G49334Y.1](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/redefining-age-lower-colorado-river-southwestern-united-states-reply).

## Tests to pursue, rather than choosing a side by authority

| Question | Evidence needed | Archive status |
|---|---|---|
| Are beds repeated by faults? | Georeferenced field mapping, marker-bed offsets, fault geometry and a restoration that fits the same observed sections | Main-paper structural figures and original map comparison pending |
| Does remanence date deposition? | Mineral identification, formation context, component-specific magnetic behavior and tests distinguishing primary from later magnetization | S210/S211 methods and sample-version audit available; no independent mineralogical or field test |
| Are the young mineral ages analytically credible? | Identified grains, calibration, correction inputs, uncertainty and repeat analyses | Selected S210 summaries reproduced; not a full raw-data replication |
| How do mineral dates constrain the sediment? | Sample custody, exact bed position, primary/reworked status and secure contacts | Regional and bed-transfer gaps remain explicit |

These are this archive's proposed audit requirements. A young detrital age, if reliable and belonging to the deposited assemblage, limits how old its host bed can be. An older grain can be reworked into younger sediment. Neither logical relationship decides fault geometry, and neither automatically dates all nearby units. Possible later mineral alteration must be tested rather than invoked solely to protect a preferred chronology.

The existing [Highwall arithmetic](HIGHWALL-WASH.md), [directional-table audit](LOST-CABIN-REVERSAL.md) and [sediment audit](LOST-CABIN-SEDIMENTS.md) therefore address parts of the dispute, not a complete regional solution. The Comment is external criticism of the paper, not independent review of this archive. The Reply is a defense, not independent replication. Publication of disagreement is also not evidence of historical fabrication.
