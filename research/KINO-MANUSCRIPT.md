# Kino manuscript: retrieval path and editorial limits

Research draft, 2026-10-08. No manuscript facsimile inspected or independent authentication completed.

## Institutional retrieval path

S192, [UNM's Misiones finding aid](https://library.unm.edu/cswr/docs/nmarchives/FindingAids/mss867Misiones.pdf), pp.20-21, lists MSS 867, AGN Misiones Legajo 27: CSWR volumes 84A (parts I-II) and 84B (III-V); Box 8, Folder 81 (photostats of Books III-IV); reel 107A contains several pages, alongside other archival series. These are retrieval locators, not inspected manuscript images or confirmed current availability.

The entry dates the work to 21 November 1708, Dolores. It lists five parts/708 pages; the continuation mentions 419 numbered original pages plus 15 index pages and says the title's map was not reproduced. Preserve these descriptions without equating their counting units. An omitted reproduction does not show the original lacked a map. The catalog date does not date every narrated event.

## What Bolton actually claimed

S39, [1919 English edition](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documenti_1/PimeriaI.pdf), printed pp.23-24 (PDF pages 25-26), visually inspected here. Bolton identifies nearly all of volume 27, Sección de Misiones, Archivo General y Público, Mexico City. He describes 433 small folio pages of text and a 14-page contents table. This differs from the finding-aid counts; no missing-page arithmetic is justified before matching scope, pagination and reproduction structure.

Bolton bases his authenticity judgment on three signatures compared with Kino letters and characteristic spelling. He also describes changes in ink, pen and handwriting. In his account, manuscript pages 265-338 contain two other hands with Kino corrections; page 339 onward, including the index, returns to Kino's hand. Thus even this editor's account does not describe an entirely autograph text. These manuscript numbers must not be confused with the English edition's printed pages.

He reports supervising the transcript with William Edward Dunn. The edition regularizes chapter headings, changes some paragraph and marginal-heading placement, and omits underscoring whose authorship he considered uncertain. His assertion that these omissions preserve meaning is an editorial judgment, not an independently demonstrated result here.

## What this changes

The manuscript is now traceable through a specific institutional reproduction catalog, in addition to Bolton's account. The original folios, signatures, corrections, copying dates and custody chain remain uninspected. Neither catalog agreement nor the editor's confidence authenticates the travel observations for this investigation.

The [early Gobien account](GOBIEN-CROSSING.md) already establishes that a crossing claim was printed in 1705. The manuscript check should test the detailed wording, dates and river identities in [the route audit](KINO-ROUTE.md), rather than treating the later translation as the only source of the general claim. Shared Kino-derived information remains dependent evidence.

Next obtain a page-level reproduction manifest and match the November 1701 and March 1702 passages to original foliation. Compare corrections and surrounding text, reconcile the count descriptions, and locate the referenced map separately. Confirm the modern AGN reference before treating Bolton's historical shelfmark as a current one. No inquiry has been sent to an archive.
