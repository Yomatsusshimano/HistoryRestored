# Eiffel archive survival and Koechlin recruitment

Research draft, 2026-10-08. No independent historical review.

The current [ANMT Eiffel inventory](https://recherche-anmt.culture.gouv.fr/archives/archives/fonds/FRANMT_IR_152_AQ_2021_6/view:fonds/n:3), recovered as a 106-page PDF through its normal browser download, changes where to look. Pages 5–6 explain that only part of roughly 400 linear metres offered in 1976 was collected after selection, and that the liquidator sold tower plans to the Société de la Tour Eiffel. Missing files here cannot establish that records never existed. This is the archive's custody account; it does not establish motives for losses or verify present custody of every plan. Page 12 places hiring registers in 152 AQ 11 from 1911 onward, too late to serve as tower-construction payroll.

The digitized [Koechlin dossier, ANMT 2021 6 4](https://recherche-anmt.culture.gouv.fr/ark:/60879/1247075.1339885) supplies a separate company document. All six viewer images were inspected, with focused readings as follows:

| Image | Observation | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Third-person biography says Koechlin joined Eiffel in 1879 as chief of studies and had been a principal collaborator for ten years | Author and creation date unknown; approximately 1889 is an inference from wording, not an authenticated date |
| 4 | Letter addressed to Eiffel, dated 21 October 1879 and signed M. Koechlin, acknowledges a letter of the 19th and proposes starting on 1 November, or 3 November if the office closes for All Saints' Day | Proposed start is not verified attendance |
| 5 | Receipt endorsement reads 22 October 1879 | Endorsement belongs to this correspondence, not every sheet |

The catalog dates the dossier to 1879. That year must not automatically date its biography: the ten-year statement implies a later retrospective account. Image 3 includes tower-project discussion; neither it nor image 6 has received a complete paleographic transcription. These sheets must not become a single contemporaneous 1879 statement about the later tower.

This establishes a checkable recruitment trail earlier than the 1900 monograph, while preserving company provenance. It does not independently confirm fabrication quantities, actual attendance, or an erased identity. Appointment records and original project drawings remain needed.

For supplier evidence, [Geindre collection 227 J](https://archives.meurthe-et-moselle.fr/sites/default/files/Contenu/Guide/227_J.pdf) is a lead rather than a recovered ledger. Its finding aid describes 1995 exhibition panels in entries 1–4, later notes including personnel and copied letters in entry 5, and collected documents/correspondence spanning 1871–1999 in entry 6. Underlying documents were not inspected. No original 1887 dispatch book was recovered; absence from these selected descriptions is not proof that none survives elsewhere.

Next: trace the tower-plan transfer to a current item-level inventory, recover supplier orders/dispatches, and compare the recruitment letter with appointment records. The full investigation remains active.
