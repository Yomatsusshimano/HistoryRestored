# Shelbyville: historical elevation and sidewalk-repair controls

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York). C030/R004; S398–S399. No independent review. [Measurement and retrieval ledger](../data/shelbyville-elevation-controls.json).

The Reddit lead now has a specific historical elevation reference to pursue. It remains a reference point at the courthouse, not a measured sediment thickness at the vault. A local project announcement also identifies sidewalk voids as a repair concern, but does not establish when the vaults were built.

## A locatable 1907 benchmark

[USGS Bulletin 421](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/0421/report.pdf), published in 1910 and compiled by S. S. Gannett and D. H. Baldwin, reports a **650.23-foot elevation** for an unstamped aluminum tablet at the east end of the second step on the courthouse's south front (printed/PDF page 68). Page 67 attributes this regional primary-leveling work to **P. E. Fletcher in 1907**, under H. Foster Bain. The courthouse entry itself has no individual observation date; the regional survey year must not become an invented day of measurement.

The title page and pages 5, 6, 67 and 68 were rendered and visually inspected. Text extraction across the packet located candidate entries; it does not establish full visual inspection of the 74-page file. Native HTTPS delivered the PDF after the browsing tool returned 403 errors. The acquired bytes are hashed in the ledger.

Page 5 places these elevations on the precise-level network adjusted in 1907 to a common mean-sea-level datum, and warns that not every point was finally determined or accepted because additional network information was pending. Page 6 distinguishes primary from precise leveling. Its primary circuit closure criterion is **0.05√D feet**, where D is circuit length in miles. This is a stated acceptance criterion, not a measured error bar for the courthouse mark; its circuit length, raw observations and actual closure are not acquired here.

The report also explains that stamped elevations are approximate field results and can differ from adjusted published elevations. This particular tablet is described as unstamped. The published number must not be silently assigned a modern datum such as NAVD88, a modern geoid model or an ellipsoid height.

## Planned sidewalk work

A [Shelbyville Works project announcement](https://shelbyvilleillinois.net/governor-quinn-awards-shelbyville-illinois-1million-in-funding-for-downtown-streetscape-improvements/) presents a January 30, 2013 publication date and January 29, 2017 modification metadata. It includes sidewalk void repair and curb/gutter adjustments within planned Main Street improvements between Washington and Broadway, divided at Morgan. It names city engineer Alan Spesard. These are announced scope and financing claims, not as-built records, measured grade changes or proof of the 2020 work's execution. The site's text and date metadata were inspected; an independently timestamped original 2013 version was not acquired. No anonymous commenter is promoted to an engineering authority by this association.

## What a valid elevation comparison needs

| Required evidence | Present status | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original historical benchmark description and height | Recovered in Bulletin 421 | Gives a defined historical point rather than a town-wide height |
| Recovery record proving the same tablet and supporting step survive in place | Not acquired | Replacement, movement or rebuilding would invalidate a direct comparison |
| Modern surveyed orthometric height and documented datum transformation | Not acquired | A difference between datum conventions is not geographic upheaval |
| Historical and modern offsets from the mark to the target pavement, vault floor and original soil surface | Not acquired | A courthouse step is not the grade at 212/216 E Main |
| Dated street profiles, ordinances and as-built work | Not acquired | Separates intentional grading and later repair from deposition |
| Target sediment log with undisturbed boundaries, provenance and contextual dates | Not acquired | Distinguishes natural deposition from construction fill and dates the event |

Neither the courthouse height nor the Morris Bridge water reading elsewhere on page 68 measures burial at the vault. They refer to different places and surfaces. No subtraction between them is used as a downtown flood depth. Horizontal modern parcel coordinates are not elevations. No sediment volume, flow path, event date or vertical displacement is inferred from this batch.

The next documentary test is benchmark recovery and the actual design/as-built street profile. A paired measurement could test change since 1907; it would not by itself resolve an earlier event or demonstrate a global chronology break. All twenty objectives remain active.
