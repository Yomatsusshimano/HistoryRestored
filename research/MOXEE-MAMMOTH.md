# Moxee City: tusk age and burial age are separate

Research draft, 2026-10-08. Case C024; no independent review.

[Lillquist, Lundblad and Barton (2005), S160](https://www.gis.cwu.edu/geog/documents/Moxee-City-Mammoth-Morphostratigraphic-Taphonomic-Taxonomic-Considerations.pdf) was visually inspected throughout (printed pp.417–428). It reports **CAMS 79942, 14,570 ± 50 radiocarbon yr BP**, measured on tusk collagen (p.422). Stafford Research Laboratories prepared the sample (p.426); the assay certificate, detailed chemistry and quality controls are not supplied. This is not a calibrated calendar age. The [structured record](../data/moxee-mammoth.json) preserves unknowns.

The tusk lies near the base of Unit 4 (pp.421–422, Figure 4). Rounded, sediment-filled cracks and exfoliation contrast with a less-weathered tip and fresh construction-related fractures (pp.420,423). The authors infer exposure and partial burial before final transport/deposition. They interpret Units 2–4 as three floods, but neither exposure duration nor the intervals between those deposits are measured here. Mammuthus identification uses tusk structure; the narrower Columbian-mammoth attribution is explicitly circumstantial (p.423).

The abstract places the first two floods between 15,300 and 14,570 radiocarbon BP; p.425 instead uses 19,000 and 14,570. Preserve that discrepancy pending clarification.

## What follows, and what does not

This investigation's inference: a dated piece of tusk measures carbon associated with tissue formation. Even if accepted as a close death-age estimate, reworking prevents equating it automatically with final deposition. Physical weathering evidence makes this a concrete issue rather than an explanation invented solely to accommodate mismatched dates.

There is also an ordering limitation in the proposed age of the lower beds. In forward-running time, let D be death and B2, B3, B4 the deposition times of Units 2–4. The proposed sequence supplies D < B4 and B2 < B3 < B4. Those relations alone do **not** imply B3 < D: death could precede or follow the lower deposits while the tusk waits elsewhere for transport. Therefore the tusk's occurrence in Unit 4 does not, by itself, prove that Units 2 and 3 predate death. Additional chronology is required. This logical audit does not establish that the authors' lower-bed ages are false.

For H3-M, do not count burial here as demonstrated flood mortality. For H3-D, retain redeposition as the study's supported interpretation while keeping transport origin and delay unquantified. For H1, three interpreted local deposits do not establish three globally synchronous events or their separation in years. This case cannot resolve the unrelated Dead Canyon GX-1457 assay.

Next inspect museum accession and excavation records, original CAMS/Stafford documentation, and independent sediment chronology. Test the mapped contacts and proposed flood count against alternative depositional sequences before using the site as a time anchor or hydraulic constraint.

The [contact and dike audit](MOXEE-FLOOD-COUNT.md) now separates the reported three-unit sequence from the inferred number of floods. Dike termination alone is not a measured dry interval.
