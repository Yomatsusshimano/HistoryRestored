# Earlier exhibition remains and documented ground alteration

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S356; C006. No independent review.

Alfred Picard's official *Rapport général*, volume2, **1891**, now supplies a specific alternative to catastrophic burial for some reported Champ-de-Mars remains. Its original title and printed26–30 were visually inspected through [Cnum's institutional scans](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?8XAE349.2/0035). These selected pages cover investigation, grading and sewer history; the complete volume and underlying field records remain uninspected.

## What the report attributes to earlier works

Page27 attributes foundation debris to previous exhibitions, mentions abandoned sewers and older perimeter ditches, and reports a60-by-approximately200m quarry opened in1878 beside the École militaire. Page28 attributes irregular terrain partly to alterations in1867/1878 and drainage arrangements. Page29 records190,000m³ of excavation,10,000m³ of old masonry demolition and416,000m² of grading. Page30 says the prior sewer network served1867/1878. These are administrative reports, not newly authenticated measurements.

## Preserve the investigation's spatial limits

Page26 reports50general pits,1.20m in diameter,6–9m deep with8m average depth. Its named location counts sum to45and end with an open-ended designation: five pits are unallocated by that list, not demonstrated missing from the work. The average depth to sand/gravel is separately7.90m after fill. This is depth to a contact, not measured fill thickness at every location.

Page27 then describes17additional investigations for the Palais des machines, including divergent results and a deeper borehole. It does not identify those pits as Eiffel foundations. Source geological age labels remain source interpretations; this investigation has not independently dated the layers.

## How this changes the buried-remains test

S355's unsigned January1887note reports fragments in unspecified exhibition pits. S356 now supplies positive documentary evidence for a competing site-history explanation, rather than merely invoking ordinary construction as a possibility. But the exact fragment/pit joins are missing. Similar materials and the same broad site cannot demonstrate that both reports describe the same objects.

The official report's authorship does not make the attribution immune to testing. Its expenditure footnote on p27 explicitly says administration figures were supplied to Picard. Survey notebooks, pit plans, earlier exhibition drawings, demolition records and retained specimens could confirm or contradict the attribution. The footnote concerns expenditure; it must not be generalized into an explicit source attribution for every geological sentence.

Neither190,000m³ divided by the graded area nor the pit depth proves a uniform mud layer. Grading combines cuts and redistribution; the pages do not provide a spatial volume balance. The target platform is labeled35.60m, but its datum and point-level survey control have not been joined here.

Next retrieve the numbered pit locations and1867/1878construction/clearance plans, then match individual remains and depths. A shared catastrophic horizon requires independent dating and sediment evidence beyond this reported construction debris.
