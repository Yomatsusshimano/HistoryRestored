For an unreset inherited grain incorporated during deposition, the age-before-present inequality is `A(deposition) <= A(grain)`, allowing for dating uncertainty. A maximum depositional age is an older limit, not proof that deposition occurred close to that age. Reworking is expressly discussed by the original author; it is not a newly discovered objection ignored by the conventional study. Conversely, showing inheritance provides no positive date for a historical catastrophe.

The Highwall photograph (Figure37, printed96) is the same outcrop used in the later supplement; its duplicate publication is not another field observation. ## Sediments constrain the event sequence separately

Printed21 reports alternating fine mud/silt beds and erosive cross-bedded sand, vertical burrows in the lower15m, local mudcracks and carbonate nodules, and upper paleosols including a10cm carbonate soil layer traced on both wash walls. The author interprets repeated fluvial/lacustrine sedimentation and changing aridity. Figure37 labels a Highwall paleosol and interprets a chemical magnetic overprint linked to exposure.

This archive separates three propositions:

1. **Reported features:** burrows, cracks, soil-like horizons and erosion surfaces occur at described positions.
2. **Interpretation to test:** they formed in place during intervals between depositional episodes, rather than being transported clasts, later cracks or postdepositional alteration crossing the whole section.
3. **Chronology:** the number and duration of those intervals require additional evidence; neither the word paleosol nor bed thickness supplies a numerical duration.

If interbedded horizons demonstrably formed in place and were subsequently buried, they challenge a model in which the entire measured sequence was deposited continuously in one uninterrupted flood. They do not exclude a later flood eroding older strata, several flood pulses separated by exposure, or a catastrophe elsewhere. Such alternatives need a specified contact and affected beds, rather than treating every exposed layer as the same event.

## Discriminating observations

| Competing explanation | Evidence that would distinguish it |
|---|---|
| In-place exposure between depositional episodes | Soil profile rooted in substrate, downward alteration from a buried surface, burrows truncated/sealed by the next bed, lateral continuity across sections |
| Transported soil/ash fragments within one event deposit | Clast boundaries, rotation, abrasion and matrix-supported mixing; lack of a continuous substrate-linked profile |
| Later alteration of an already deposited sequence | Features cutting several beds or following younger fractures, with cross-cutting relations inconsistent with successive buried surfaces |
| One later flood reworking older deposits | A mapped younger erosion/deposition boundary and a separately dated younger unit; old source-grain ages alone cannot identify it |

These are proposed follow-up tests, **not preregistered successes** or features all verified here. Field photographs, thin sections, surveyed contacts and sample-level analytical context are needed. This keeps physical sequencing evidence in the test even while the magnetic calculation remains unresolved.

Follow-up visual inspection of Table5, printed56/PDF64, resolves the uncertainty convention. The first analytical row is RC15-LCW-111, aliquot label#10, **5.489 ±0.788Ma at one sigma**. Separate columns without and with J uncertainty both round to0.788Ma. Methods printed13 (text-read) also specifies one-sigma errors including counting, J and correction-factor contributions. Identical rounded errors do not establish a zero J contribution.

The next two displayed rows, also labeled RC15-LCW-111#10, are6.720 ±0.843 and7.108 ±0.382Ma, with one-sigma errors. No unique crystal identifier distinguishes these rows in the printed table; the archive uses explicit page/row locators, not invented laboratory IDs. [Transcribed rows](../data/lcw111-youngest-rows.json) retain isotope-ratio, radiogenic-fraction and reported error fields.

For the first two rows, direct age±one-sigma intervals are4.701–6.277 and5.877–7.563Ma and overlap. Thus the youngest point estimate is not, from these uncertainties alone, a sharply separated youngest age population. This overlap neither proves a common eruption nor licenses pooling different inherited grains into an eruption date. The entire99-grain distribution, analytical suitability and source context remain to be assessed.

Doubling the youngest row's printed one-sigma error gives a descriptive two-sigma interval3.913–7.065Ma. This is an interval around a **mineral age**, not a depositional-age interval or a probability that a catastrophe occurred then. A reworked grain can predate its host sediment by an unknown amount. The source's interpreted ashfall and depositional connection still needs evidence beyond choosing the smallest point estimate.

The printed J field is rounded to0.0007, with reported J error0.000000904; it should not be treated as the full-precision original calibration input. No isotope-age recalculation is claimed.
