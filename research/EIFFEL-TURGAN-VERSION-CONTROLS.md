# Narrowing the bound-volume date discrepancy

2026-10-11. C006 / S343–S345. Selected original scans and holding-institution metadata; no independent review.

The [previous proposal audit](EIFFEL-TURGAN-PROJECT-ACCOUNT.md) retained an 1882 bound-volume title, a 28-page scanned article, and an ANMT description of a March 1886 issue with 22 pages. Follow-up inspection now supplies two controls against treating the bound title as the date of every article.

First, CNAM's printed page 22, vue177, ends mid-sentence while describing the Nice observatory's floating dome. Page23/vue178 continues that sentence and the same figure sequence; page24/vue179 continues the mechanism discussion into page25. No article-end imprint appears on page22. This rules out calling page22 the endpoint of the **CNAM scanned version**. It does not establish that the ANMT 22-page description is an error, nor authenticate the contents of that uninspected copy.

Second, the next article begins at vue184 with livraison355 and a subject change to Coulon's Martinique rum business. Its printed page7/vue190 contains a table headed 1883, 1884 and 1885, followed by prose discussing the 1885 export result retrospectively. These are reported historical results, not prospective forecasts. Thus the scanned volume contains textual material referring to years later than its 1882 title page, beyond the tower's proposed 1889 exhibition. Under the text's own chronology, the title cannot date all contents to1882. This does not independently authenticate those trade statistics, date the physical binding, or establish a precise publication day for the Eiffel article.

The [Huntington collection record](https://www.huntington.org/collections/lib-713603) gives an 1886 publication date and copy713603. Its [full library catalog](https://catalog.huntington.org/record=b1754320), acquired through a normal HTTPS request, describes **28 pages**, Paris, Librairie des Dictionnaires, 1886, with a cover title. It records a list of Eiffel works for1867–1885 inside the front cover and a bound-in leaf listing constructions from1885–1889. These are item-description assertions; covers, added leaf, tipped-in bridge list and physical copy were not inspected. The web tool could read the collection page but not the full catalog; the separately acquired catalog HTML supplies the detailed description.

The Huntington metadata makes a 28-page1886 version a specific retrieval target, rather than a hypothetical reconciliation. A later bound-in list illustrates why physical additions must be dated separately, but it does not prove that the CNAM volume has the same binding history. Neither institution's catalog is an independent witness to the manufacturing operations narrated by the publication. Catalogs may also share bibliographic descriptions.

The surviving explanations include different editions or states, composite binding, and a catalog pagination error. The current evidence does not select among them for the ANMT copy. There is no basis here for claiming that the tower was already built in1882, that a chronology was fabricated, or that the proposed price was actual expenditure. The pages remain evidence of a published proposal, with the exact scanned issue date unresolved.

Neither the complete article nor the complete volume has been audited. Images remain linked rather than mirrored.
