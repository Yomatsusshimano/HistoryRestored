# An undated plan distinguishes intended construction from depicted terrain

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S368; C006. No independent review.

The Musée Carnavalet / Paris Musées [catalog record](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/palais-general-des-terrains-destines-a-la-construction-du-palais-des) identifies an anonymous pen-and-wash drawing of land intended for the Archives palace. Its public [IIIF manifest](https://apicollections.parismusees.paris.fr/iiif/320293875/manifest) delivered one image, 2611 × 2160 pixels. The complete image was surveyed visually, with six original-resolution detail crops inspected for title, central footprint, street/marsh labels, scale, river frontage and western parcels. This is not a complete transcription. [Acquisition and claim ledger](../data/archives-palais-site-plan.json).

## What the original drawing says

The lower-right title reads **“Plan général des terrains destinés à la construction du Palais des Archives.”** The catalog and manifest instead begin **“Palais général…”**. Retain both readings rather than silently correcting the institutional title or treating the difference as evidence of historical manipulation.

The central pink footprint is labeled as the emplacement of the palace buildings and commercial buildings. This is explicit intended construction, not proof of a completed palace. Smaller footprints appear inside the wider site boundary, but their colors and depiction do not establish that every structure existed when drawn. No complete existing/proposed legend has been authenticated here.

No elevations, sediment thicknesses, dated excavation units or tower pier identifiers have been recovered from this inspection. The marsh label is a cartographic depiction, not a sediment sample. Neither colored boundaries nor the absence of an unmistakable separate channel can certify when filling occurred.

## Catalog limits and the 1813 candidate relationship

The inspected catalog HTML and manifest give anonymous authorship and medium but no creation date or explicit inventory-number field. The canvas delivery label **AZE_CARDTOP000193_001** is retained as a delivery identifier, not promoted to an authenticated accession number. The series title mentions several streets and bridges; those collection headings cannot date the underlying drawing. Published dimensions are catalog metadata, not our physical measurements.

The [26 March 1813 letter](CYGNES-NAPOLEON-1813-LETTER.md) requests an ownership plan from Concorde to Iéna, plus Archives design and emplacement plans. This drawing's subject makes it a relevant candidate for the latter request. There is no verified archival link, date, recipient annotation or exact extent match establishing that it is one of the requested plans. The drawing does not authenticate the letter's underlying minute or the Lazare inventory item.

Its value is a specific spatial lead: the proposed palace footprint and surrounding parcels can be compared with authenticated works records and later foundation plans. It cannot yet identify any buried wall described in 1887 or establish that an intended building was actually built.

## Next discriminating work

Obtain the object's dating and provenance record; transcribe the faint parcel annotations with uncertainties; authenticate the plan's connection, if any, to the 1813 requests. Register stable landmarks against dated maps and the tower plan with explicit error estimates before associating structures or deposits. Actual palace works accounts, execution drawings and excavation locations must supply the construction-to-remains connection.
