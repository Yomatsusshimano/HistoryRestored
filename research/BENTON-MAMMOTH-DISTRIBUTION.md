# Mammoth discovery geography in Benton County

Research draft, 2026-10-08. This regional comparison informs the wildlife investigation; it has no independent review.

[Barton and Last's 2010 poster, S154](https://web.archive.org/web/20201124150656id_/https://coyotecanyonmammothsite.org/resources/barton-last_2010_discovery-distribution-of-mammoth-remains_ccms.pdf) was recovered from a 2020 Internet Archive capture. The whole poster, enlarged discussion and map legend were visually inspected. Its compilation draws on newspapers, museum collections, publications and personal accounts. Those underlying records have not been independently audited here. The [structured summary](../data/benton-mammoth-distribution.json) preserves source counts and arithmetic separately.

| Subregion | Reported mammoth finds | Printed percentage | Count divided by 45 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Horse Heaven Hills | 30 | 66.3% | 66.67% |
| Pasco Basin | 11 | 24.3% | 24.44% |
| Lower Yakima River Valley | 4 | 9.3% | 8.89% |

The counts sum to 45. Printed percentages do not exactly match that denominator; the cause is unknown. This small arithmetic discrepancy does not establish a wrong specimen count, chronology or interpretation. The source excludes other, uncertain proboscidean identifications from these regional mammoth totals. A reported find is not automatically an independently verified individual, and none of these totals counts dated simultaneous deaths.

The authors attribute 21 Horse Heaven Hills finds to Fry's 1969 survey over approximately 25 square miles: 70% of that subregion's 30 finds and about 47% of the entire 45-find compilation. They associate many other discoveries with construction and agriculture, and leave the relative influence of discovery activity and ancient environmental differences unresolved. These percentages describe the compiled records, not detection probabilities or ancient population density. No common search-effort denominator is supplied for the three subregions.

The map distinguishes Ice Age flood sediment, loess and unknown deposits, with separate symbols for poorly located finds. Its photographs include discoveries attributed to geologic reconnaissance, residential construction, landscape maintenance, mountain biking and quarrying. This is evidence about how remains came to attention, not evidence that those activities sampled habitats equally. No exact coordinates have been inferred from the small map; contextual unknowns remain unknown.

For the single-upheaval test, a county-wide concentration of mapped fossils is insufficient. A discriminating comparison needs authenticated specimen identities, original locality accuracy, dated host units, reworking assessments and independent dates, with discovery effort considered. Shared geography does not establish shared mortality or final burial. Conversely, unequal discovery counts alone cannot disprove a shared episode.

Next recover Fry's original 1969 survey and the cited West Richland and Dead Canyon reports; crosswalk their site identifiers, depositional observations and specimen custody to the poster before expanding the coordinate inventory. Compare flood-hosted and loess-hosted records without turning tentative context labels into certain assignments. The missing Coyote Canyon AMQUA assay abstract was not found in the checked university-file and site-resource archive paths; that search result is not evidence against the assays.
