## What the manuscript establishes

In the main paragraph, Derham identifies the second page of de la Hire's preface to the first part of his astronomical tables. He alleges that it credits Picard, rather than Gascoigne, with first applying telescopic sights to mathematical instruments. Derham's accusatory language expresses his position; this archive does not adopt it as an independently established verdict or evidence of intent.

Derham says Gascoigne's papers are in his hands and offers copies for publication in the Society's Transactions, if the Society wishes. He refers to earlier letter dates;1638 is readable, while the inserted/overwritten later year is provisionally read as41. Those are leads to documents he says he possesses, not authenticated dates of invention. The paragraph also references1686 in describing the competing attribution's time comparison; that number is not promoted to an invention date or reconciled by changing the manuscript.

The catalog's November30 date is distinct from the visible Saint Andrew's Day1713 heading and docket. These agree at the catalog/reading level, but independent dating of ink, paper, handwriting and archival custody has not been performed. No full diplomatic transcription is claimed. Abbreviations, insertions and uncertain readings remain available in the original images for another reader to challenge.

This establishes a surviving record of **an allegation, reported possession of earlier papers, and a proposed publication remedy**. It does not establish that the proposal was accepted, which copies were supplied, whether the possession claim was accurate, or whose technical priority is justified.

## Printed argument and technical discrimination: 2026-10-09 update

On p603 Derham identifies the table publication as1687 and quotes de la Hire in Latin. Crucially, he acknowledges that the quoted words do **not expressly call Picard the inventor**; they describe Picard applying telescopes. Derham infers priority from the implication and links it to Auzout's account in Transactions issue21. This qualifies the stronger1713 allegation. The separate de la Hire text remains necessary: Derham's quotation is a transmitted witness, not a second original. A Google Books catalog identifies the1687 title, publisher and Madrid holding-copy lead, but the attempted PDF download returned403. No preface inspection is claimed.

Derham says papers collected through Towneley, alongside Towneley's own papers, are now in his hands. On p604 he says descriptions and drawings are in Gascoigne's handwriting, and refers micrometer priority to issue25. These are specific custody and antecedent leads, not independent handwriting authentication.

Derham argues use as early as1640, but that conclusion remains his argument until original letter dates and contents are authenticated.

The article is positive evidence that Derham publicly advanced the priority claim with technical extracts, beyond the earlier proposed remedy. It does not prove that the Society accepted his conclusion, establish the precise editorial chain from1713, or show deliberate theft. These descriptions make ordinary development and transmission testable; they provide no demonstrated ancient-recovery mechanism. Neither the printed voices nor the manuscript have independent review.


## Earlier claim and response: issue21 and issue25 recovered

2026-10-09. S293 preserves [Auzout's complete printed extract](../sources/originals/invention/derham1713/auzout1667-article.pdf), pp373-375; S294 preserves [Towneley's complete printed extract](../sources/originals/invention/derham1713/towneley1667-article.pdf), pp457-458. All five historical scan pages were visually inspected. The [acquisition ledger](../data/micrometer1667-acquisition.json) distinguishes downloaded files from rewritten excerpts excluding modern covers. Shared-page neighboring articles are outside these conclusions.

Auzout's heading dates his letter December28,1666. That is a letter date, not a demonstrated publication day. His opening describes a method he and Picard have, judges it superior to earlier practice and reports24000 or30000divisions per foot with an asserted3-4angular-second precision. He discusses refraction, celestial motion and incomplete lunar sampling. This is a claim to a method and its performance, not an independently verified accuracy trial. In this extract he does not name Gascoigne. Silence does not establish prior access, copying or deliberate suppression. His further claim that no astronomer had foreseen the discussed eclipse-diameter change is a separate priority assertion, not equivalent to inventing all telescopic sights.

Towneley's printed response explicitly cites issue21/p373. He says scattered Gascoigne papers establish instrument use before the Civil Wars, and reports possessing the first instrument plus two improved ones. These are concrete antecedent/custody claims with no objects authenticated here. He also reports unsuccessfully retrieving an optics treatise and describes the available instrument as imperfect. He says he improved it with an ingenious watchmaker, unnamed in this extract, and distinguishes attainable land-angle precision from difficulty measuring moving celestial objects. The fractional division is1/4000inch; conditional12inches per foot gives48000divisions, consistent with his above40000 wording. That arithmetic establishes internal compatibility, not actual resolution or measurement accuracy.

His national-credit framing is explicit. It documents his motive for speaking publicly, without establishing the opponent's intent. The editorial note says description and observations are being sought; their actual delivery and acceptance require later records. No specific day is assigned to this excerpt from a manuscript date found only in a later catalog.

The sequence now has an explicit printed link: Auzout's account, Towneley's cited response, and Derham's later reuse of that dispute and reported papers. It is evidence of a **publicly contested attribution and reported improvement process**. Three surviving printed records are not three independent instrument tests. We must distinguish original principle, improved device, telescopic-sight application, claimed precision, exclusive priority and subsequent attribution. This advances the mechanism question without converting a credit dispute into proof of widespread historical rewriting or recovered ancient technology.
