## The wording changes

Selected transcription, S190 printed p.71, second footnote; long s normalized and line breaks joined:

> Il passa Rio Hila, & se trouva en 1700 proche du Rio Colorado, & l'ayant traversé il fut bien surpris, en 1701, de le voir dans la Californie, & d'apprendre qu'environ à 30 ou 40 lieues du lieu où il étoit alors, le Colorado, après avoir fait une Baye d'une assez longue étendue, alloit se jetter dans la Mer.

The footnote introduces this as the substance of what the Jesuit Gobien says in the preliminary letter on Kino's discovery. Its pronouns need care: the passage explicitly names crossing Hila, then being near Colorado, and subsequently says “having crossed it.” The latter pronoun's river antecedent is not resolved here. The following “le voir” also requires contextual interpretation. Do not turn this syntactically difficult sentence into an unequivocal Colorado-crossing record.

In contrast, S189 p.133 states:

> Le P. Kino n'ayant point passé Rio de Hila, encore moins le Rio Colorado ...

The first clause denies crossing Hila, directly conflicting with Buache's explicit first clause. The second denies crossing Colorado. Our earlier commentary that Buache's passage does not say he crossed Colorado needs qualification: it contains an ambiguous crossing pronoun, though it does not explicitly repeat Colorado as the object. Preserve the ambiguity rather than converting it into either a denial or a definite crossing.

## Buache's competing geographic interpretations

On p.71 Buache treats the peninsula as established from Kino's map, while asking whether it could also be called an island at certain times. Pages 71-72 introduce an account attributed to Ellis: a traveler reportedly observed a narrow isthmus submerged during high tides. Buache then compares a 1604 Spanish manuscript's small isthmus at 30 degrees with Delisle's mapped gulf. Page 73 invokes tides and currents in interpreting this possibility.

These are nested reports and cartographic reasoning, not Buache's own surveyed tidal observations. A tide-submerged isthmus is also a different physical claim from a permanently open channel or a rapid regional displacement. Testing it requires a located sill, elevation/datum, tidal range, connection geometry and period. None is established by these paragraphs. The manuscript's geometry and Buache's inference do not supply a modern route.

Buache names the 1705 fifth collection of *Lettres edifiantes*, already represented by S38 in this archive. The next step is its preliminary letter attributed to Gobien, not another retrieval of the Picolo report previously inspected. Compare exact wording and episode chronology with S190 and later Kino editions before assigning an error to a particular writer. Neither disagreement nor dependence establishes deliberate suppression.
