# Sacramento: reported street-fill quantities in1867

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York). R008 follow-up, S457. No independent review.

## From advocacy to reported work

[Sacramento Daily Union, “Street Improvements,” 1January1867](https://cdnc.ucr.edu/?a=d&d=SDU18670101.2.5) reports street grading during the preceding year and attributes copied figures to the City Auditor's books. Those original books are not acquired. Archive metadata identifies volume32/number4918; the selected original-image table appears on viewer page1. The masthead was not independently inspected. Selected prose was read as OCR; the **HIGH GRADE table and totals were visually inspected at magnification**.

This advances the earlier [1863letter comparison](SACRAMENTO-1863-HIGH-GRADE-LETTERS.md): the article reports work and accounts rather than merely advocating it. It remains a newspaper report, not an independent survey or contractor certification. The article's preceding annual-assessment table, cumulative low-grade costs, sewers, paving and ongoing projects have different scopes; no combined city sediment budget is asserted.

## Physical inference and limits

The account supports investigating a substantial, spatially specified program of deliberate fill. Its volume concerns reported street work, not a measured flood-deposit volume. Converting a city accounting total into uniform mud thickness would require verified areas, original elevations, placed versus compacted volume conventions, material provenance and parcel associations. None is established here. No such conversion or global extrapolation is performed.

Natural flood sediment may underlie later engineered fill. Their relative contributions require actual logged sections, samples and grade controls. The original Reddit photograph remains unauthenticated and unjoined to any table reach, so these quantities cannot be assigned to its pictured ground.

Next retrieve the City Auditor books and assessments behind the named reaches, contractor quantities and material sources, then join them to original grade surveys and the identified demolition parcel. This addresses physical feasibility and attribution without assuming either a global catastrophe or that every buried opening has the same cause.
