# Buried infrastructure reported during exhibition excavation

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S355; C006. No independent review.

The original [Le Moniteur de l'exposition de1889, p479](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?FOLXAE14.3/0035), issue108, has a running header dated **23January1887**. The complete page was visually inspected; only the two short construction notes in its middle column were audited. Search context had placed the passage near a January9header. That association was wrong: the original page supplies the correct publication date.

## Two separate notes, two different location scopes

The first note names the tower site: about200m of sewer must be moved before its earthworks. It attributes the work to Manoury, commissioned by Eiffel, and says sewer work began on Wednesday morning. That relative wording does not establish an exact start date.

The following note concerns exhibition works more generally. It reports strongly frozen surface ground and irregular foundation pits containing concrete blocks, cut stone, retaining walls and old Portland-cement conduits. A printed divider separates the notes. This second note does **not** name the tower foundations, specify depths, identify a deposit horizon or date the masonry. Only the conduits are explicitly called old.

## What this changes

There is now a dated, near-contemporary published report of subsurface construction remains encountered during the exhibition works. It is a documentary observation claim, not an inspected archaeological assemblage. The nearby sewer note makes pre-existing infrastructure a concrete retrieval target; it cannot locate every reported fragment or explain its burial.

This source does not distinguish demolition fill, earlier occupation, installed utility works, redeposition or catastrophic burial. Ordinary site history is a competing explanation requiring evidence, not an automatic answer. A catastrophe interpretation likewise needs a measured stratigraphic horizon, material provenance and a dated burial event. The page provides none of those controls.

Retrieve exhibition pit plans, daily work reports, sewer relocation drawings and any retained fragments. Join each observation to a location and depth before correlating it with other buried streets or deposits. Do not turn a January1887publication into a date for deposition, or unspecified exhibition pits into the tower's foundations.
