# Moxee flood count: what the contacts establish

Research draft, 2026-10-08. Companion to [the tusk chronology audit](MOXEE-MAMMOTH.md); no new fieldwork or independently validated flood count.

[S160](https://www.gis.cwu.edu/geog/documents/Moxee-City-Mammoth-Morphostratigraphic-Taphonomic-Taxonomic-Considerations.pdf), pp.421–425 and Figure 4, interprets Units 2–4 as three flood deposits. The following separates its reported observations from the additional inference about events.

| Reported feature | What it can constrain | What it does not measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Units 2–4 lack internal grading/stratification; contacts 2/3 and 3/4 are described as conformable and gradational | Field subdivision and relative position | Number of separate lake drainings or duration between units |
| Some dikes stop at unit tops; others cross boundaries | Relative deformation relationships, if termination or truncation geometry is verified | A dry interval or its length |
| Dikes do not extend into Unit 5 loess | Different deformation history of flood units and overlying loess | Three distinct dry intervals within Units 2–4 |
| Developed soil in underlying Unit 1 | Earlier landscape stability before the flood sequence | Soil development between each proposed flood |

The authors themselves allow later dike formation or reactivation for cross-boundary examples (p.425). This is useful evidence against assigning every dike to a unique flood solely from the number of units it crosses.

## The cited mechanism is not a drying clock

[Black (1979), S161](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/7096853), printed pp.59–63 / PDF pp.71–75, distinguishes different dike origins. He proposes flood loading and underwater infilling for several surveyed sites, while leaving another site's origin unresolved. His proposed minutes-to-days filling times are interpretations, not measured durations or bounds applicable to Moxee. The report explicitly limits its proposed mechanism's geographic/stratigraphic application. Its three-day reconnaissance does not independently test Moxee's contacts.

[Howard, Pritchard and Mackenzie (2020), S162](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2020AM/webprogram/Paper357379.html) report experimental production of dikes through sediment loading and water/air escape. This is an abstract-level result: experimental dimensions, scaling and raw observations have not been recovered here. It shows an alternative mechanism to investigate, not a calibrated replication of Moxee or proof of one regional flood.

## Assessment and discriminating test

Our inference is narrower than either a three-flood confirmation or a single-flood replacement. The inspected evidence supports depositional and deformation sequencing, but does not yet determine the number of independently initiated floods or elapsed time. A dike that ends at a contact may have been truncated before subsequent deposition, or its propagation may have stopped there. Distinguishing those possibilities requires the actual geometry and sediment fabric; a line ending on a schematic cannot settle it.

Recover the original section logs and close photographs, trace each boundary and dike laterally, and check for erosion of pre-existing fills, displacement, fill provenance and independently recognizable exposure surfaces. Compare a model with separate inundations against deposition/deformation stages within one inundation. Each must explain the full contact network and sediment transport, not merely the number of labeled units. Independent dates or validated intervening exposure indicators are required to estimate separation in years.

This finding does not remove the different evidence for repeated flooding at other sites, such as the [Grand Coulee intervening sediments and opposed flow indicators](MISSOULA-STRATIGRAPHY.md). Those require their own tests. Nor does a rapid dike-forming mechanism establish common fossil mortality, a global flood, or historical rewriting.
