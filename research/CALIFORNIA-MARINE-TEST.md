# Marine incursion, inland lake and a through-going passage

Research draft, 2026-10-08. Linked to C005/C017; no independent review or completed coastline reconstruction.

The island-map question needs three separate tests: environment, time and connectivity. A marine fossil can bear on environment without establishing either the date of a map's depicted geography or a continuous passage to the Pacific. Conversely, the freshwater Cahuilla evidence cannot establish that every older regional deposit was nonmarine.

## A real environmental disagreement to preserve

[McDougall and Miranda Martinez (2014), S183](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/evidence-a-marine-incursion-along-lower-colorado-river-corridor), interpret lower Bouse foraminiferal assemblages in Blythe Basin as marine, followed by saline-lake conditions. Their abstract reports planktic fossils above which assemblages become less diverse and gives a biostratigraphic age range of 8.10-5.3 Ma for the oldest southern portion. That range is not a directly measured event interval. Only the complete USGS abstract/metadata were inspected; full methods, specimens and age controls remain unaudited.

[Bright et al. (2016), S184](https://pages.uoregon.edu/rdorsey/Downloads/BrightEtal2016.pdf), instead interpret Hart Mine Wash fauna and isotopes as a stratified lake followed by mixing and catastrophic spillover. Figure 2 links depicted valves to samples, including Cyprideis in HM49/HM50 and Candona in HM53/HM54. These are reported identifications, not independently classified specimens. Figure 1 labels highest formation outcrop elevations, not contemporaneous water levels. The subsequent [methods and supplement audit](BOUSE-METHODS.md) recovers S185, documents trace fossils that qualify categorical prose, and identifies the assumed-rate basis of the proposed 3-300-year transition. Specimen-level validation and independent age controls remain pending.

The latter paper explicitly investigates the same Hart Mine Wash outcrop as the former study. Opposing interpretations are therefore not two geographically independent demonstrations. This initial comparison does not resolve the marine-versus-lake disagreement or survey all subsequent research. It identifies an accessible, specimen-linked test case and prevents selective citation of only the interpretation favorable to a proposed reconstruction.

The recovered [marine-study supplement](BOUSE-MARINE-LEDGER.md), S186, adds morphological notes and thirteen Hart Mine Wash laboratory records. It makes the identification rationale auditable, while showing why shared locality must not be treated as proven identical-bed sampling. The main marine paper remains abstract-only here.

## Consequences for the historical map claim

| Proposed inference | Required evidence | Present boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marine water once entered part of the lower Colorado corridor | In-place assemblages, depositional context and a supported environmental interpretation | A published marine interpretation exists; a competing lake interpretation requires examination |
| The deposit records water during the seventeenth-century map interval | Deposit-specific chronology and a demonstrated association with that interval | Neither inspected study supplies this historical association; geological age assignments remain to be audited rather than discarded |
| California was isolated by a Gulf-to-Pacific channel | A connected route with compatible elevations, in-place deposits and contemporaneous age constraints along its full length | An inlet reaching Blythe would not by itself establish an outlet to the Pacific |
| A rapid regional change explains the island-to-peninsula depictions | Dated geometry change plus independent observation/transmission history of the maps | Existing comparison-sheet source dates and copying relationships are not shoreline measurements |

The prior [Cahuilla audit](CAHUILLA.md) addresses different deposits and a later chronology. Its freshwater evidence cannot simply be pooled with Bouse fossils under a generic label of aquatic remains. Likewise, an old fossil reworked into a later bed would require separate dating of fossil origin and final deposition. Reworking must be demonstrated, not supplied whenever dates disagree.

## A concrete sequence for the next geographical test

The published Hart Mine Wash sample-position and isotope tables are now recovered as S185. Next recover original species identifications and age constraints, and reconcile trace entries with the counting records. Test the environmental explanations on the same stratigraphic observations, keeping ecological assignments distinct from dating assignments. Resolve whether any marine indicator is reworked and how that conclusion was obtained.

Separately, georeference one selected island-map depiction with declared control points and uncertainty. Define the northern channel implied by that specific map rather than moving a candidate route after inspecting geology. Test continuous connection, not just a wet locality. Present-day elevation alone cannot exclude former deformation, but any proposed displacement must be specified and checked against geological structure and dated markers.

Only after a route, period and physical model are specified can uninspected transect observations become a prospective test. The already-read maps and Bouse papers are discovery evidence, not held-out predictions. No coordinates have been inferred from the small regional figure, no uplift amount has been fitted, and no event date selected. A revised chronology would need independent justification before these geological deposits could explain the historical maps.
