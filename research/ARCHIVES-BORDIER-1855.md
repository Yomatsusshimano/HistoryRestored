# Reported palace height, location and abandonment in Bordier's 1855 guide

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S371; C006. No independent review.

Selected original pages of Henri Bordier's [*Les Archives de la France*](https://archive.org/details/lesarchivesdela00bordgoog) were visually inspected: title at PDF9; printed **27–29** at PDF41–43; printed **391–395** at PDF403–407. Printed page labels were checked rather than inferred from one fixed offset. The complete palace passage and quoted speech occupy pp27–28; p29 is the immediately following **Soubise** plan. The supporting-documents section contains the entire four-article palace item printed as section IV on pp392–393, with preceding and following item boundaries inspected. This is an original 1855 publication, not an inspected 1812 manuscript or construction account. [Inspection ledger](../data/archives-bordier-1855.json).

## A reported structure, with unknown measurement provenance

On p28 Bordier reports that the abandoned palace had risen approximately **two metres above ground**, and places complete abandonment at the **beginning of 1816**. He attributes the reversal to the misfortunes of 1813 and the two succeeding years. The paragraph gives no survey, measurement date, contractor, quantity certificate, foundation depth, or present-day datum. The number describes reported above-ground progress; it is not deposit thickness, excavation depth, or a verified surviving ruin.

This is a specific historical report of executed work worth testing. It makes a blanket inference of zero preliminary construction unjustified. Conversely, repeating the figure in later accounts would not create independent physical corroboration. The inspected paragraph does not identify the underlying document or establish Bordier as an eyewitness.

The abandonment language differs in scope and date from the [1859 legal report](CYGNES-1859-PROPERTY-DISPUTE.md), which reports interruption in 1814. Interruption and complete abandonment may be different stages, but their actual administrative relationship remains unverified. A later study used to route this retrieval gives 1 February 1815 and an August 1813 works start; its citation bundles several works, including Bordier. The inspected Bordier passage does **not** supply those exact dates. Do not assign every statement in a bundled citation to every cited work. The later study remains a navigation aid, not an authenticated works chronology here.

## A narrower location and a map target

On p27 Bordier places the intended palace beside the Champ-de-Mars, at the corner of **quai d'Orsay and avenue de la Bourdonnais**, on the right flank as described in his text, opposite a proposed barracks on the left. Footnote 2 points to a **Plan de Paris published in 1815, chez Jean**. This provides a concrete map-retrieval target beyond the broad Concorde–Iéna frontage in the program.

The map itself has not been acquired or authenticated. Street names and relative placement do not establish exact boundaries, orientation, executed footprint, or overlap with an Eiffel pier. Those require map-edition identification and spatial registration. The following p29 plan depicts the Soubise establishment, with rue des Quatre-Fils, rue du Chaume and rue de Paradis; it must not be reused as the river-front palace plan.

## Ceremony, speech and printed program

Bordier's p27 account and footnote name Montalivet in the ceremony and first-stone inscription, consistent with the [institution-supplied notice](ARCHIVES-FIRST-STONE-1812.md). The full speech quoted across pp27–28 celebrates centralized historical records and the imperial project. It is a later printed attribution; the original speech manuscript and the inscribed plaque remain uninspected. Its rhetoric does not demonstrate an earlier catastrophe or coordinated fabrication.

Section IV on pp392–393 is headed **21 March 1812** and repeats the broad location, 100,000 cubic metres of intended accommodation, successive usable quarters, reserved gardens, stone/iron construction and 200,000-franc first-works allocation. It ends after article 4 before a separate 6 May 1812 archival-access order. This is the complete item **as printed by Bordier**, not proof that he reproduced the entire multi-project decree. It carries no inspected original seal, signed manuscript, administrative reference or copying history.

Two textual controls matter. The downloaded OCR misreads the decree heading as **1813**; the original printed scan reads **1812**. On p393 article 2 visibly prints **Les palais**, whereas the [1844 quotation](ARCHIVES-PROGRAM-1844.md) reads **Les plans**. Preserve that variant without deciding its author, correctness or motive. Agreement on the other specifications does not establish independent textual lineage.

Next retrieve the named 1815 Jean map and original administrative works records. Test the proposed corner against the undated terrain plan and later excavations, and locate records that distinguish ceremony, commencement, interruption, final abandonment and demolition. Actual executed dimensions, reused materials, foundation association and sediment dates remain unknown. All twenty objectives remain active.
