## Observation and event interpretation

The diagrams contain19 CS arrows across9 cores and12 question-mark arrows across6 cores. These are counts of the paper's interpretations, not independently authenticated earthquake counts. The same event could have several local records; some local contacts could be missing, ambiguous or unrelated. Neither31 combined arrows nor the19 definite-looking labels can be substituted for the paper's separate30-contact sandy-unit thickness selection.

The plotted sections depict several distinct patterns: peat/mud burial contacts, sand/mud couplets in accretionary banks, cross-bedded sands in inlet settings, and gravel lags. That heterogeneity is evidence to explain rather than erase through a pooled layer label. A reconstruction needs the original logs, depositional associations and correlatable dates before joining horizons across sites.

KI11's Figure8 log, explicitly reused from Morton et al.(2002), alternates sand and muddy sand beneath a mud cap, has a rounded basal1.5ka age marker, and has no CS or question-mark arrows. PDF25 already states that its alternations may reflect coseismic or aseismic processes. Its basal age does not independently date each transition. The later redrawing and original study are dependent evidence for this core.

W18's0.3ka label appears near a CS arrow but Table3 assigns its age to AD1700. It is not a separate radiocarbon confirmation. Four introduced-oyster arrows are also historical-assumption markers, rather than four calibrated ages. W29 has two rounded0.1ka labels; the drawing agrees qualitatively with the table's young basal median, while the prose's approximately1.3ka remains conflicting.

Each panel has its own elevation axis relative to MTL. Equal vertical positions on the page do not establish a shared level or common event. No cross-core horizon alignment, chronology fit or prospective test is claimed.
