# Streptochilus ecology and the limits of the marine inference

Research draft, 2026-10-08. S187; no independent review.

[Darling et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0902827106) report genetic correspondence between living planktonic *Streptochilus globigerus* and benthic *Bolivina variabilis*. Their genetic sample comprises five individuals and nineteen clones; these are not nineteen independently collected organisms. Chamber-level Mg/Ca analyses involve eight tests. Temperature interpretation uses a multispecies planktonic calibration. The inspected text also acknowledges earlier complications in benthic Mg/Ca-temperature relationships.

This evidence distinguishes life position from salinity. A species capable of growing in both bottom and water-column habitats challenges a strictly planktonic classification. Both sampled settings remain marine. It does not demonstrate freshwater tolerance.

The [Bouse supplement](BOUSE-MARINE-LEDGER.md) identifies *S. cf. subglobigerum* and *S. latus*. Those names cannot silently be replaced with the living species studied genetically. Applying the living result to fossil forms needs morphological and ecological justification; the shared genus alone is insufficient.
