# Arctic hyena teeth: a locality with reworked fossils

Research draft, 2026-10-08 (America/New_York). C013/S24; no independent review.

[Tseng, Zazula and Werdelin (2019)](https://doi.org/10.5334/oq.64) describe two isolated teeth as *Chasmaporthetes cf. C. ossifragus*. The qualifying **cf.** is retained: the authors reserve a firm species assignment because the material is limited. This audit read the seven-page paper's relevant text and visually checked Figure 1 (page 2), Table 1 and the locality/age descriptions (page 3). No tooth was handled or independently identified.

The locality is CRH 11A, a lower Old Crow River point bar, reported at **67.819° N, 139.904° W**. This is a collection-locality coordinate, not a demonstrated place of death or an exact position for each tooth. The source does not specify a geodetic datum or positional error. The map uses the reported decimal coordinates for orientation without a datum transformation; it does not plot migration routes.

## Three different chronological statements

1. **Regional context:** page 1 describes fossils reworked from multiple Pleistocene/Holocene units into point bars. Page 3 gives an approximate 1.4–0.012 Ma age span in its locality age discussion.
2. **Authors' preferred tooth interpretation:** page 3 favors no younger than approximately 0.85 Ma, probably nearer 1.4 Ma. Page 5 reasons from other North American occurrences and the lack of younger hyenas in well-sampled faunas.
3. **Direct specimen date:** none supplied here. No radiometric result or laboratory uncertainty is assigned to either tooth in this inventory.

The preferred age therefore depends partly on the broader fossil record. It is not an independent radiometric check of that same record. A contrary younger age should be tested through specimen identification, preservation and dating quality, not rejected solely because it disagrees with the existing range.

## What the locality establishes and leaves open

The paper supplies identifiable museum objects and a northern collection locality supporting investigation of an Arctic occurrence. The age and transport limits prevent converting that occurrence into a single dated ecological community. Co-collection from a river bar is insufficient to establish coexistence with camels, mammoths, muskoxen or other predators. It is also insufficient to show that all material entered the bar during one ancient flood.

A dispersal interpretation needs dated populations along a feasible route; a catastrophic transport interpretation needs source terrain, transport evidence and depositional ages. Neither can be obtained simply by connecting fossil points on a map. The paper's proposed predator competition should likewise remain a hypothesis rather than an observed cause of extinction.

The comparison paragraph on page 3 refers once to **CMN 24957**, whereas the referred-specimen section, figure caption and table use **CMN 24958**. The record follows the consistent identifier and retains the discrepancy for catalog checking. It does not silently merge two museum objects.
