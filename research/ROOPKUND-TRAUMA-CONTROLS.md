## Findings and their evidentiary roles

The supplement describes incomplete, mostly disarticulated cranial material. It separates sharp cracks interpreted as later damage, possibly from landslides, from three unhealed compression-fracture cases interpreted as near-death injuries. Hail is proposed as a possible explanation for some deaths, without an established date or specimen-level join to the dated samples. It explicitly notes that long-bone lengths were unavailable for DNA-tested individuals; a morphology-to-genetic-group association is tentative.

The causal distinction matters: an injury assessment is not a sediment measurement, and a possible near-death impact is not a uniquely identified projectile or weather event. A hypothesis of landslide damage after death does not establish when remains moved, how far, or whether their present positions share one deposition episode. The inspected trauma passage describes three cases by anatomical location without catalog numbers linking them to the dating ledger. Such a join may exist in collection files; its absence here is not proof those files never existed.

## What cannot yet be combined

| Proposed combination | Evidence required before adoption | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Injury and calibrated age | Authenticated skull-to-sampled-bone association or direct dating of identified injured element | No join established |
| Injury and genetic group | Same association, with collection and laboratory identifiers | No join established |
| Three injuries and one storm | Compatible individual dates plus diagnostic causal and contextual evidence | Neither shared date nor unique cause established |
| Later bone damage and one redistribution | In-place deposit/collection context and independent movement-age evidence | No dated redistribution unit established |
| Morphological robustness and ancestry | Measurements on associated DNA-tested elements with documented matching method | Authors' tentative association remains a hypothesis |

Do not fill these gaps by transferring the mean date of a genetic group to an unlinked skull, treating lack of healing as an exact death timestamp, or grouping every crack as one catastrophe's injury. Likewise, the hail explanation must earn its fit rather than becoming the default because it appears in a peer-reviewed supplement.

## Next discriminating records

Recover the injury specimens' accession numbers, photographs and original descriptions; trace the Lucknow/Pune/Kolkata collection histories and any element associations. Keep field associations, curatorial reconstruction and laboratory sampling distinct. Compare diagnoses using named observations and independent specialist assessment. Seek contemporaneous or independently dated environmental records only after the specimen-age joins are established. For movement of remains, prioritize contextual and stratigraphic records rather than inferring a deposit date from damage morphology.
