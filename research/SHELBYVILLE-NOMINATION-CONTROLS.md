# Shelbyville: recovered 1976 nomination and its limits

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York). C030/R004; S393–S394. No independent review. [Inspection and retrieval ledger](../data/shelbyville-nomination-controls.json).

The district nomination is now publicly accessible through the [National Archives record, NAID 28894113](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/28894113), despite the earlier NPS placeholder downloads. The complete delivered file has 94 pages. This audit examines selected nomination pages, maps and two streetscape photographs; it does not claim a complete inspection of the packet or its later correspondence.

## Retrieval and dates

Illinois' [alternative-access guide](https://dnrhistoric.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/dnrhistoric/preserve/siteassets/pages/hargis/accessing-national-register-documentation-outside-of-hargis.pdf), visually inspected as a complete page, directs researchers to the National Archives using the register number. Searching 76000729 returned the Illinois district record and an advertised [original scan download](https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/electronic-records/rg-079/NPS_IL/76000729.pdf). The full delivered PDF's byte count and SHA-256 are preserved in the ledger. It is linked rather than copied into this repository.

PDF page 5 identifies Robert Wagner, Staff Researcher, Historic Preservation Services (Paul Sprague), and dates preparation **27 May 1976**. PDF page 1 records receipt **28 June 1976** and entry **22 December 1976**. These are distinct administrative dates, not construction or sediment ages. The file contains later material; its entire contents should not be assigned the nomination preparation date.

## A selected inventory, not every building's history

PDF pages 7–12 contain entries 1–31 under the selected significant-sites section. All six pages were visually inspected. **Neither 212 nor 216 E Main is individually identified in those entries.** This is a bounded finding about that list, not an assertion that the buildings are missing from all survey material or were considered historically unimportant.

The description on PDF page 3 reports 398 structures, excluding garages and minor outbuildings, with different significance/survey groupings. Those summary categories do not supply 398 individual construction records. Its discussion of new street-level facades and later additions/alterations describes changes to visible architecture; it does not measure a raised street or establish buried original storefronts.

Selected builder attributions provide concrete leads for the broader construction audit:

| Nomination entry | Reported attribution | What remains to authenticate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8, PDF page 8 | Courthouse built in 1880; architect O. H. Placey | Original contracts, plans, construction accounts and surviving fabric |
| 10, PDF page 9 | Conn Brothers established in 1865; contracting work including the courthouse | Business and project records; this is a later historical assertion |
| 25, PDF page 11 | 318 West Main, built in 1858 by Haydon Bros. Contractors | Original parcel/building records and attribution basis |
| 28, PDF page 12 | Library at 154 North Broadway, built in 1904; Patton & Miller | Original design, payment and construction records |

These attributions neither identify the builders of 212/216 E Main nor establish inheritance or miscrediting. The bibliography on PDF page 5 names an 1881 county history, an 1891 biographical record and an unpublished local history. These sources have not been traced here to claim-specific original documents. Repeating the nomination's names in later publications would not constitute independent confirmation.

## Maps and photographs

PDF pages 14–15 and 59/61/63 were visually inspected. They provide district boundaries, significant-site locations, building-stock categories and photo directions. The building-stock map distinguishes significance, prior survey photography and broad before/after-World-War-II categories. It is not a dated deposit map. No exact age category has been assigned to the modern 212/216 polygons without controlled registration.

PDF pages 18/20 show Main Street views; pages 19/21 are their caption backs. The caption attributes views 1a–b to R. Wagner, **May 1976**, looking east from **121 West Main**. The drawings on page 61 support the general photo direction. The images show storefronts, upper stories, sidewalks and a distant courthouse. They do not expose the sidewalk vault or identify its construction date. No exact modern 212/216 facade match or quantitative grade comparison is adopted from these views.

## What changes next

The missing nomination is recovered, and its bibliography and earlier survey dates now identify specific record families to pursue. PDF page 1 names the Illinois Historic Structures Survey (October 1973) and Historic Landmarks Survey (July 1974). Parcel-specific survey cards, original deeds and dated photographs are better next targets than treating this selected district narrative as a complete building inventory. Original grade profiles, the Reddit photograph and a dated physical deposit remain missing. All twenty objectives remain active.
