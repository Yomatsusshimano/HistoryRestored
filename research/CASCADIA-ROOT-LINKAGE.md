# Cascadia root linkage and final-ring evidence

2026-10-09. Source-level audit of physical tracing, width correlations and the sampled wood. These evidential roles are distinct. No specimens, field notebooks or current accession inventory were inspected.

## What the Copalis field account reports

S60, [Jacoby, Bunker and Benson (1997)](https://web.njit.edu/~dbunker/publications/Jacoby%20etal%2097%20Geology.pdf), printed p.1001/PDF p.3, describes a dead red cedar on the Copalis riverbank at the upper end of a dead-cedar stand. The paragraph was visually inspected. The authors report that the trunks had lost outer rings through decay and/or fire. The sampled tree's root section retained bark and an intact outer ring around most of its perimeter; slight abrasion at the top had removed outer cells. Three trunk cores and three radii from the root section were averaged for the Ozette comparison. The authors identify a complete final ring assigned to1699.

This is an explicit primary publication's report that root and trunk belonged to one sampled tree. It is stronger than inferring association from file labels alone. The paragraph does not print GF2/CP-GF2 identifiers or show the sampled tree's root-to-trunk continuity. Figure3 on that page depicts survivor-ring plots, not a physical tracing photograph of this dead cedar. S61's GF2 table grouping and the complete numerical trunk crosswalk provide the documentary bridge; a contemporaneous field/accession crosswalk remains unrecovered.

## Seven traced trees in the supplement do not mean seven exact final years

S231's [methods and TableS1](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/paleo/treering/measurements/correlation-stats/wa130.txt), sections “Correlation between trunk and root” and “Roots,” were text-read. The authors describe vertical wood slices extending from trunk to root in seven trees, and width-pattern matches in four trees. Some trees have both kinds of evidence. TableS1's seven T rows are CP-789, CP-791, JN-560, PXnt778, PX-782, PX-783 and GR-777.

Two of those traced trees are absent from the eight exact-endpoint rows: CP-789 is recorded only as >1680, and PXnt778 as >1670. The supplement explains that such limiting dates concern bark retained only along radii toward which rings taper away. Tracing can establish relative continuity while an incomplete radial sequence still supplies only a bound. The count of seven traced trees and the five T flags in the eight-row main table therefore refer to different subsets.

GR-777 makes the distinction particularly clear: TableS1 reports width r=.28 over62 rings as not significant under its stated P<.01 criterion, alongside T. The source's physical tracing supports its relative ring link even though this short width comparison fails that criterion. The width statistic must not be upgraded to significant, nor may physical tracing be erased because of it. Our archive has not independently inspected the slice.

## Consequences for the chronology test

There is primary documentary evidence of sampled-tree association and of physical tracing for specified trees. The correct remaining limitation is that this investigation has not authenticated those physical observations or custody, rather than that the publications supply no association evidence at all.

Tracing provides a check on relative trunk/root ring correspondence that does not depend on a high width correlation. It still inherits the trunk's calendar placement, and roots and trunks are parts of the same trees. Neither the T count nor duplicate numerical records supplies independent absolute dates. Bark and latewood observations constrain a growth endpoint conditional on correct ring identification and calendar placement; tree death and earthquake onset also remain distinct, as the1708 exception illustrates.
