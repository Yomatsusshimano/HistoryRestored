# Dead Canyon: retain the conflicting bone result

Research draft, 2026-10-08. Regional comparison for the mammoth investigation; not an independently reviewed chronology.

[Barton (1999), S156](https://dnr.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/ger_washington_geology_1999_v27_no2-3-4.pdf), printed/PDF p.24, site 01, reports post-cranial mammoth remains from a blowout within the Touchet Formation at Artesian Coulee/Dead Canyon. The visually inspected entry gives **4,905 ± 140 radiocarbon years BP, GX-1457**, describes it as anomalously young, and contrasts it with an **11,000–13,000 yr BP stratigraphic interpretation**. This is a different locality and specimen record from Coyote Canyon.

The [structured record](../data/dead-canyon-date.json) keeps both statements. The entry does not identify the exact dated bone element, dated chemical fraction, pretreatment, collagen quality, blank correction or confidence level of the printed uncertainty. The stratigraphic range's precise timescale definition is not supplied there. No calibrated interval or significance of the discrepancy is calculated by mixing these numbers.

Calling the assay anomalous does not demonstrate contamination. Conversely, its numerical precision does not establish that it dates the animal's death accurately. Possible measurement/preparation problems, mistaken specimen or stratigraphic association, later intrusion, or a valid younger age must be discriminated using records and material. They are alternatives, not findings. The reported result must remain visible even though it challenges the older interpretation.

The entry cites Newcomb and Repenning's 1970 fossil paper, Newcomb's 1971 map and Waitt (1980). The 1970 paper remains unrecovered. The [downloaded USGS 1971 PDF, S157](https://pubs.usgs.gov/imap/0653/report.pdf) contains a cover and four pages of accompanying reservoir geology and groundwater text, all visually inspected. It does not contain the map sheet or GX-1457 assay details. The retrieved file therefore cannot stand in for the missing original dating evidence simply because the compilation cites its publication.

This is an unresolved local dating/context discrepancy. It neither establishes a common global catastrophe nor justifies rejecting all radiocarbon or conventional stratigraphy. It also supplies no evidence that the result was concealed: the younger value and laboratory identifier are printed in the public 1999 geological publication.

Next recover the original laboratory report and fossil paper, trace the specimen accession and sampling history, and inspect the bone-to-bed association. If preserved material permits a later independent assay, compare explicitly identified fractions and quality controls. Until then, retain GX-1457 as a reported, unaudited measurement rather than selecting either chronology as established.
