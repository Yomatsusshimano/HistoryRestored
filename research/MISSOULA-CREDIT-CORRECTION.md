# A published correction restores geological discovery credit

2026-10-09. C008 / S11, S336. Selected original scans inspected; no independent review.

The [2020 corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103401), appended as PDF p. 53 to the [author-linked review](https://dlgeorge.github.io/pubs/OConnorBakerEtAl2020.pdf), explicitly credits **Thomas Cooney** with discovering and guiding the authors to four ice-rafted erratics near Hawk Creek. It also acknowledges his guidance at Telford Crab Creek sites. The authors attribute the omitted credit to changes during manuscript revision and report that the first online version incorrectly called him Thomas Cooley. This is their published account of the mechanism; original drafts and first-posting bytes have not been recovered.

The retrieved review's Table 3, printed/PDF p. 39, has four Hawk Creek rows: three cite O'Connor field sites 05-19-2019-04, -05 and -14; the fourth cites Thomas Cooney's October 17, 2018 written communication. The appendix therefore contains his correct name, while the appended correction extends discovery credit to all four. A field recorder and discoverer need not be the same person. Neither the underlying correspondence nor original field visits was inspected. This copy cannot itself authenticate the reported earlier Cooley spelling.

The correction also changes a Glacier Peak age range from 13.7-13.5 to 13.7-13.4 ka as a typographical correction. It supplies no new assay and does not date every erratic. Available-online date is October 13, 2020, printed on the corrigendum.

This gives objectives 16 and 18 a concrete, bounded attribution case: a named contributor and an explicit public correction. It does not identify ancient erased builders, demonstrate intentional suppression, establish the catastrophe hypothesis or prove worldwide historical rewriting. The review and its authors' correction are dependent records. Preserve the correction alongside the table rather than treating the uncorrected attribution as the complete account.
