# Allen's Creek: a documented 1902 flood-response control

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York). S429–S431; follow-up to Reddit lead R005. No independent scientific review.

The [July 8, 1902 council proceedings](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020708.pdf), S430, PDF1/2 (printed317/318), were visually inspected. A resolution's recital attributes destruction of seven culverts, loss of embankments and impassable streets to recent rainfall increasing Allen's Creek waters. Council adopted a call for a July16 special election to seek $25,000 for repairs. That is an election call, not an inspected result or completed expenditure. Council also adopted a request for the fire commissioners to supply siphons and help removing water from cellars in the flooded district. Addresses, depths and completed aid remain unknown.

This documents contemporary municipal reporting of a damaging flood and cellar water. The account places the flooding before or by the July8 report, with its day provisionally narrowed by the newspaper below; duration and original rainfall measurements remain unverified. The seven-culvert count is reported rather than independently mapped. No sediment profile, thickness or deposit age is supplied. Cellar flooding does not by itself establish that mud buried buildings or caused a present bricked opening.

The [August 4 proceedings](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020804.pdf), S429, PDF1/2 (printed325/326), were visually inspected. A Board of Public Works report discusses rebuilding culverts destroyed by recent floods, then recommends repairing the culvert at **Liberty and First** and removing a reported **two-foot tile across First feeding it**. The record does not separately identify this culvert as one of the seven destroyed in July. Recommendations and reports received/filed are not proof of executed work. Adjacent estimates concern other named culverts or Oxford Road and cannot measure Liberty sediment.

The City Engineer's text mentions an Allen's Creek map and submitted plans; those attachments were not inspected. The July and August records plausibly concern a shared local flood response, but exact event identity remains unverified. They share municipal lineage and are not independent physical observations. The Liberty/First location narrows a drainage search; it does not identify S428's1898 Schlenker property or the surviving brick building.

The complete two-page July8 delivery is hashed in the [structured controls](../data/ann-arbor-schlenker-controls.json). A separate11-page [July7 packet](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020707.pdf) includes the same printed317 page; PDF1/11 were visually inspected. The filename does not date the enclosed July8 session. Overlapping pages are one record, not extra witnesses. The five-page August delivery is hashed; only PDF1/2 were visually inspected. The August web-reader502 was followed by a successful normal public download.

Next acquire the submitted map/engineering plans, event-day rainfall or contemporary newspaper account, completed culvert repairs and identifiable cellar records. Join historical parcels and construction phases before comparing original floor/ground levels and natural strata. This local flood is a candidate comparison, not a date assigned to the broader catastrophe hypothesis. All twenty objectives remain active.

## Contemporary newspaper: a dated storm and a mud report

The [July 11, 1902 Argus-Democrat](https://aadl.org/files/documents/pdf/aa_argus_democrat/aa_argus_democrat_19020711.pdf), S431, PDF1, describes a storm on Monday afternoon. The preceding Monday was **July 7**, an inferred event date consistent with the July8 council response. This is documentary dating, not the age of a sampled deposit. The headline says over two hours while the body gives3:30–5; retain that discrepancy. A box attributes **2.45 inches of rain** to the University Observatory but says yesterday afternoon. Its precise date, gauge and observation window require the original log or daily edition; do not assign that reading to July7 as authenticated data.

PDF4, Aftermath of the Flood, reports that **over14 inches of water** covered the Ann Arbor Fluff Rug Co. main floor and carried mud onto stored carpets. It reports mud remaining after the water receded. This is a useful contemporary sediment-related lead, but water depth is not mud thickness. No sampled deposit, measured mud thickness, stratigraphy or composition is supplied. The inspected paragraph gives no business address. Its parcel and any relationship to the Schlenker building or original Reddit images remain unverified.

The complete eight-page issue is hashed in the structured controls. PDF1/4 and selected rainfall/mud passages were visually inspected; the remaining pages were not fully reviewed. The newspaper supplies a separate contemporary reporting channel, not a proven independent physical measurement. Next identify the business in contemporary directories and maps, authenticate the original weather record, and locate surviving deposits. This finding supports a local flood investigation; it does not establish a shared worldwide catastrophe.


## Address control: rug factory and hardware store are separate leads

The [1902 city directory](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/directory-1902-aa-glen_v_mills.pdf), S432, PDF90/printed86, lists the Ann Arbor Fluff Rug Factory, Schlemmer Bros., at **409–421 Huron W**. PDF296/printed292 instead gives **409–429 Huron W** for the same proprietors. Both entries were visually inspected; the discrepancy is printed, not merely an OCR substitution. Retain both ranges until a parcel/map crosswalk resolves them. The volume's title confirms1902 (PDF5), but its exact compilation date and event-day occupancy are unknown.

A [June24,1898 advertisement](https://aadl.org/files/documents/pdf/aa_democrat/aa_democrat_18980624.pdf), S433, PDF2, lists the office and factory at **409–411 W. Huron Street** and names Geo. P. and Henry J. Schlemmer. S418, the [1906 Beakes volume](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bf/Past_and_present_of_Washtenaw_County%2C_Michigan_%28IA_pastpresentofwas01beak%29.pdf), PDF381/printed377, reports a move from East Huron to409–421WestHuron without dating the move. These are commercial and biographical reports, not surveyed boundaries or independently verified occupancy. Their shared business informants may limit independence. Differing ranges do not by themselves prove expansion.

This gives the1902 newspaper's mud report a plausible **West Huron business-location target**. It remains distinct from the **213–215 West Liberty** Schlenker building candidate and from the original Reddit images, which are still unmatched. Do not transfer the rug business's reported water depth, floor level or mud to another address.

S432 PDF297/printed293 separately lists **Christian Schlenker's hardware business at116LibertyW** and **residence at213same**. That distinction narrows the building-history test: a residence address in1902 is not proof the later brick hardware store already existed. Compare original maps and construction records with S418's1906 account of a recently completed store. No original residence fabric, replacement date or shared foundation is established by this entry.

Next acquire contemporary parcel/fire-insurance maps, resolve the West Huron ranges and July1902 occupancy, and examine site-specific engineering or sediment records. Continue comparing the116/213WestLiberty phases separately. No deposit thickness, original Reddit building identity or common catastrophe chronology is established.


## Map control: factory beside creek, then changed channel depiction

The [April22,2013 Phase I assessment for415WestWashington](https://www.a2gov.org/media/xw5f1typ/415-washington_phase-i_2013.pdf), S434, contains EDR reproductions of Sanborn sheets. PDF92 (August1899, Volume1Sheet3) and PDF93 (December1908, Volume1Sheet6) show the named Fluff Rug factory south of WestHuron, north of WestWashington and west of the railroad, beside a depicted creek. PDF94 (June1916, Volume1Sheet10) still names the factory but no longer depicts that same surface channel. These selected map pages were visually inspected. This spatial evidence strengthens the WestHuron factory target without resolving every address range or proving July1902 occupancy.

The assessment, PDF33/printed28, interprets the change as rerouting Allen Creek into underground storm sewers between1908 and1916. Preserve that as a map-based interpretation until construction/as-built records establish execution and timing. Channel omission does not itself measure burial, prove a sudden event or explain Schlenker's openings. Three editions and the later report are linked evidence, not four independent observations.

The report's subject is415WestWashington, **not the factory parcel**. Its urban-land soil classification indicates mechanical reworking; this is mapped context, not a measured natural layer or a test of1902mud at the factory. No factory sediment thickness, depositional age, original floor elevation or regional ground-level change has been adopted. Only PDF1/2,13,33,92–94 of the793-page delivery were visually reviewed; unreviewed appendices may contain further leads. The complete public delivery is hashed; the commercial map compilation is linked rather than mirrored here.

Next obtain original sheets, drainage construction records, a modern parcel crosswalk and site-specific boring/excavation sections. Distinguish disturbed fill from a natural flood layer through stratigraphy, composition and dated context. This creates a testable local flood/drainage case while leaving the broader catastrophe and chronology claims unresolved.

PDF162/printed30, under WashingtonStreet geological evaluation, describes four exploratory borings to20.5feet and predominantly sand with minor gravel. It reports upper5–10feet commonly heterogeneous and interprets that material as fill, with source unknown. This is a report-level description, **not an adopted sample-specific thickness of natural mud**.

The section heading names WestWashington, but its opening wording refers to NorthMainStreetMunicipalGarage; the geological paragraph returns to WashingtonStreet and mentions W-2. Retain this internal inconsistency. Inspect individual W-series logs (referenced AppendixXI) and a location plan before assigning depths to points. These observations provide a useful disturbed-ground comparison. They neither establish nor exclude an older natural flood layer beneath or within fill. The source and age of the heterogeneous material remain unknown; no1902date, rug-factory parcel join, Schlenker opening cause or regional burial thickness is adopted. Next inspect logs, coordinates, contacts, material descriptions and dated inclusions. The three properties remain separate.
