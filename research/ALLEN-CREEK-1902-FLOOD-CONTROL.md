# Allen's Creek: a documented 1902 flood-response control

Research draft, 2026-10-11 (America/New_York). S429/S430; follow-up to Reddit lead R005. No independent scientific review.

The [July 8, 1902 council proceedings](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020708.pdf), S430, PDF1/2 (printed317/318), were visually inspected. A resolution's recital attributes destruction of seven culverts, loss of embankments and impassable streets to recent rainfall increasing Allen's Creek waters. Council adopted a call for a July16 special election to seek $25,000 for repairs. That is an election call, not an inspected result or completed expenditure. Council also adopted a request for the fire commissioners to supply siphons and help removing water from cellars in the flooded district. Addresses, depths and completed aid remain unknown.

This documents contemporary municipal reporting of a damaging flood and cellar water. The account places the flooding before or by the July8 report, but its exact day, duration and rainfall measurements are unverified. The seven-culvert count is reported rather than independently mapped. No sediment profile, thickness or deposit age is supplied. Cellar flooding does not by itself establish that mud buried buildings or caused a present bricked opening.

The [August 4 proceedings](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020804.pdf), S429, PDF1/2 (printed325/326), were visually inspected. A Board of Public Works report discusses rebuilding culverts destroyed by recent floods, then recommends repairing the culvert at **Liberty and First** and removing a reported **two-foot tile across First feeding it**. The record does not separately identify this culvert as one of the seven destroyed in July. Recommendations and reports received/filed are not proof of executed work. Adjacent estimates concern other named culverts or Oxford Road and cannot measure Liberty sediment.

The City Engineer's text mentions an Allen's Creek map and submitted plans; those attachments were not inspected. The July and August records plausibly concern a shared local flood response, but exact event identity remains unverified. They share municipal lineage and are not independent physical observations. The Liberty/First location narrows a drainage search; it does not identify S428's1898 Schlenker property or the surviving brick building.

The complete two-page July8 delivery is hashed in the [structured controls](../data/ann-arbor-schlenker-controls.json). A separate11-page [July7 packet](https://aadl.org/sites/default/files/docfiles/Minutes_19020707.pdf) includes the same printed317 page; PDF1/11 were visually inspected. The filename does not date the enclosed July8 session. Overlapping pages are one record, not extra witnesses. The five-page August delivery is hashed; only PDF1/2 were visually inspected. The August web-reader502 was followed by a successful normal public download.

Next acquire the submitted map/engineering plans, event-day rainfall or contemporary newspaper account, completed culvert repairs and identifiable cellar records.
