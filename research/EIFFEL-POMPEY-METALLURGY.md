# Pompey furnaces and the limits of a production-capacity argument

2026-10-11. C006 / S340. Sourced draft; no independent review.

The [CNAM volume](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/redir.php?ident=8XAE353.5&onglet=c) contains Hallopeau and Campredon's metallurgy chapter. Its [title scan](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?8XAE353.5/0002) prints **1893**, whereas 1889 names the exhibition. Printed105-107 and127 were visually inspected; the first delivery view is blank. [Acquisition ledger](../data/eiffel-metallurgy-acquisition.json) records the selected image URLs, byte lengths and hashes. Images are linked rather than republished. No complete-volume inspection is claimed.

## What the source actually measures

| Locator | Reported item | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p127 / vue130 | Two Fould-Dupont blast furnaces at Pompey, each capable of110tonnes per24hours, operating for casting/refining iron | Rated pig-iron output; neither observed utilization nor finished tower-part production |
| p107 / vue110 | Tower metal-weight row printed7 347 230 | Table-wide weight notation suggests kilograms, or7347.230tonnes; the row has no separate unit label or defined component exclusions |
| p107 | Information collected from industrial exhibits, mechanical/chemical results and supplied notes/brochures | Compiled industry account, not an original acceptance-test register |

Two times110 gives220tonnes of rated pig-iron capacity per24hours. Dividing a tower weight by that number would not estimate construction time: it would omit conversion yield, puddling, rolling, cutting/drilling, rival orders, downtime and assembly. The furnaces' reported capacity in this exhibition survey also does not authenticate their configuration throughout the tower's manufacturing period.

The tower row must remain separate from Eiffel's reported6360tonnes of workshop deliveries. His figure excludes site rivets and directly supplied accessories; this survey gives no matching component boundary. Subtraction cannot identify missing iron, reuse or a production deficit. The scope and unit interpretation need reconciliation against the underlying weight schedules before those totals become comparable.

## Dependence and hypothesis test

This publication is earlier than Eiffel's1900monograph and has different named authors. That establishes a separate documentary witness, not independent measurement of Pompey's tower deliveries. The authors' own description identifies information supplied by exhibitors. Their industrial praise and information sources must be retained when weighing corroboration.

The survey supplies a concrete upstream industrial facility to investigate. It neither proves that every tower piece was newly made nor supports inheritance merely because dispatch records remain unacquired. Neither account should be made to fit a predetermined result.

An inaccessible local-history page was a search lead for a claim of4200acceptance-test bars. Its normal HTTPS request failed certificate-name validation, and the web fetch failed. The original source of that count was not recovered; it is **not an accepted measurement or a claim attributed to S340**. No certificate bypass was used.

The next evidence needed is the original Fould-Dupont order/test/dispatch sequence, with batch identifiers joined to Levallois part registers and site receipts. A preserved batch-level acceptance series could test newly rolled material much more directly than rated furnace capacity. No labor sufficiency, component manufacture date, inherited structure, common catastrophe, independent review or completion is established here.
