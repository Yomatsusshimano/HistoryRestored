# Carbon-thread lamp tests: observations, failures and dates

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C007 remains SOURCED_DRAFT. No independent historical or experimental review.

The October notebook provides a participant's account of actual tests, beyond the assertions in the March metal-wire patent draft. It names Charles Batchelor and records both light production and failures. It does not establish exclusive invention, independent replication or recovery of older technology.

## Original records and scope

Two archive items in notebook N-79-07-31 were recovered through their public IIIF manifests. All five images were visually inspected and preserved under the archive's displayed CC0 license. [Structured records and image hashes](../data/lamp-trial-records.json) distinguish documentary dates, reported observations and missing measurement conditions.

- [N052105, S177](https://edisondigital.rutgers.edu/document/N052105): two images, right-hand pages 105 and 107; microfilm 33:594. Page 105 is headed October 22, 1879, 9 a.m., with Batchelor's name. It describes carbonizing cotton thread attached to platinum wires, then placing it in a bulb under vacuum. It reports roughly half a candle of light using 18 cells, resistance initially 113 ohms and subsequently 140. Vibration is suggested as a cause, not demonstrated. Page 107 lists eleven material/preparation entries; that is not eleven successful lamps or a denominator for a success rate.
- [N052111, S178](https://edisondigital.rutgers.edu/document/N052111): three images, right-hand pages 111, 113 and 115; microfilm 33:596. Page 111 explicitly points to pages 105, 107 and 115, linking numbered tests within the notebook. Page 113 contains filament sketches; these alone do not identify the final shape of every tested lamp. Page 115 records the duration and failures below.

The archive's metadata and original handwriting are distinct evidence layers. Neither documentary dating nor these downloaded images independently authenticates the paper, ink or chain of custody. This is selected notebook coverage, not the full experimental archive.

## What the tests report

| Entry and locator | Reported observation | What cannot be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No.9, pp.105/111 | Cotton-thread carbon, about half a candle with 18 cells; 113 ohms initially, later 140; p.111 gives 1:30 a.m. connection | No calibrated luminous output, voltage, current, vacuum pressure or modern efficacy |
| No.9, p.115 | On from 1:30 a.m. to 3 p.m., explicitly labelled 13½ hours; then raised to three gas jets for one hour, after which glass cracked and the lamp failed | Not 14½ hours at constant output, a population mean lifetime, or a documented filament-only failure |
| No.2, p.111 | About 22 candles on 18 cells | No duration, calibration or controlled comparison with No.9 supplied here |
| No.3, p.111, continuing on p.115 | About 1½ gas jets; lead wire melted; conduction across mica proposed tentatively | Tentative mechanism must not become a diagnosed cause |
| No.10, p.115 | About one gas jet, considerable resistance, and a small arc | Light cannot be assumed wholly incandescent or directly comparable to another lamp |
| No.11, p.115 | Numerous tar-treated examples reportedly broke during carbonization | Count unknown; no numerical failure rate |

Clock arithmetic agrees with the written duration: 15:00 minus 01:30 is 13.5 hours, assuming the same day. Adding the reported extra hour gives 14.5 hours across two operating stages. That sum is a calculation from the account, not an independently observed duration. A gas-jet comparison is retained in its original unit; no conversion to candles or lumens is imposed.

Resistance readings are reported measurements without recovered instrument calibration or uncertainty. Cell count is not voltage: battery chemistry, arrangement, condition and load would be needed to reconstruct electrical input. The entry time of 9 a.m. is not substituted for the explicitly recorded 1:30 a.m. test start.

## Preserve the date discrepancy

Page 105 says October 22; page 111 visibly says October 21. The digital archive catalogs the two items accordingly. The [edited Document 1831, S179](https://edison.rutgers.edu/images/archive/innovations/TAEBdocs/Doc1831_EL_10-22-79_linked.pdf), printed p.449, note 9, argues that the latter portion was written on October 22 despite its October 21 heading, because it discusses the preceding night's experiments. That note was visually checked; the edition's selected text was also read. Its editorial reasoning is preserved as an interpretation, not silently substituted for the manuscript date. The edition and scans represent the same underlying notebook, not separate witnesses.

The cross-references support continuity between the records, but do not independently prove the calendar correction or a global chronology change. Candidate explanations include a mistaken heading or an entry spanning working sessions. The exact act of inscription remains unresolved. The old edited-PDF URLs failed because those particular paths were unavailable; the current essay links supplied a working /images/archive/ path. The earlier failed access was not evidence of missing underlying records.

## Consequence for attribution and next tests

Batchelor is a named contributor in a contemporary technical record, and the text describes collective work. Assigning every observation to Edison alone would erase this documented participation; assigning all invention credit to Batchelor from these pages would exceed them. Successful light production, unstable resistance, manufacturing failures and changed test conditions coexist in the record. This supports an experimental development account without proving every later commercial or biographical claim.

The [patent comparison](LAMP-DRAFT-TO-PATENT.md) remains a separate metal-wire design lineage. These carbon tests must not be used to validate the March platinum/iridium performance assertions. The [Upton follow-up](UPTON-LAMP-CALCULATIONS.md) now inspects N-79-08-22:171-179 and adds conditional power calculations. Specimen correspondence and measurement procedures remain unresolved. A second staff account of the same trial would offer corroboration, not an independent experiment. Ownership instruments and contributions outside this notebook remain unresolved.
