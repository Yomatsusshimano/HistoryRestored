# Fish Creek: what the older age-model comparison tests

SOURCED_DRAFT, 2026-10-08. This is an audit of model dependencies, not a new chronology or independent review.

## Inspected source

[Dorsey et al.2011, S214](https://pages.uoregon.edu/rdorsey/Downloads/DorseyEtal2011.pdf), printed774–775 and783–784, was read as extracted text; Fig11 on printed785/PDF15 was visually inspected. The paper describes mapping to account for structural omission or duplication. It anchors26 reversals at228 sites using a fossil-based Miocene–Pliocene boundary at691m and U–Pb tuffs at4454/4488m, dated2.65±0.05/2.60±0.06Ma. Its preferred correlation and two alternatives are compared using present-day thicknesses; the alternatives are rejected for accumulation-rate spikes and disagreement with the tuff constraints. ClassB magnetic sites have less well-resolved components but polarity interpreted from great-circle trends. These are source descriptions, not reproduced measurements.

## Dependence matters

The numerical ages assigned to the lower magnetic sequence are conditional on both its correlation to the polarity timescale and the reconstructed ordering/thickness of the section. The fossil boundary is an input to the selection described here. Agreement between that input and the resulting chronology cannot then be counted as an entirely separate successful prediction of that same boundary. This does not erase other observations or establish that the age model is wrong.

Figure11 compares alternative time assignments to the plotted section. It is not a demonstrated test of the specific2021 fault-duplication reconstruction. A geometry-changing proposal must first specify which observations are repeated, how the sites reconnect, and how its restored sequence fits the retained tuffs and other constraints. Merely shifting a correlation line on the unchanged thickness axis is a different test.

The source's accumulation-rate objection also needs a scoped interpretation. A rapid-deposition hypothesis cannot be rejected solely because it requires rates unlike a preferred smoother history: abrupt deposition is precisely what it proposes. It still must meet independent constraints on sediment supply, transport, facies, physical continuity and dated units. Conversely, calling a rate spike possible does not resolve an actual mismatch with a reliable dated tuff. The two objections must be evaluated separately.

## Current constraints and discriminating work

| Constraint | What is currently documented | What remains to be tested |
|---|---|---|
| Original structure | S215 flags a faulted interval below04Pw30; S214 reports structural mapping | Exact traced contacts, offset markers and competing restorations at that interval |
| Young sanidine | S210 selected-group arithmetic reproduced; location corresponds closely to the earlier magnetic site | Collection/bed identity, raw reduction and calibration, alteration or contamination tests |
| Magnetic polarity | S215 assigns04Pw30 normal polarity, gradeb, six analyzed samples | Site-specific demagnetization trajectories, selection and primary-versus-later remanence |
| Lower age anchor | S214 explicitly uses a fossil-based boundary | Original specimens, stratigraphic ranges and reworking status; do not count an assumed anchor twice |
| Upper tuffs | S214 gives two U–Pb ages and section heights | Original analytical and depositional-context audit; retain these constraints in competing restorations |
| Rate history | Figure11 shows alternatives with much sharper peaks | Reproduce input horizons/rates, then test transport and accumulation feasibility under each geometry |
