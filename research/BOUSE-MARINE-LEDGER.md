# Marine interpretation: taxonomy and recoverable sample records

Research draft, 2026-10-08. S186 supplements S183; no independent specimen review.

The marine interpretation has a recoverable identification rationale and named laboratory records. Its supplement provides more than an unsupported environmental label. It does not, by itself, supply the specimen images, abundance matrix or age calibration needed to reproduce the complete interpretation.

## What the taxonomic notes actually support

Under *Streptochilus cf. S. subglobigerum*, the authors describe five to six chamber pairs, chamber shape and sutures, a patterned wall becoming finely cancellate, and a loop-shaped aperture with an internal plate. The `cf.` qualification is preserved: do not promote this to an unqualified species determination. A separate *Streptochilus latus* entry describes five chamber pairs, a smooth porous wall and an internal apertural plate. These are the authors' morphological observations; this archive has not reclassified their specimens.

The ecology paragraph cites observations of living Streptochilus in marine surface waters and isotope-based interpretations of deeper upper-water-column habitat. Those cited studies remain to be inspected directly. This is an explicit ecological argument, but not a measurement of the fossil organisms' water salinity. It also does not demonstrate absence from every possible nonmarine setting.

Other entries disclose identification limitations: juvenile Globorotalia is left at genus level, Neogloboquadrina specimens are described as poorly preserved, and Tenuitella retains a `cf.` designation. The Ammonia discussion acknowledges taxonomic confusion and occurrences in estuarine and saline-lake settings. Thus the supplement itself does not justify treating every foraminifer occurrence as uniquely marine.

The species notes do not assign the listed Streptochilus forms to individual Hart Mine Wash laboratory IDs or reproduce the 99% biserial assignment used by S184. That quantitative claim still requires the main paper's assemblage data and specimens. Broad fossil codes in the location ledger are not a species-count table.

## Consequence for the comparison

Both studies address Hart Mine Wash, but locality overlap is not a demonstrated sample join. The lowest explicitly elevated south-bluff record recovered here is 112.4 m, while the Bright DCL sample is reported at 111.2 m. Two additional south-bluff records lack numerical elevations, and other ledger rows have not been fully audited. Therefore this subset cannot establish that the marine study omitted the transition, or that nominal elevations identify identical beds. A field-section correlation with datum and uncertainty is required.

Age fields such as late Miocene to early Pliocene are interpreted assemblage ages, not independent numerical assays or measured deposition intervals. The recovered notes do not reproduce the paper's 8.1-5.3 Ma biostratigraphic calculation. Neither rejecting nor accepting that age is warranted solely from this supplement.

Next retrieve the main article's specimen plates, occurrence/count tables and zonal calibration; match them to these IDs. Inspect the cited living-Streptochilus ecological work and test its applicability to the fossil forms.
