# Artists Drive: similar glass, distinct stratigraphic controls

2026-10-09. C005/S328; primary-source audit, not independently reviewed.

The original [USGS 2001 field guide](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2001/ofr-01-0051/ofr-01-0051.pdf), Stop C3 by Jeffrey R. Knott and Andrei M. Sarna-Wojcicki, supplies a missing link in the [Willow Wash investigation](WILLOW-WASH-NOMLAKI.md). PDF113-117, printed C105-C109, were visually inspected. Search excerpts can omit intervening headings: the original Table C3-1 places FLV-119-WW under **lower Nomlaki**, not the separate Nomlaki Tuff heading. Neither the table nor this audit authenticates the entire mixed sample as one eruption.

## What the original table preserves

Table C3-1, printed C108/PDF116, lists FLV-119-WW as row15, alongside JRK-DV-39 and JRK-DV-104 under lower Nomlaki (>3.58 Ma). Its note identifies FLV-119-WW as the Black Hole section sample at Willow Wash. The source says groups represent best matches/inferred correlations. Values are weight-percent oxide recalculated to a 100% fluid-free basis; they must not be silently equated to the raw 2008 values.

Selected nine-oxide rows, original labels, group headings and the PDF hash are retained in [structured records](../data/artists-drive-nomlaki.json). The table does not label the FLV-119 row as a light- or dark-coloured fraction, report individual shard distributions, or identify analysis2199. Consequently it cannot yet be joined to the fraction-specific 2021 record as the same analytical population.

## Distinguishing similar eruptions

The prose on C106-C107 describes a lower-Nomlaki ash as much as60cm thick, reversed in magnetic direction, below the lower Mesquite Spring tuff. The lower-Nomlaki age estimate is **3.58-3.9 Ma**, based on reverse polarity and stratigraphic position below the reported3.35Ma lower Mesquite Spring control. This is an interpreted bracket, not a local direct mineral age or a confidence interval. The eruption source remains uncertain.

C109 describes the separate Nomlaki Tuff as a25cm bed about4m above the lower Mesquite Spring tuff. Its3.27Ma age is attributed to a personal communication from A. Deino. That original assay and calibration are not supplied by this selected passage; the value is not substituted for the later Dry Creek analysis.

The authors distinguish lower Nomlaki from Nomlaki using heavy rare-earth element composition, shown in Figure C3-4 for JRK-DV-39 and reference Nomlaki samples. That plotted distinction has not been reproduced numerically here. It is a specific proposed discriminator, not proof that FLV-119 shares the same trace-element signature. The paper also discusses similar major-element compositions across substantially different Mesquite Spring/Glass Mountain ages, reinforcing why glass resemblance alone cannot transfer a date.

Figure C3-3 depicts separate sections and polarity assignments. Its caption says solid connecting lines represent either continuous exposure **or** high chemical similarity; dashed lines represent inferred correlation. A solid line therefore cannot automatically be reported as a physically traced bed. Original field measurements, correlation coefficients, and paleomagnetic raw data remain uninspected.

## Relationship to the 2008 estimate

The [2008 age audit](NOMLAKI-AGE-CONTROLS.md) reports a4.06±0.46Ma lower-Nomlaki estimate combining two extrapolations, including Artists Drive section2. The 2001 chapter contains that named section and relevant controls, but does not supply the exact pair of extrapolated ages, weights, or later error-model calculation. Its earlier bracket and the later estimate use different constructions; this audit neither equates them nor declares them statistically contradictory.

Next recover quantitative rare-earth analyses of JRK-DV-39, FLV-119's separate populations and the reference Nomlaki samples; identify whether the 2001 FLV-119 row represents one component. Then recover the precise age-depth inputs and hiatus assumptions used for Artists Drive section2 in2008. No regional target-bed date, historical waterway, or catastrophe follows from these correlations alone.
