## Arithmetic reproduced; geographic model not reproduced

TableS1 gives relative pixel counts at30arc-second resolution under an assumed LGM sea level135m below present. The [calculation](../analysis/bat_island_area_check.py) reproduces all three rounded loss percentages:

| Group | LGM pixels | Current pixels | Recomputed loss | Printed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hispaniola group |112050 |92958 |17.04% |17% |
| Little Bahama Bank |23450 |3312 |85.88% |86% |
| Northern Lesser Antilles |15175 |1292 |91.49% |91% |

The formula uses LGM pixels as denominator. These are not authenticated square-kilometre measurements. Reproducing subtraction/division does not validate the input masks, bathymetry, projection, sea-level assumption or spatial classification. Original rasters and area-weighting would be needed to check the physical estimates. A longitude/latitude pixel grid does not imply uniform ground area; the table alone supplies no correction audit.

FiguresS2a–c show predicted climatic habitat at present,6ka and21ka against present-day island boundaries. They do not display a fully reconstructed21ka coastline. Colored model habitat must not become an observed fossil range, an animal corridor or evidence of abrupt shoreline change. Separate time slices also cannot measure the rate or duration of a transition between them.

The regional groups must stay separate. A large estimated land loss for the Little Bahama Bank does not establish the same percentage for Hispaniola, much less an excavation history for Jérémie. Raster estimates and habitat projections from one study are not independent geographic confirmations. Checking them remains relevant to wildlife and old-geography objectives, but neither a common catastrophe nor a causal extinction mechanism follows.
