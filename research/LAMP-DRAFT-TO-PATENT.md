# March 1879 lamp draft and US 227,229

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C007 remains SOURCED_DRAFT without independent review.

The surviving manuscript and granted specification show a detailed documentary development relationship for a metal-wire vacuum lamp. They do not establish successful performance, absolute priority, a Woodward rights transfer, or recovery of ancient technology.

## Technical statements versus measured results

Draft pages 55-59 describe insulating coatings, an infusible bobbin, a second enclosure, evacuation and gradual electrical heating before glass sealing. Pages 61-67 assert increased light without melting and propose an explanation involving gases and cracking. These are the author's assertions and mechanism, not independently verified material science or a dated table of trials.

Page 69 specifies about 30 feet of wire, .005-inch diameter and approximately 750 ohms when incandescent; it names platinum and iridium, but this inspection does not resolve whether the handwritten wording means an alloy or alternatives. The grant's PDF p.2 explicitly uses alternatives. Do not silently replace uncertain manuscript wording with the clearer later wording.

Pages 69-77 connect high resistance and parallel lamp operation (the historical term is multiple arc) to smaller supply conductors and economy. Pages 79-83 describe the thermal regulator and alternate enclosure arrangements. These are design arguments. No measured network efficiency, lamp lifetime, reproducible vacuum pressure or comparative trial series was recovered from this item.

The metal-wire design must remain separate from the carbon-filament disclosure in US 223,898, already examined in the [broader patent audit](LAMP-PATENT-COMPARISON.md). Sharing high resistance and evacuation does not make the March draft the original filing of that later carbon patent.
