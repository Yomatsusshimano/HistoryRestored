# Field-control meaning and the Grand Coulee outlet-history conflict

2026-10-11. C008 / S27, S29. Selected original scans inspected; no independent field verification or hydraulic run.

## What the upper constraint represents

[S27](https://dlgeorge.github.io/pubs/DenlingerGeorgeEtAl2021.pdf), printed pp.7–8 / PDF8–9, describes preserved loess and uncrossed divides as upper-stage indicators. Nearby definite erosion or crossing strengthens a bracket; erosion alone gives limited information about water depth. A lone erratic supplies a lower limit and may have been deposited below peak stage. These are interpretations of geomorphic observations, rather than direct recordings of peak water level.

Table1 row45 attributes the Babcock noncrossing interpretation to Waitt, with a quad-derived elevation. The inspected methods do not provide that point's soil section, photograph, mapped boundary, excavation history or event assignment. Do not infer that this exact point carries preserved loess merely because the general methods use it elsewhere.

Our conditional 433/431m contradiction requires the two printed heights to constrain one level surface. A dynamic model can have different stages at different places and times. That possibility needs actual outputs; it cannot be used to excuse any discrepancy without testing it. Likewise, absence of a mapped crossing does not establish a numerical depth threshold without authenticating the field interpretation.

## An acknowledged structural reconstruction problem

[S29](https://doi.org/10.1130/2021.0062(07)), printedp268 / PDF24, explicitly retains a conflict between model routing and stratigraphic interpretations. An open, deeply incised Grand Coulee diverts water from western routes; the cited stratigraphy suggests an outlet near470m early in the last glacial period. Ice filling an already incised channel and an earlier, incompletely retreated rock cataract are distinct alternatives. The authors propose a hybrid sequence but leave incision timing and effects unresolved.

Printedp263 / PDF19 describes a Frenchman Springs gravel exposure at47.0154,-119.9981. It is a separate site, not the nearby Babcock noncrossing record. Rounded spherical screening gives approximately22km separation from each Babcock control; site labels must not substitute for point-specific provenance. The guide overlaps S27 authors and references its simulations, so it supplies interpretation and retrieval leads rather than independent hydraulic replication.

## Tests that could distinguish outlet histories

The following are investigation requirements, not fitted scenarios or successful predictions. They retain source alternatives without selecting whichever route makes a known control fit.

| Candidate outlet state | Required physical record | Required model comparison | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incised rock channel, open to flow | Authenticated channel geometry and sediment evidence tied to the tested flood episode | Routing and stage with that open geometry; test western bounds and eastern drainage together | Actual published input/output package missing |
| Incised rock channel restricted by ice | Evidence of ice position and blockage at the relevant time, separately from bedrock excavation age | Explicit ice geometry, lake storage and drainage; retain the same field-validation rules | Blocking surface and event correlation unverified |
| Rock cataract not yet fully retreated | Stratigraphic order that permits a higher rock barrier during the nominated flood | Explicit former rock geometry and a water/erosion budget for its removal | Original geometry and removal timing unverified |

An earlier excavation date does not by itself prove an outlet was ice-free. A modeled need for blockage does not prove that rock, ice or sediment supplied it. A successful present-terrain routing calculation would not establish the former surface. These are separately testable connections.

Before converting row45 into a strict model rejection threshold, obtain its original field annotations and identify the preserved surface, survey reference and relevant flood episode. Then compare modeled stages with authenticated one-sided bounds, including coordinate and vertical-reference limits. Simultaneously check the outlet sequence against the original stratigraphy cited by S29, rather than relying on the model's preferred route to date the incision.
