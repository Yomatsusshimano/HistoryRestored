# Crust, mountains and drainage: a test of rapid deformation

Discovery shortlist, 2026-10-30 (America/New_York). Six purposively selected targets, not six unexplained events or a prevalence estimate. No independent archive review.

## Revised question

The project owner proposes that a pulse of crustal movement raised some regions, lowered others and tilted terrain, changing drainage without requiring a global mud blanket. We distinguish temporary seismic shaking, permanent fault displacement, regional warping, sediment liquefaction and long-term mountain building. A mechanism demonstrated locally does not establish a simultaneous global episode. The original twenty objectives remain intact; this is a separate operational branch of H1/H2.

## What would make the proposed explanation competitive?

1. **Fix the event being tested.** Declare a candidate time window, duration, footprint and displacement pattern before combining sites. Preserve the recent-historical, Holocene, late-Pleistocene and million-year cohorts separately. Dates above concern different objects and methods; they are not an event-age dataset ready for a statistical fit.
2. **Predict a geometry.** In a small local coordinate system, a first tilt model is vertical displacement `w(x,y)=a+b*x+c*y`. A plate-wide calculation needs Earth curvature and a common geodetic reference. Compare this with a localized fault step and a rebound dome. Infer displacement only from demonstrably correlative surfaces after accounting for original gradients, water-level changes and datum/geoid effects. A shoreline is an approximate former equipotential, not automatically a globally flat ruler.
3. **Explain the water route.** Reconstruct the outlet and basin before/after movement. Demonstrate whether water could escape, pond or reverse course, and account for eroded material. A drained lake, a tilted basin and an excavated canyon require different observations.
4. **Look for the same dated disruption elsewhere.** Require compatible independent event constraints plus a spatially coherent deformation pattern. Similar-looking valleys or exposed marine fossils alone cannot establish common timing. Retain stable, well-sampled controls where the model predicts movement.
5. **Keep failures.** A recent common event is challenged if securely dated displacement episodes do not overlap, young intact strata cross its predicted ruptures, or observed deformation is better predicted by separate local processes. Detection and preservation must be adequate before treating an absent trace as adverse evidence.

The present shortlist supports the physical possibility of abrupt regional movement and drainage change. It does not establish one recent event linking the Appalachians, Alaska and Grand Canyon. Nor does it establish inherited structures, chronology fabrication or the other attribution propositions.
