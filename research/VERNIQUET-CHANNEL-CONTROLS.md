# Verniquet facsimile: title date and unresolved channel classification

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S360; C006. No independent review.

Paris Musées / Musée Carnavalet catalogs a nineteenth-century heliogravure series titled *Paris de 1789 à 1798, Fac-similé du plan de P. Verniquet*. Six delivered sheets were acquired and surveyed together in a thumbnail overview. Complete images of the [title sheet, plate XXXI](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/en/node/640022), [western river sheet, XXXI C](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/en/node/640055), and [Champ-de-Mars sheet, XXXI D](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/de/node/640067) were visually inspected, with selected title and western river crops. Sheets B, E and F were only surveyed in the overview; their details have not been authenticated. [Acquisition and inspection ledger](../data/verniquet-channel-controls.json).

## Title and date roles

The reproduced title names **VERNIQUET**, describes a geometrical survey on the Observatoire meridian, and reads **Parachevé en 1791**. That is a reproduced claim of completion, not proof of the physical facsimile's production year or an exact survey date for every feature. The museum's object title covers **1789–1798**, while its catalog dates the reproduction objects to the **nineteenth century** and attributes reproduction work to Paul Dujardin, Charles Chardon and Imprimerie Nationale.

Eiffel's inspected S17 foundation opening refers to **Berniquet (1789)**. The newly inspected title therefore gives an author spelling and date-role difference that must remain visible. Identification with the same mapping project is a plausible retrieval inference, but the exact edition consulted by Eiffel is not authenticated. No date is silently corrected, and no historical fabrication follows from differing survey, completion, represented-period or reproduction labels.

## What the western sheet shows

Sheet C depicts the Seine and a long parcel-divided tract along its left-bank side, separated from the adjoining mainland by a narrow drawn feature. The area south of it connects toward the Champ-de-Mars/Ecole-Militaire setting shown across sheets C/D. This supplies qualitative geographic context for the former island/channel account and for the [earlier Roussel facsimile](ROUSSEL-CHANNEL-FACSIMILE.md).

However, at the delivered resolution the exact island label, all small shoreline annotations and the meaning of the narrow feature have not been securely transcribed. The distinction between an open channel, residual ditch, boundary or partly filled watercourse requires the map legend and a clearer original impression. Eiffel's statement that the 1789 map shows a ditch is **not independently confirmed** by this inspection. No complete channel outline, width, water depth or infilling rate is extracted.

Actual sheet widths range from 1476 to 1516 pixels despite `styles/4k` in their institutional URLs. Crops add no original resolution. No map-registration fit, modern coordinates, foundation-shaft placement or quantification of the Roussel-to-Verniquet change was performed.

## Dependence and the next test

S359 and S360 reproduce different named maps, but both are delivered through the same nineteenth-century facsimile atlas tradition. Six companion sheets are not six independent observations; the earlier impressions, copying fidelity and exact represented epochs remain untested. Institutional images are linked and hashed, not mirrored here.

An authenticated original impression and its legend are needed to classify the narrow feature. Original channel-filling authorizations, works records and dated sections would test when and how it changed. Stable control points must be registered before linking the river-side tract to Eiffel's foundation shafts. The 0.89-metre prose-layer mismatch remains a separate issue. No common catastrophic burial date or chronology rewrite has been established. All twenty objectives remain active.
