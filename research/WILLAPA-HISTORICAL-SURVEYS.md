## Recovered original1873 sheet

NOAA's [Historical Map & Chart Collection](https://historicalcharts.noaa.gov/) returned T01341B-00-1873, catalog title *Shoalwater Bay WA Sheet No9*, T-Sheet,1873,1:10,000, U.S. Coast Survey. The unchanged [public JPEG](../sources/originals/cascadia/T01341B-00-1873.jpg) is27,434,630 bytes and9286x15909 pixels; SHA256 `d60df01a4bf9543d486157efe336fb7652cb28f1b4299989fd3dcb2a17e675dc`. The full sheet was visually inspected at a reduced display scale; not every small annotation was transcribed.

Its printed heading names the U.S. Coast Survey, SectionXI, Shoalwater Bay, W.T.,1873 and SheetNo9. It depicts the southern peninsula between Pacific Ocean and Shoalwater Bay, including lakes, vegetation patterns and settlement/landscape linework. Manuscript annotations accompany the original heading; their complete dates and purposes remain unverified. Do not assign every mark to1873 from the heading alone.

This is a genuinely recovered nineteenth-century map, but the southern-peninsula footprint does not supply the northeastern Willapa marsh observations needed for S245's recovery argument. The sheet's patterns have not been matched to an original symbol manual or modern ecological classes. Vegetation hatching is not automatically a measured high-marsh boundary. No coordinate, elevation or area is derived from this inspection.

##1922 report: original survey limitations

The [NOAA-hosted T03921 descriptive report](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/desc_reports/T03921.pdf) has seven pages. Its cover/title and PDFp3 were rendered and visually inspected. The [unchanged public report](../sources/originals/cascadia/T03921-descriptive-report.pdf) preserves all seven pages; pp4-7 remain uninspected. Automated PDF text extraction yielded blank content; findings use visual reading. The renderer reported missing display fonts, while the inspected image text remained legible.

The title identifies a Willapa Bay shoreline revision,1922, with Fred L. Peacock as chief of party. PDFp3 names W.H. Overshiner Jr. and C.K. Green as survey contributors. The account lists rerun coastal segments, low-water line observations made where tides permitted, and instrument-dependent positioning. It reports work during rainy weather and retention of survey cuts that cleaning might obliterate.

The report describes apparent clay-bank sloughing south of Goose Point that was barely sufficient to appear at1:20,000. It reports16square statute miles of area,21.7statute miles of shoreline, and5miles of roads/sloughs. These are reported work totals, not a measured erosion volume or the surveyed length of every bay margin. Selected revision coverage is not proof of unchanged unvisited shoreline. A low-water line run under favorable tide conditions is not automatically the same vertical reference as S245's interpreted ordinary-high-tide marsh boundary.

## Modern metadata is not the date of the landscape

[NOAA InPort64142](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/64142), WC46C03, identifies the historical timeframe as1922-01-01, with2021 creation and2026 catalog update. The record describes vector conversion, georeferenced raster inputs and NAD83 coordinates; it links T03921. Those later dates concern data processing/cataloging, not newly formed coastlines. The generic January1 timeframe is not treated as an exact observation day.

A separate [NOAA InPort64144](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/64144) has a1926 timeframe and different footprint. It was read for route selection, not used as1870s evidence. Neither readily accessible product can substitute for the target nineteenth-century marsh maps. No vector files, transformation accuracy or field-to-map positional comparison have been audited here.
