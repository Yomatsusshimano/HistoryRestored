# Sanson candidate antecedent: a shared drawing, not a second survey

2026-10-08. C005; S188 compared with S15 panel II. SOURCED_DRAFT; no independent review or modern georeferencing.

## Inspected object

Stanford's [Glen McLaughlin collection object xw933tj8982](https://purl.stanford.edu/xw933tj8982) supplies a scan of *Le Nouveau Mexique, et la Floride*. Its title attributes the compilation to Nicolas Sanson; the cartouche prints Paris, Pierre Mariette and **1656**. The title says it draws on various maps and accounts. This is a visible imprint, not an independently authenticated printing or survey date.

The institutional IIIF manifest distinguishes a 1656 state from a 1679 state and reports reissues. Those are catalog claims; the inspected image itself prints 1656. We have not inspected the other states. This candidate therefore does not resolve why the later S15 cartouche attributes panel II to **Sanson 1666**. Do not silently substitute one printed year for the other.

## Visual comparison

Full-sheet images and enlarged northern-coast/title details were inspected. These are qualitative correspondences, not fitted coordinates.

| Feature | S188 Sanson image | S15 panel II |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern island outline | Deep narrow indentation between elongated projections; small offshore islands | Same distinctive arrangement, redrawn |
| Northern mainland | Agubela de Cato | Agubela de Cato |
| Northern island label | R. de Estiete | R. de Estiete |
| Adjacent interior label | Talaago | Tolaago; lettering differs |
| Western coast sequence | C. Blanco, C. de S. Sebastien, C. de Mendocino, Pto. de los Reyes, Pto. de Monte Rey, Pto. de Francisco Draco | Corresponding sequence with spelling/abbreviation changes |
| Latitude | Northernmost island coast above the native 45-degree level | Same relationship |
| Layout | Broad New Mexico/Florida map with extensive inland information | Narrow California excerpt in a multi-map comparison |

The combination of named features, unusual outline and relative placement supports a shared cartographic lineage. It does not establish that this particular physical impression was copied directly, exclude an intermediate edition, or identify the ultimate observations behind the drawing. The two images must not be counted as independent confirmations of a former channel. Conversely, copied geometry alone does not prove that the original geography was false; independent terrain and survey evidence still decide that question.

## Corrected northern label

Our preceding panel-II note and structured record incorrectly read the northern land label as **Anian**. Enlarged comparison reads **Agubela de Cato** in both images. Corrected in the current records; the earlier error remains visible in Git history. No identification of this named land with a modern locality is made.

## Coordinate and chronology limits

Native longitude numerals are visible, but no prime-meridian conversion or projection has been established. The sloping meridians make raw pixel alignment inappropriate. Similar latitude placement is an internal drawing comparison, not measured agreement with modern latitude. A map's printed year cannot date a coastline transition.

Next inspect candidate Sanson editions around 1666 and the comparison sheet's source statement, then identify defensible modern control points with explicit uncertainty. Preserve multiple possible landmark identities. Only after that can a route and geological transect be fixed. The already viewed images remain discovery evidence, not held-out prediction successes.
