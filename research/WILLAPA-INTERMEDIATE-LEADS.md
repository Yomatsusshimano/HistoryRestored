The report describes Grassy Island as attached to the peninsula, and reports gradual northern-peninsula accretion during1965–1999 followed by tip erosion in mapping beginning1999. These statements concern named broader landforms, not measured change at historical vector49/51or22/50/52. They provide original-source retrieval targets rather than a georeferenced third observation. The vegetation-distance discussion cites Phipps1990; the erosion discussion refers to Washington State Department of Transportation maps without giving exact sheet IDs in the inspected passage.

The report describes soil and habitat heterogeneity on Grassy Island: stabilized sandy dune terrain and southern salt-marsh soils. It also discusses non-native beachgrass, vegetation succession, fire suppression and restoration beginning2002. These are reported mechanisms capable of altering vegetation boundaries and imagery appearance. They are not authenticated causes at our selected historical trace coordinates. A change in marsh code or green tone cannot be assumed to represent either sediment catastrophe or ecological continuity.

## What can be tested next

These accounts supply discriminating tests beyond the1922/2006endpoints: recover the1955and1966images, Phipps's1977–1987comparisons and the1999-onward WSDOT maps; identify actual observation dates, shoreline definitions, footprints and uncertainty. The official Ecology publication list identifies Phipps's *Coastal Accretion and Erosion in Southwest Washington:1977–1987*,90-21, August1990. Its original report has not been inspected here. Catalog and later report references are dependent on their originals, not additional independent surveys.

The attachment described in2010and separation reported by the1966caption create a testable topological history. Neither account has been spatially authenticated to the precise historic outline. They support testing local coastal and vegetation evolution as explicit alternatives, while leaving rapidity, cause and global correlation unresolved. No intermediate georeferenced boundary, signed change or catastrophe age is claimed.
