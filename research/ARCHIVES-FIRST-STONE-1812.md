# A dated first-stone notice and a separately cataloged palace design

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S369–S370; C006. No independent review.

Two images supplied by Archives nationales to Wikimedia Commons were acquired through ordinary HTTPS and visually inspected in full. The public file descriptions identify the notice as **AB/Vb/1/4** and the design image as **AE/II/3809**; their embedded institutional photographic metadata repeat these references. These are exact retrieval targets, not a completed examination of the original archive folders. Unchanged image copies and their hashes are preserved in the [acquisition ledger](../data/archives-firststone-1812.json).

## A reported ceremony, not a works account

The [first-stone notice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Affiche_annonçant_la_pose_première_pierre_du_palais_des_Archives_de_l%27Empire_sur_l%27Ile_des_Cygnes-_Archives_antionales-AB-Vb-1-4.jpg) dates the palace's foundation to **15 August 1812**, using the printed Roman date XV août M. D. CCC. XII. Its body says the first stone **was laid by Jean-Pierre Bachasson de Montalivet**, minister of the Interior. Louis Bruyère is listed among those present as director of public works of Paris; Pierre-Claude-François Daunou appears as keeper of the imperial archives, and Jacques Cellérier as architect. A selected body crop was also inspected to check those roles. The imprint identifies Mame's printing house.

The catalog calls this an announcement of the first-stone laying. The original's completed-action wording instead reports or commemorates the laying. It does not directly establish the exact printing day, physical stone's survival, construction volume, depth of foundations or subsequent progress. The heading celebrates Napoleon's reign; it does not say Napoleon personally laid the stone. Roles in the presence list must not be substituted for the named stone-layer.

The description associates the notice with the Île des Cygnes. No explicit island name was recovered from the notice's printed body. Its exact ceremony location therefore remains a catalog association, rather than an independently registered findspot. The modern repository coordinates on Commons describe the institution and must not be used as palace or foundation coordinates.

## A completed-looking image can depict an unexecuted proposal

The [separate design image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Projet_pour_le_Palais_des_Archives_de_l%27Empire-Archives-nationales-AE-II-3809.jpg) depicts a large quadrangular palace with interior courts beside a river, with surrounding buildings and a distant domed building. The institutional description identifies it as a **project designed by Jacques Cellerier**, dated **1812**, reference **AE/II/3809**. The delivered image is 1263 × 841 pixels. Its catalog date and attribution are metadata; no legible date or signature in the displayed image independently authenticates them here.

This image is not a photograph of a completed building or an executed foundation plan. The pictured palace cannot establish actual height, floor area, underground remains or surveyed distances. Its quadrangular composition also cannot be equated automatically with the elongated pink emplacement in the [undated Carnavalet site plan](ARCHIVES-PALAIS-SITE-PLAN.md). Their design versions, orientations, scales and exact footprint relationship remain unverified.

| Record | What this release establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S369 original notice image | A printed claim of a first-stone ceremony on 15 August 1812, naming the stone-layer and participants | Executed foundation extent, receipts, subsequent construction or exact coordinates |
| S370 original design image and description | A separately identified Archives palace project, cataloged 1812 and attributed to Cellerier | Completed palace, material age, actual construction dimensions or identity with S368 |
| S367 printed 26 March 1813 letter | Later prospective foundations and plan requests | Retraction of the ceremony or certification of channel filling |

The 1812 ceremony report and 1813 prospective foundation wording refer to different kinds of action. The records alone do not establish whether the ceremony preceded substantial foundation work, which construction stage the letter meant, or whether particular plans changed. Preserve that unresolved sequence; do not force it into either a fabricated chronology or a fully reconciled construction narrative.

## Preservation and remaining test

Commons identifies both deliveries as Archives nationales contributions through cooperation with Wikimédia France and labels them with the Public Domain Mark. This archive preserves the [unchanged notice](../sources/originals/Archives-AB-Vb-1-4-firststone.jpg) and [unchanged design](../sources/originals/Archives-AE-II-3809-project.jpg), crediting **Archives nationales (France), photographic workshop**. Embedded metadata remain intact. A checksum proves byte consistency, not historical authenticity or independent corroboration.

Next inspect AB/Vb/1/4 and the associated decree, ceremony minutes, construction accounts and execution drawings; obtain AE/II/3809's full provenance and version relationships. Match actual works and dated parcels to later excavations before assigning buried masonry. No authenticated works quantities, exact tower-parcel connection, common deposit or historical-rewriting mechanism has been established. All twenty objectives remain active.
