# Missoula: repeated beds and a conditional chronology

2026-10-08. C008 / S29. Sourced draft, not independent field verification.

Source: Waitt, Atwater, Lehnigk, Larsen, Bjornstad, Hanson and O'Connor (2021), *Upper Grand Coulee: New views of a channeled scabland megafloods enigma*, pp. 245–300, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1130/2021.0062(07)); [institutional PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10635924). Relevant text pp. 273–277 inspected; Figure 17B–H (p. 273) and Table 1 with accompanying chronology (p. 277) visually checked. This is a field guide integrating earlier work and new interpretation, with overlapping authors; it is not independent replication of every underlying observation.

## Evidence and conditions

At the Ranger Station section, Stop 2.3, the authors report northward current indicators in four flood beds but southward indicators in intervening varved sediment (p. 275). Thus there is a directional observation to test, beyond mere visual repetition of layers. Stops 2.4 and 2.5 link that section to a wider composite through stratigraphic comparisons. The reported coordinates are transcribed in [the structured record](../data/missoula-stratigraphy.json); datum and positional error are not guessed.

The composite contains approximately 1,800–2,600 counted/interpolated couplets interpreted as annual varves (p. 276). Figure 17 explicitly includes interpolation across uncounted packets and neglects losses by erosion and fault truncation. Compound couplets produce different conservative and generous counts. This is an interpretive range, **not a statistical confidence interval**, and neither endpoint is a secure total duration without validating annual deposition and the composite correlations. The schematic intake histories also neglect isostatic effects.

These reports challenge the proposal that the entire nominated sequence formed in one uninterrupted rapid event. To overturn that interpretation, a competing model must explain the intervening sediment, opposed flow directions, correlated sections and preservation, rather than merely relabeling each couplet as a pulse. Conversely, counting layers does not by itself prove annuality or the identity of every flood's water source. Original logs, photographs, seasonal sediment indicators and correlations remain necessary independent checks.

## Absolute ages are conditional

The guide equates an ash age with charcoal W-3141, reported as 12,910 ± 160 radiocarbon years BP and calibrated there with IntCal20 to 15.9–15.0 cal ka at 95% confidence (p. 277). Using that anchor and the couplet counts, it places the last Lake Missoula floods at 15.8–14.9 cal ka. The latter is a derived chronology, not another independently measured radiocarbon age. No calibration has been rerun here.

Table 1 divides 26 analyses from one Sanpoil field sample into groups of 19 and 7. Group a is correlated with Sg; group b resembles older set C and is described as possibly reworked. The prose retains uncertainty about the mixture's origin. Nineteen analyses are not nineteen independent eruptions or nineteen independently dated layers. The structured record preserves this dependency, which matters before using the ash as an exact regional tie point.

Detrital wood USGS-1860 is reported as 14,490 ± 290 radiocarbon years BP, with a calendar interval of 18.6–16.9 cal ka; inferred deposition is 17.2–15.8 cal ka (Figure 17D). Wood age and depositional age must remain separate. The older wood is consistent with a reworking explanation; these overlapping ranges do not measure a precise storage time.

## Original 1986 count and annuality argument

[Atwater (1986), S163](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1661/report.pdf), printed pp.11,14,16, is now directly inspected. Page 11 reports counted ranges totaling 1,586–2,291, then estimates additions for erosion (100–200), nine uncounted intervals (200–300), and mostly arhythmic deposits (200–400). The stated 2,000–3,000 total is explicitly rough. Endpoint addition gives 2,086–3,191; this arithmetic is not a corrected duration or confidence interval. These older quantities must not be silently substituted for the later guide's differently scoped 1,800–2,600 range. See [structured transcription](../data/sanpoil-varve-counts.json).

Pages 14 and 16 argue that regional glacier chronology allows at most approximately three years per typical couplet and that one year is plausible, using a proposed fraction of the glacier's excursion spent blocking the Columbia River. This is a contextual consistency argument. Our inference: an upper bound alone cannot establish a one-year period or rule out subannual deposition. It also does not demonstrate that rapid deposition occurred. The underlying glacier chronology and blockage-duration assumption require their own checks.

Figure 9 (p.14) compares F-8, F-9 and V-9 across four localities, including lateral changes and inferred erosion. Figure 12 (p.16) shows borehole material bracketing F-11. These give observable correlation and sediment-fabric targets beyond a simple layer count. Neither image by itself dates a couplet. Full plates and bed-by-bed correlations remain unaudited.

Retain the reported repeated-deposition interpretation while testing its timescale separately. Independent seasonal indicators, additional chronological controls, and the paleomagnetic record are next discriminators; repeating the same varve-derived chronology cannot independently validate annuality.
