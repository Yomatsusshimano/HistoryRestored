# Original construction agreement: obligations, ownership and payment evidence

Research draft, 2026-10-11. Source S347; case C006; no independent scientific review.

The manuscript agreement dated **8 January 1887** specifies construction obligations and a **1,500,000-franc contribution paid in three milestone installments**. It distinguishes the City's future ownership from Eiffel's operating rights. These clauses strengthen the documentary construction account; they do not establish that payments occurred or that every component was newly manufactured.

## Source and inspection

Archives nationales (France) supplied the scan of **F/12/3770** to Wikimedia Commons in cooperation with Wikimedia France. [Description and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Convention_sign%C3%A9e_sur_les_modalit%C3%A9s_de_construction_et_d%E2%80%99exploitation_de_la_Tour_de_300_m%C3%A8tres_-_F-12-3770.pdf); [unchanged public PDF](../sources/originals/eiffel/convention-1887/F-12-3770.pdf); [attribution and checksum](../sources/originals/eiffel/convention-1887/README.md); [structured readings](../data/eiffel-convention-1887.json).

The nine digital pages were rendered and visually surveyed. Digital page 1 contains the first manuscript page; pages 2-8 contain paired manuscript pages 2-15; digital page 9 contains manuscript page 16. Payment and ownership pages were additionally inspected at enlarged rendering. This is a targeted clause audit, not a diplomatic transcription of all handwriting or physical authentication.

The opening names Lockroy acting for the State, Poubelle for Paris and Eiffel personally. It cites municipal deliberations of 22 October and 28 December 1886. Article 1 commits Eiffel as contractor to constructing a 300-metre tower, ready for operation at the exhibition opening. Articles 4-6 describe construction scope, project approval and acceptance procedures. Referenced drawings, estimate, reports and municipal deliberations are not separately reproduced as annexes in this PDF; the complete archival dossier remains uninspected.

## Financial and ownership distinctions

| Provision | Original locator | Inspected obligation | What remains unproved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| State contribution | Article 7, manuscript p8, PDF p5 left | 500,000 francs when the metal frame reaches first-floor level; another 500,000 at the second floor; another 500,000 when completed and provisionally accepted for operation | Dates and amounts actually paid; milestone certificates |
| Operating rights | Article 7, manuscript p8; article 11, manuscript p11/PDF p6 right | Exhibition operation and twenty subsequent years beginning 1 January 1890; exhibition operation for Eiffel's profit and risk | Actual revenues, accounts, later amendments or renewals |
| City ownership | Article 11, manuscript p11/PDF p6 right | City becomes owner after the exhibition and return of the Champ-de-Mars park; Eiffel retains the stated operating enjoyment, then returns it to the City | Legal/physical handover records and subsequent changes |
| Security | Article 16, manuscript p15/PDF p8 right | 100,000-franc security; refundable after complete completion and definitive acceptance except 10,000 francs until contract expiration | Deposit and refund transactions |
| Worker relief | Article 17 continuation, manuscript p16/PDF p9 | One-percent retention applies only to the State's 1,500,000-franc allocation; resulting 15,000-franc retention does not exhaust Eiffel's accident responsibility | Actual retention, relief expenditures and accident records |

The three installments sum to 1,500,000 francs. One percent of that amount is 15,000 francs. These simple checks establish the relationship between the contractual quantities, not a reproduced cash ledger. Provisional acceptance for the last contribution installment and definitive acceptance for security release are distinct conditions.

The closing states three originals, Paris, 8 January 1887. Signature marks corresponding to G. Eiffel, Poubelle and E. Lockroy are visible. Reading those marks is not forensic signature authentication. The 2025 Commons upload is a digitization/publication event, not the date of the agreement.

## Comparison with earlier and later records

The [20 November 1886 bulletin](EIFFEL-NOVEMBER-1886-BULLETIN.md) reports a 5 November vote for a 1,500,000-franc subsidy. The agreement supplies a later explicit installment schedule for the same nominal amount. This does not authenticate the earlier commission minutes, establish transfer of funds or prove that the printed report and contract have independent informational origins.

The earlier [3,150,000-franc proposal](EIFFEL-TURGAN-PROJECT-ACCOUNT.md), the contractual State contribution and the later participant account's [supply and total project costs](EIFFEL-SUPPLY-RECONCILIATION.md) describe different roles and scopes. They cannot be treated as interchangeable prices or subtracted to infer missing funds without matched estimates, amendments, receipts and final accounts.

References to earlier municipal deliberations distinguish antecedent site decisions from this final dated construction/operation agreement. They do not, by themselves, resolve every earlier newspaper reference to a convention. That requires the original preceding agreements and their dates.

## Inference and next discriminating evidence

This is adverse evidence against a version of the inherited-tower claim that says there was no explicit contemporary construction agreement or only an unexplained founder attribution. A contract committing to future construction is compatible with the progressive assembly records already inspected. It remains weaker than authenticated completion, delivery and component manufacture evidence for deciding whether all fabric was new.

The next useful records are the three payment warrants and receipts, milestone inspection and acceptance reports, annexed estimate and drawings, commission reports, and original supplier dispatch and batch-test records. Each can test whether the promised sequence was carried out. Missing records in this downloaded document do not demonstrate that they never existed.

No event date, inherited civilization, historical fabrication mechanism, independently confirmed prediction or institutional adoption follows from these clauses. All twenty original objectives remain active.
