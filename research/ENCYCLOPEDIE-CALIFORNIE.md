# California in the Encyclopedie supplement: disagreement preserved

2026-10-08. C005; S189. Original article, volume II, printed pp.131-137; Google Books qWrqlNvt6ZoC. All seven article page images and title leaf PP13 visually inspected. Selected passages transcribed below; no complete critical transcription or independent translation review. The title leaf prints Amsterdam, M. M. Rey, MDCCLXXVI (1776). Article ends with signature (E.); attribution to Samuel Engel follows the bibliographic lead, not an expanded name printed at the article's end.

## Why this changes the map audit

The article explicitly directs readers to the fourth and fifth geographical supplement maps on p.133. Its fifth-map discussion identifies panel I as a map of America from 1604. This connects the prose to the comparison-map project, though it does not authenticate the binding or printing date of the detached LOC impression S15.

The text does not present an uncomplicated march from mistaken island to settled peninsula. It argues over which reports deserve trust, favors older maps in places, challenges Kino's coverage, and acknowledges large disagreements in coordinates. This is primary evidence of published disagreement. It is not a field survey or proof that either preferred geography existed.

## The Kino conflict, stated precisely

Selected French transcription, p.133 left column (long s normalized; line breaks joined):

> Le P. Kino n'ayant point passé Rio de Hila, encore moins le Rio Colorado, n'a point pu rendre compte des rivieres qui viennent de l'ouest; il faut donc s'en tenir aux anciennes cartes qui doivent reprendre leurs droits.

Working translation: Father Kino, not having crossed the Hila River, still less the Colorado, could not report on the rivers coming from the west; one must therefore retain the old maps, which should regain their standing.

The [Kino route audit](KINO-ROUTE.md), S39 pp.316-317 and S40 p.148, records a reported Colorado raft crossing on 21 November 1701. That is a specific conflict about reported travel, not merely differing coastline artwork. The deferred crossing of March 1702 is a separate episode and must not erase the earlier claim.

S39/S40 are later editions of the same attributed participant account, not two independent witnesses. S189 is an eighteenth-century compilation/argument, not an eyewitness denial. Without the manuscript, the particular report available to the article writer, and its transmission history, we cannot decide whether the denial reflects incomplete information, a narrower intended scope, misreading, or another error. It does not establish intentional suppression. Even a confirmed river crossing would not by itself survey the entire northern Pacific connection.

## What remains open

No inspected original dated 1666 was recovered in this search. Dealer references to a 1666 Sanson wall map are retrieval leads, not proof that panel II derives from it. S188 prints 1656; S15 prints 1666; retain both. Next recover the exact report cited by S189 and its coverage of the 1701 crossing, alongside the Sanson edition chain. Also recover the plate volume/title context before assigning a printing year to the detached S15 sheet.

The full twenty-part investigation remains open. This result advances documentary transmission and claim discrimination; it supplies neither a fitted former coastline nor an independently verified chronology rewrite.
