# Muskox population history: observations and model assumptions

Research draft, 2026-10-08 (America/New_York). C012/S23. No independent review.

## Inspected evidence

[Campos and colleagues (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851807/) analyze mitochondrial control-region sequences. Results/Figure 1 describe 149 samples (135 ancient, 14 modern), with regional counts: Urals 26, Taimyr 54, Northeast Siberia 12, North America 14, Greenland 43. The graph's vertical quantity is effective population size multiplied by generation length; its time axis uses radiocarbon years.

The [author-hosted PDF](https://pgl.soe.ucsc.edu/campos2010.pdf), page 5, reports 447 collected specimens, 216 sequencing successes, 162 target-length sequences and 52 additional partial sequences. The latter two counts total 214, leaving a two-specimen reconciliation unresolved here. Thirteen radiocarbon-infinite sequences are discussed separately from the finite-dated analysis (page 2).

The methods use five geographic states, a uniform prior for ancestral location and a continuous-time migration model. The authors favor environmental explanations, but acknowledge insufficient temporal/geographic detail for statistically supported climate comparison. Their human-arrival tests concern Taimyr and Canada. These statements are source reports, not independently reproduced findings.

## Access and provenance

Main HTML results, Table 1, figure captions and discussion were read. The six-page PDF was downloaded locally; page 5's methods and Figure 4 were rendered and visually checked. PDF extraction emitted object-pointer warnings, so the displayed page was used to check the sampling counts and geographic-model wording. Figure 4 is a source sampling map; it has not been georeferenced or digitized into specimen coordinates.

The supplementary PDF link returned HTML rather than PDF bytes, and a later main-page request also returned a browser-check page. No supplementary specimen table, laboratory documentation or sequence alignment was audited. The public repository links the paper; it does not redistribute the article. A local retrieval hash is in S23. The mirror is the same study, not corroboration.

## Consequences for the catastrophe test

Three quantities must not be substituted for one another:

| Quantity | What a test would need | Boundary in this audit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Census abundance | Counts or a defensible observation model | A genetic reconstruction cannot simply be relabeled as a headcount |
| Regional biological history | Dated, identified specimens and spatial sampling coverage | A pooled curve cannot establish simultaneous change at every locality |
| Depositional or geographic event | Independently dated sediments, deformation, transport and terrain | Genetic ancestry does not measure a mud layer, land uplift or a former shoreline |

Inference over named regions cannot establish an exact physical migration corridor. A model that permits transitions between states needs separate geographical evidence before those transitions can be drawn as routes. Likewise, an inferred ancestral state among sampled regions does not demonstrate the absence of an unsampled source population.

The title's causal language must be tested against the actual comparisons. Failure to detect a genetic change around a nominated arrival time is not proof that people had no effect anywhere, at any scale. Equally, an unproven human effect is not positive evidence of climate forcing or a catastrophe. Detection power, sampling, timing uncertainty and competing mechanisms matter for each explanation.

The reported sample accounting should be resolved before reconstructing inclusion/exclusion decisions. The 162-versus-149 difference is numerically consistent with thirteen additional sequences, but arithmetic alone does not verify the sample-ID correspondence. The separate two-specimen discrepancy is preserved without inventing missing rows or accusing the authors of fabrication.

No chronology transformation or common mortality/deposition event was fitted here. This study provides a route to testing muskox population history, while leaving the proposed global mechanism unestablished. Its 2010 interpretation is not presented as a review of current consensus.

## Next discriminating work

Retrieve the supplement and deposited sequences; join specimen IDs, dates, locality precision, taxonomic assignment and sequence eligibility. Reconcile counts without dropping unmatched records. Keep radiocarbon bounds, calibrated ages and model-inferred times separate.

Reproduce the reported analysis only after recovering inputs and settings. Test sensitivity to spatial pooling, chronology, inclusion criteria and unsampled populations. Compare any inferred changes with independently dated environmental sequences at matching locations; do not treat visual curve resemblance as a causal test.
