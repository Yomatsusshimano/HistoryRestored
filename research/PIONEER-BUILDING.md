# Pioneer Building construction evidence

Research draft, 2026-10-08. This is a building-specific follow-up to C001 and S91's rebuilding list. It concerns the Pioneer Building at First/James, **not the Grand Central Block** studied elsewhere in C001. No independent review or site inspection has occurred.

## Project identification

S91 page 35 lists H. L. Yesler's Pioneer building at James/Front with a reported $250,000 project figure. The list combines work underway and projected work. It is not an invoice or completion certificate. The building name and corner provide the link to the following archival records; a parcel/title-chain audit remains outstanding.

## Photograph inspected

[MOHAI photograph 2011.26.7.14 (S92)](https://digitalcollections.lib.washington.edu/digital/collection/imlsmohai/id/8524), from the David Thomas Denny II Family Papers, was inspected through UW's displayed 700 × 521 pixel image. Visible features include a substantial multi-story facade at right, exterior platforms and braces, a ladder, and piles of boards and other material beside the roadway. These observations are compatible with active building work. They do not reveal every stage of the structure's construction.

The catalog identifies the right-hand building as the Pioneer Building, describes it as nearly completed, and dates the photograph **circa 1890**. The photographer is unattributed; John P. Soule is suggested, not established. The approximate date is catalog information, not a legible exposure date verified on the original print. Scaffolding alone cannot distinguish initial construction from repair; the catalog's construction identification must remain separate from what the pixels show.

This is positive evidence of work at the identified building, subject to attribution and dating. It cannot determine total labor, material supply, exact completion, or whether any earlier fabric was retained. A Commons copy was a discovery route, not a second independent photograph.

## Working section now inspected

[UW ARC0508 (S93)](https://digitalcollections.lib.washington.edu/digital/collection/ac/id/1348/) catalogs an ink-on-drafting-cloth working section from the Elmer Fisher Collection, assigned to 1889–1890. Its notes identify Section A.B., sheet 4, for H. L. Yesler. The catalog's later building history says completion in 1892. The initial image request failed; a subsequent visit through the collection viewer's own IIIF link succeeded. The full section and enlarged lower portion were visually inspected. The title names the Pioneer Building, H. L. Yesler and Fisher; the lower section depicts stairs and different floor levels. No legible calendar date or street-grade datum was established in the inspected portions. Dimension numerals were not transcribed because fractional marks and reference surfaces require closer checking. The lowest depicted space must not automatically be called a formerly buried street floor.

## First-floor plan and exterior lighting label

[UW ARC0503 (S94)](https://digitalcollections.lib.washington.edu/digital/collection/ac/id/1334/) is cataloged as a first-floor working plan for the same client and corner, likewise assigned to 1889–1890. The full image and enlarged entrance area were inspected. The drawing includes bank/vault labels and an entrance tile design naming the Pioneer Building. Immediately outside that entrance, rectangles are labeled **“Hyatt Lights”**; the entrance steps carry an **“up”** annotation.

This is a specific design annotation to compare with specifications and fabric. It is not a measured sediment boundary, installation receipt or as-built survey. Identifying its lighting construction and the space served requires companion details. The two drawings share an architectural collection and must not be counted as independent absolute dates. Their design dates remain catalog assignments; no independently dated revision sequence has been recovered.

The next discriminating evidence is a dated sequence: original section/revision markings, lighting specifications and installation records, early construction photographs, contemporary progress reports, and occupancy or completion records. Compare those records by building and construction phase. The present photograph and drawings do not establish an inherited structure, a complete construction schedule, or a catastrophic burial date.
