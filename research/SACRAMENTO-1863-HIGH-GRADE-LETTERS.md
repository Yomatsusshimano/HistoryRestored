Search covered the Sacramento Daily Union, 13-18 February1863, exact phrase “high grade”. It returned six indexed items; this is not a complete issue-by-issue census. Source scans and OCR remain distinct.

[S455: “High Grade,” 18 February1863](https://cdnc.ucr.edu/?a=d&d=SDU18630218.2.8), archive metadata volume24/number3716, viewer page2. The article heading, opening, advocacy and closing signature were visually inspected in the original-image viewer at increased magnification; selected archive OCR was also read. The letter, signed **A Taxpayer**, disputes a claim attributed to “A. C.” that taxpayers do not favor raising the streets. It names claimed supporters and argues for raising and grading. This is an anonymous participant's advocacy, not a vote tally, contractor account, measured deposit or proof of execution. The opponents' original Bee letter remains unacquired.

The opening and conclusion match the passage attributed to this letter in S453 PDF12 footnote36. The **18 February date is therefore supported for that quoted passage**. S453's bibliography gives13February; its discrepancy remains preserved. Neither the matching quotation nor an OCR search proves no related article existed on13February. The issue's dated masthead was not independently read; the date/number are repository metadata associated with the displayed scan.

The original letter image was not visually reviewed, so access is FULL_TEXT_PORTION, not SCAN_INSPECTED.

The S456 OCR article container continues into separately headed flood and unrelated news. Those passages must not be attributed to Citizen. ## Consequences and remaining test

These letters establish earlier published discussion of the grade project at the stated access limits. They provide more specific documentary controls than present-day explanations alone. **Support for a proposed project is not evidence that a given street was already filled in February1863.** Anonymous signatures do not identify builders or authenticate the named supporters' own statements. Both letters appeared in one newspaper and are not independent geological observations.

The next necessary work is original ordinances and execution records tied to a named parcel: dates, grade surveys, retaining-wall construction, fill source/quantity, and logged strata. Only that association can distinguish natural flood deposits from later engineered fill at the Reddit photograph's site. The photograph itself remains unmatched. Regional floods and later construction may coexist; neither supplies a worldwide event date or a rewriting mechanism.
