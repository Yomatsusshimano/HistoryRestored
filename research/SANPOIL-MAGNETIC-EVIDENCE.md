# Sanpoil magnetic evidence: observation versus inherited chronology

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C008; partial source access, no independently reproduced magnetic analysis.

[Steele (1991), S164](https://doi.org/10.1016/0033-5894(91)90067-F) reports, in the publisher abstract, different remanent declinations between beds and a common west-to-east trend at three Sanpoil sections. Inclinations were erratic, attributed to recording effects or compaction. The author interprets the declination changes as consistent with modern secular-variation rates and separated floods. The full methods, numerical directions and statistical tests have not been inspected here.

This is adverse evidence for treating the entire sequence as one rapid deposit. It is a different observable from couplet counts. However, the independence of its inferred duration remains to be checked: recover exactly how bed ages entered the rate calculation, how samples were oriented and demagnetized, and how sediment deformation or delayed magnetic recording was tested. Do not invent angular differences, confidence levels or a minimum duration from the abstract.

[Hanson and colleagues, S165](https://opus.uleth.ca/bitstream/handle/10133/3701/Hanson_Enkin_Barendregt_Clague_QR%202015.pdf), in the retrieved 2014 article-in-press version of the study subsequently cited as 2015, examine sediment provenance and deposition using rock magnetic properties. PDF p.3 says 46 flood units were accessible but varves could not consistently be identified. Its 700–2,600-year duration cites Atwater (1986) and Hanson (2013). The detailed magnetic sampling covers 49 samples through just over two lower Manila Creek units, with another 288 samples across the lower 25 units for remanence and susceptibility only. Those are distinct sampling scopes, not 46 independently dated floods.

The later paper's introduction also cites Steele and earlier magnetic work for separated-flood timing. Repetition of that conclusion is not another independent dating measurement. Its new magnetic-property data may test sediment source and depositional changes; those functions must remain separate from inherited chronological assignments. Only selected text portions were inspected, and a PDF screenshot request timed out. Figures and sample tables are not claimed as visually audited.

The current assessment therefore includes both the reported multi-section magnetic trend and the incomplete chronology audit. Uncertain annuality does not erase the magnetic evidence; a magnetic interpretation does not automatically validate every annual layer or date each flood. Next recover Steele's numerical dataset and methods, and test the age dependence of the secular-variation comparison before assigning a quantitative interval.
