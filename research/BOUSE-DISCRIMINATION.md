# What distinguishes the Bouse explanations

2026-10-08. Retrospective synthesis of S183-S187. No independent review or fitted reconstruction.

The recovered measurements support a change in the sampled environment, but do not uniquely identify its cause. The proposed lake spillover and marine-to-lake interpretations overlap in predicting environmental change. A historical Gulf-to-Pacific passage adds separate requirements that neither local interpretation establishes.

## State each proposition separately

These operational versions are introduced for comparison; they are not verbatim claims attributed to Nathan.

- **H2-L:** A stratified lake at Hart Mine Wash became more mixed during outlet change or spillover.
- **H2-M:** Marine influence reached the southern Bouse basin, followed by a change toward lake conditions.
- **H2-T:** The particular Bouse horizons under discussion formed during the historical island-map interval.
- **H2-G:** A contemporaneous water route connected the Gulf to the Pacific north of the depicted California island.

H2-L and H2-M are not exhaustive or necessarily mutually exclusive over the formation's entire history. A basin can experience marine influence at one stage and a lake at another. To compare them as alternatives, specify the same beds, locality and interval. H2-T and H2-G cannot be inferred simply by selecting one environmental explanation.

## Physical requirements before fitting an outlet-change model

A lake-spillover explanation requires an initial basin and water level, an outlet sill, a release hydrograph, inflows and losses, and an erosion/deposition history. An environmental transition alone does not measure those inputs. A model must account for why sediment, fauna and isotopes change together, and why the later condition persists over the recorded section.

A marine-influence explanation requires a route to the sea, relative sea level and basin elevation through the relevant interval, and a water-exchange history consistent with the ecology and sediment. A transient flood flushing an estuary and permanent loss of marine connection are different models. They should not share an assumed prediction of immediate return to marine conditions without specifying the boundary conditions that cause it.

For both versions, compare the same observations at their actual sampling resolution. If alteration, reworking, unpreserved intervals or changing ecological behavior are invoked, require evidence and quantify their effect. Do not count such possibilities as either established explanations or automatic disproofs.

The salt and water budgets must use the same control volume. Record initial and incoming water composition, basin storage, inflow/outflow, evaporation, and mixing assumptions before treating an isotope shift as a salinity change. Oxygen isotope values alone do not supply all these quantities. No measured basin volume, outlet hydrograph or salt budget has been reproduced in this archive.

## Conditions for a historical-map test

First select a particular map and trace the channel it actually depicts, with control-point and line-position uncertainty. Keep the route fixed while collecting its discovery evidence. Then specify a date window, deformation history and expected depositional traces. Georeferencing uncertainty must not be silently absorbed into unlimited shoreline movement.

Only observations withheld from that model's construction can test a new prediction. Existing Bouse data and maps are already inspected discovery material. Freeze the route, date/deformation model, sampling coverage and rejection conditions publicly before examining new target observations. A missing marine bed is informative only where the model predicts preservation and the sampling could detect it.

Present status: H2-L and H2-M remain incompletely discriminated here. H2-T lacks an established chronological association. H2-G lacks a specified and verified continuous route. These are different evidential gaps, not four confirmations or four global refutations. None establishes historical fabrication.

## Chronology dependencies recovered

The [chronology audit](BOUSE-CHRONOLOGY.md) now separates underlying-rock limits, fossil-range dating and the Bouse-Lawlor ash correlation, using S194/S195. A secure older limit alone cannot exclude historical deposition; a primary correlated ash provides a different test. Exact stratigraphic transfer and raw dating analyses remain pending. Environmental disagreement does not erase these distinct chronological constraints.
