# Chicago street grades: proposed work versus completed construction

2026-10-09. C025 opens a second-city documentary comparison for the buried-street investigation. No Chicago site, sediment, historical grade benchmark or original completed street-work record has been inspected. A1859 transcription now adds a source-reported completed river project, kept separate from street raising. This draft tests an engineering explanation without treating planned work as demonstrated completion.

## What was read

S234 is the Chicago Public Library's [transcription of Mayor Levi Day Boone's1855 inaugural address](https://www.chipublib.org/mayor-levi-day-boone-inaugural-address-1855/). The library dates the inauguration March13 and cites the Daily Democratic Press, March14,1855, p.[2]. Its street/drainage paragraphs propose high grading, timber stringers at least six inches thick, lake-shore sand/gravel between them, and oak planking at least five inches thick. Sewers are to precede surfacing. These are the speaker's recommendations; no particular block, historical elevation or executed quantity follows. The newspaper scan was not inspected.

S235 is the Illinois State Archives [Document32 catalog/explanation](https://www.ilsos.gov/departments/archives/teaching-packages/early-chicago/doc32.html), titled Estimate for Street Construction and dated September14,1855. The modern explanatory note links grade changes to drainage and describes adjoining owners paying for improvements. Neither the estimate scan nor its PDF transcription was retrieved, so no quantities, costs, contractor or street location are transcribed here.

S236 is [Document35](https://www.ilsos.gov/departments/archives/teaching-packages/early-chicago/doc35.html), an Ontario Street sketch catalog-dated December16,1856. The archive's explanation attributes the proposal to William B. Ogden, describes raising streets/sidewalks to address shifting sand, and says the council approved the plans. These are catalog/explanatory assertions, not this investigation's reading of the drawing or council action.

## What these records discriminate

The mayoral transcription supplies a specific proposed way to construct an elevated street from placed materials. Sand and gravel beneath a roadway are consequently compatible with an engineered deposit. That compatibility is not proof that a particular below-grade facade resulted from these works. Lake-shore sediment as a proposed fill source also cannot be converted into evidence of marine flooding.

The sources describe differing purposes: drainage/sewer planning and a location-specific sand problem. They should not be merged into one uniform citywide depositional event, height or date. Administrative proposal, approval, construction, later alteration and modern surviving fabric each require their own evidence.

A blanket claim that raised grades could only result from a natural catastrophe faces a documented engineering alternative. A claim that every Chicago lower level is explained by these particular projects remains untested. No natural flood, earlier burial, construction extent or chronology break is established by this draft.
