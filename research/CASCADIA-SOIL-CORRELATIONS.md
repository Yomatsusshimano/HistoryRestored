## A correlation diagram is not one continuous surveyed section

Figure32, pp.84-85, compares Willapa Bay, Grays Harbor, Copalis River and Puget Sound. Its legend calls the columns generalized from outcrop drawings. Vertical positions are typical depths below modern marsh/swamp, not one shared surveyed elevation datum. Dashed correlations pass through sections where a soil is unrecognized; question marks identify speculative links. The Copalis W connection is queried.

The legend distinguishes mud, probable tsunami deposits, vented sand, gradual-submergence peat, growth-position plants and microscopic fossils. It also separates closely limiting, minimum, maximum and ambiguous sample-age relations. Its likely-age bands explicitly allow an earthquake or earthquake series. Thus neither a drawn connecting line nor overlapping broad age bounds establishes a single synchronous event.

The legend states a greater-than35m source depth for vented sand by citation to the1992 article. That is inherited interpretation, not a measured pressure head or a source depth independently verified here. The surrounding discussion, pp.83-85, describes faulting/folding of an aquifer as an interpretation and allows upper-plate movement to accompany, precede or follow plate-boundary activity. This retains uncertainty rather than proving a unique trigger.

The diagram does not reconcile the T6/T1/A31 depth-order exceptions or A31/A40 coordinates in the [2022 source audit](CASCADIA-FORMAT-COMPARISON.md). Its generalized Copalis column cannot safely replace those original locality records.

## Environmental change and mud thickness constrain different quantities

Printed p.83 explains why mud above a buried soil may indicate subsidence but need not equal its coseismic amount: later uplift or subsidence changes tidal accommodation and sediment accumulation. Numerical estimates are considered unreliable for the South Bend antiform because most soils there had not been studied for microfossils.

At Redtail and Oyster, the authors infer roughly metre-scale subsidence for several horizons from environmental differences, especially plant and diatom assemblages. These are paleoenvironmental estimates tied to modern tidal zonation, not direct historical survey measurements. Soil W may instead record only about0.5m at Oyster. Do not transfer one estimate to every locality or set displacement equal to deposit thickness.

Figure31, printed p.78, separately labels sandy dredge spoil and fine sand probably representing dredge spoil inset against the Airport locality bank. Older soils Y/W/U are depicted below. This supplies a local engineered-fill interpretation alongside the buried-soil sequence; it does not establish the origin of all deposits or buried urban features. The section has10x vertical exaggeration and a local horizontal datum. Original dredging contracts and physical sediment reanalysis have not been recovered.

## Keep the clocks and dated events separate

Printed p.24 explicitly defines the report's conventions:

| Quantity | Reference and role | Audit consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional radiocarbon ages in tables/outcrop drawings |14C years before1950 |Not calendar ages; subtraction from1950 is not calibration |
| Text and interpretive diagrams |Calendar years before2000 |Subtract from2000 for a CE label within the relevant CE range |
| Standard calibrated BP |Calendar years before1950 |A different reference epoch from the report's calendar years ago |
| Figure32 thick/thin ranges |Reported lab errors versus error multipliers: QL1.6; other labs assumed2.0 |Ranges have different assumptions; wider prose ranges may be rounded outward to centuries |
| Dated wood, peat or rhizomes |Material formation before or after an inferred event |Retain maximum/minimum and geological offsets; sample age is not automatically event age |

The report used1993 calibration data and CALIB3.0.3. No recalibration has been executed here. A simple epoch check is exact arithmetic: CE1700 equals300 calendar years before2000 and250 calendar years before1950. Their50-year numeric difference is not evidence of a chronology break. Conventional radiocarbon ages must not be inserted into this arithmetic as if calibrated.

Source-inferred offsets can be negligible, corrected by ring counts, or unknown by decades/centuries. Do not average all date bars as direct observations of the same earthquake. A future comparison needs lab/sample identities, material context, calibration version, uncertainty definitions and correlation assumptions.

## Soil count and preservation do not supply an elapsed-time formula

Printed p.95 considers soils destroyed by decomposition and explains a conditional expectation of preserved traces for widespread subsidence exceeding0.5m, provided many decades for soil formation. This is a source inference with preservation conditions, not demonstrated completeness of the event record.

The same page cites Swan's1857 account and1870s Coast Survey maps to argue that150years is ample for restoration of high marsh from muddy tidal flats after the latest inferred event. Despite the subsection's minimum-time heading, the example does not establish150years as a lower bound. Multiplying soil count by150 would manufacture a duration. The original Swan passage and map sheets have not been inspected here; their evidence remains mediated by this report.

The authors also connect shallow burial and decomposition to loss of soil W, contrasting better preservation of deeper soil N. Those interpretations constrain any model that assumes identical preservation everywhere, but do not constitute a new measured decay experiment. A missing soil is not automatically a missing earthquake, and multiple soil names alone do not demonstrate the number or timing of independent hydrographs.

## Consequences for the full investigation

The report supports a regional sequence interpretation through multiple kinds of field evidence while retaining uncertain correlations, timing and preservation. A rapid-deposition model must explain the growth-position plants, environmental changes, intrusion geometry and contact relationships together. A conventional earthquake account must likewise justify section correlation, displacement and age association rather than relying on diagram labels alone.

Next: obtain original Copalis drawings and locality notes; resolve source depth/coordinate exceptions; build sample-specific limiting-age comparisons; inspect primary historical marsh records before estimating formation rates. The1997 report, its cited studies and the2022 compilation share observations and cannot count as independent confirmations. No global footprint, chronology transformation, hydraulic fit, prospective success or independent review is established. All twenty original objectives remain in scope.
