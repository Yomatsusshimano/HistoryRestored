## What this resolves spatially

Both maps depict a compact quadrangular Archives complex with four internal open courts beside the river end of the Champ-de-Mars, across its bordering avenue rather than centrally within the exercise ground. Both depict a corresponding barracks complex on the opposite flank. Nearby street and river labels establish the relative setting of the drawings. This is a more concrete spatial lead than the broad Concorde–Iéna frontage in the [printed program](ARCHIVES-PROGRAM-1844.md).

The placement is consistent in broad terms with [Bordier's 1855 description](ARCHIVES-BORDIER-1855.md) of the intended palace at the Orsay/Bourdonnais corner. The maps spell the avenue **Bourdonnaye**; preserve that wording rather than silently making the texts identical. Neither inspection establishes original cadastral boundaries, modern coordinates, exact building dimensions, or overlap with any Eiffel foundation. The depicted courtyard scheme also does not authenticate either map as a copy of the surviving Cellerier drawing or the [undated terrain plan](ARCHIVES-PALAIS-SITE-PLAN.md).

## Depiction is not completion

The same selected map regions visibly contain a right-bank palace labeled **projeté** and a large left-bank university scheme. The Archives symbols are rendered as shaded building footprints, but that graphic treatment alone cannot classify them as completed structures. S372's title claims to show changes made up to its date according to governmental orders; it is not an acceptance report for each symbol. These maps contain planning information alongside urban geography.

The Archives depiction therefore supports investigation of an intended site and design. It does not prove four completed quarters in 1815, independently verify Bordier's two-metre height, or establish an inherited building. No building height, foundation depth, sediment thickness or execution date was measured from the image.

In the selected Champ-de-Mars regions the river frontage is drawn as a continuous mainland edge rather than a separate island channel. That is a cartographic observation only: without knowing survey dates, revisions and project conventions, it cannot certify the year or mechanism of channel filling. The earlier [island history](CYGNES-PARTIAL-FILL-ARCHIVAL-LEADS.md) still requires original works records and physical stratigraphy.

## Which map did Bordier mean?

Both inspected maps print the year 1815, Jean's name and the street address compatible with his footnote. His abbreviated reference does not uniquely identify either title or surviving copy. The existence of two matching candidates prevents an automatic exact-edition join.

Their shared publisher and closely corresponding local schemes also prevent counting them as two independent surveys. Exact plate ancestry, dates of revision, mapmakers, survey methods and copying direction remain unverified. The labels establish publication-year claims, not the observation date of every depicted feature. S373's legible 200-toise scale has not been used to calculate an executed footprint; S372's inspected scale bar lacks legible units in the delivered detail.

Next identify the map edition Bordier consulted and retrieve original surveyed parcel plans and palace works accounts. Register stable control points with uncertainty before comparing the intended footprint to tower piers and earlier excavation reports. These maps narrow a research target; they establish neither a common catastrophe nor a fabrication mechanism. All twenty original objectives remain active.
