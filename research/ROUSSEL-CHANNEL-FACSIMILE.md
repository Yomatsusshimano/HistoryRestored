# Roussel map lead: depicted island and facsimile date limits

Research draft, 2026-10-11. S359; C006. No independent review.

Paris Musées / Musée Carnavalet supplies two objects titled *Paris en 1731, Fac-similé du plan de Roussel*, [plate XXVI](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/paris-en-1731-fac-simile-du-plan-de-roussel-pl-xxvi) and [plate XXVI bis](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/paris-en-1731-fac-simile-du-plan-de-roussel-pl-xxvi-bis). The institutional manifests identify these as heliogravure facsimiles in *Atlas des anciens plans de Paris*, attribute them to Paul Dujardin, Charles Chardon and Imprimerie Nationale, and date the objects to the **nineteenth century**. Full delivered images of both sheets and a western island/channel crop were visually inspected. They are original scans of reproduction objects, not inspected eighteenth-century impressions. [Acquisition and interpretation ledger](../data/roussel-channel-facsimile.json).

## The feature depicted

The western sheet (XXVI bis) shows an elongated island in the Seine near Chaillot, separated from the mainland on the opposite side by a narrow channel. Its label associates it with Maquerelle and the Cygnes name. The full archaic spelling and small bridge labels have not been accepted as exact diplomatic transcriptions. This is a visible cartographic feature, not merely a modern catalog's assertion about a former channel.

The feature corresponds qualitatively to the former island/channel invoked in Eiffel's [foundation account](EIFFEL-FOUNDATION-CONTEXT.md). That account also reports buried wood, pottery, bones and masonry at the river-side piers. The map does not supply object depths, dates or specimen associations. The island's outline and the foundation sections have not been georeferenced together, so no particular shaft or masonry block is placed inside the historical channel here.

## Three dates that must stay separate

1. Eiffel's retrospective account calls the cited Roussel plan **1730**.
2. The inspected reproduction objects are titled **Paris en 1731**.
3. The objects themselves are catalogued as **nineteenth-century facsimiles**, with no exact production date authenticated in this inspection.

Those differences are not automatically a historical chronology break. Survey, publication, edition, represented terrain and reproduction dates can differ. The inspected catalog does not establish which edition Eiffel consulted or whether every depicted detail belongs to one survey date. No exact 1880 reproduction date is adopted from secondary search results. The depicted title year cannot independently date either the physical reproduction or the sediment under the tower.

A direct request for the candidate Gallica manifest btv1b7711270h returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**. No access restriction was bypassed. The candidate eighteenth-century impression remains uninspected; the accessible facsimile is not silently substituted as the exact original cited by Eiffel. Related catalog searches identify multiple Roussel editions, but none was authenticated through an original impression here.

## Resolution and independence

The museum-delivered images are actually **1500 pixels wide**, despite their URLs containing a `styles/4k` path. The western delivery is 1500 × 2160 pixels. Cropping does not add original detail. Small lettering, quantitative channel width and scan-to-ground calibration remain unverified. Institutional images are linked with hashes rather than redistributed; no license was inferred from the manifests' missing license fields.

The two sheets are companion parts of one facsimile work, not two independent historical witnesses. The reproduced map is additional cartographic content, but its exact copying history and fidelity to an early impression remain untested. Agreement with Eiffel's explanation supplies a retrieval lead and qualitative geographic support; it does not establish the timing, rate or mechanism of channel infilling.

Next inspect an authenticated early Roussel impression and the specifically cited Berniquet/Verniquet map edition, then seek original works records for the channel filling. Register stable mapped controls before placing the foundation sections. No common catastrophic burial horizon, rapid worldwide upheaval or historical fabrication follows from these sheets.
