# Gobien's 1705 letter: the crossing report predates the later editions

2026-10-08. C005, S191. Selected original page images inspected; no independent review.

## Object and scope

*Lettres edifiantes et curieuses*, V. Recueil, Paris, Nicolas Le Clerc, printed 1705, Google Books Fsqj6xg8XxgC. Title PP1 confirms volume/imprint. The preliminary epistle is addressed to the Jesuits of France (PP3) and signed Charles Le Gobien (PP30). Inspected core route passage PP17-20, plus opening PP3-4 and signature; other viewed pages are not a complete-letter audit. These leaves have no ordinary printed page numbers, so Google page IDs are retained as digital locators, not invented pagination.

This is another digitized copy of the volume already represented by S38, but a newly inspected section. It is not another field witness. The author's printed signature identifies the attribution, not an independently authenticated autograph.

## Original words and working reading

PP18 reports a 1699 arrival at the banks of Rio Azul. PP19 describes Azul as receiving Hila's waters before flowing into Colorado, then says (long s normalized, line breaks joined):

> Il passa le Rio Azul, & se trouva en mil sept cens proche du Colorado, & l'ayant traversé, il fut bien surpris en mil sept cens un, de se voir dans la Californie

Working translation: He crossed the Rio Azul and in 1700 found himself near the Colorado; having crossed it, he was surprised in 1701 to find himself in California.

**Colorado is the stronger contextual reading of “it”**: it is the immediately preceding named river, and PP19-20 explains California's separation from New Mexico by that river's waters. The verb still uses a pronoun; this is a contextual translation, not an additional explicitly named object in the French. The passage does not supply a day or month, raft, measured coordinates or direct diary entry.

The remaining passage says Kino learned that the Colorado entered the sea roughly thirty or forty leagues away after forming an extended bay. That is reported information, not a surveyed distance or Kino personally reaching the stated outlet. Retain leagues as written; no unverified unit conversion.

## Three-stage comparison

| Element | Gobien 1705, S191 | Buache, S190 p.71 | Article 1776, S189 p.133 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explicitly crossed river | Rio Azul | Rio Hila | Denies Hila crossing |
| Near Colorado | 1700 | 1700 | Not the selected sentence's claim |
| Following crossing | Pronoun; Colorado strongly indicated by context | Similar pronoun retained | Denies Colorado crossing |
| Arrival in California | 1701 | 1701 | Not the selected sentence's claim |
| Nature of account | Editorial epistle reporting exploration | Summary explicitly attributed to Gobien | Geographical argument drawing on earlier reports |

Buache changes the explicit river name from Azul to Hila in the selected summary. Gobien's preceding drainage sentence distinguishes the two names; do not silently equate them or assign modern rivers without further examination. The later categorical denial conflicts with this published affirmative tradition. Exact editorial pathways and reasons remain unestablished: name normalization, compression, mistake, or other interpretation requires evidence.

The reported Colorado crossing therefore was **already in a printed 1705 account**, on the contextual reading above; it is not solely a claim appearing in the twentieth-century Kino editions inspected as S39/S40. Those later editions provide the more specific November 21, 1701 account. Agreement in year does not authenticate their manuscript or make these accounts independent, since the epistle may depend on Kino-derived information.

## A named map intermediary

PP20 attributes the map to Kino and says Father Barthelemy Alcazar, teaching mathematics at the Imperial College in Madrid, communicated it to the editor along with a map of the New Philippines. This is a concrete reported transmission link worth following. It does not establish custody of the physical manuscript or prove which engraving descended directly from it. Recover the correspondence or accession history before treating that custody chain as verified.

## Implications and next checks

The result strengthens the documentary basis for an early crossing claim and exposes a specific change of river name in a later summary. It does not establish deliberate suppression, a modern-coordinate route, a through-going Pacific channel, or catastrophic land change. Next compare the map's river labels and original itinerary/manuscript, then investigate Alcazar's communication. No need to keep Gobien's preliminary letter listed as unrecovered.
