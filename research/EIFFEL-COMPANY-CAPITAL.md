# Company capital: operating rights versus cash subscriptions

Research draft, 2026-10-11. Expanded S17 and new S350; case C006. No independent review.

The inspected sources distinguish **5,100,000 francs of nominal share capital** from **100,000 francs of cash-subscription shares**. Most initial shares were reported as consideration for Eiffel's contribution of operating rights. Nominal capital therefore cannot be treated as cash collected from subscribers for construction. Later sales of those shares or other financing could have produced cash; those transactions remain uninspected.

## Original pages inspected

Eiffel's 1900 participant account, chapter V, company formation section, is on the unnumbered opening page immediately before printed p227: [canvas 8976173](https://www.e-rara.ch/i3f/v20/8976173/full/2000,/0/default.jpg). The manifest labels this canvas **[250]**, which is not the printed chapter pagination. [Printed p227, canvas 8976174](https://www.e-rara.ch/i3f/v20/8976174/full/2000,/0/default.jpg) continues the formation account and begins operating receipts. Both original images were visually inspected. This is a later participant narrative quoting selected statutes, not the original notarized act.

Eiffel describes a late-construction operating company, a 6,500,000-franc valuation, anticipated 1,500,000-franc State contribution and 5,000,000-franc complement. The reproduced provisions then set share capital at 5,100,000 francs. These numbers have distinct roles. His wording anticipates the subsidy and does not supply its payment receipts. The account names Banque franco-egyptienne, Societe generale and Credit industriel et commercial; their actual financing transactions are not reproduced here.

The [DFIH 1908 yearbook entry](https://dfih.fr/issuers/2789/yearbook-ocr?source=annuaire_cac_1908) points to [original p2001](https://images.eurhisfirm.eu/Annuaires/CAC/1908/1908_T_2001.jpg). The next numbered image, [p2002](https://images.eurhisfirm.eu/Annuaires/CAC/1908/1908_T_2002.jpg), visibly continues the tower entry. Both were acquired by normal HTTPS and inspected. Only these selected pages were checked; the volume title and later continuation were not inspected. The 1908 edition identification comes from the hosting record and image path.

## Capital arithmetic and its limits

| Yearbook report, p2002 | Calculation in francs | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,200 shares at 500 | 5,100,000 | Nominal share capital |
| 10,000 fully paid shares allocated to Eiffel for contributed operating rights | 5,000,000 | Consideration for rights; not a cash subscription |
| 200 other shares subscribed at 500 | 100,000 | Reported cash-subscription component |
| Initial quarter paid, 125 per share | 25,000 | Reported initial payment component |
| Remaining 375 per share | 75,000 | Reportedly paid May 9-28, 1889 |

The components reconcile arithmetically. No subscription certificates, banking receipts or complete subscriber list were acquired. The [SAEF image excerpt](EIFFEL-SAEF-FINANCING.md) is not sufficiently complete or legible to authenticate these totals or match all subscribers.

The yearbook also reports five repayments of 100 francs per share during 1889. That supplies dated transaction targets, not verified profit or repayment receipts. It is a later compilation that may depend on issuer disclosures; agreement with Eiffel's narrative is not independent accounting authentication.

## Dates and ownership

Both sources give December 31, 1888 for the initiating act/foundation. They disagree on definitive constitution: **March 4, 1889** in Eiffel's printed p227, **March 2, 1889** in the yearbook p2001. The latter specifies notary Dufour in Paris. Neither date is silently corrected; the original act and constituent assembly records can distinguish a printing error from separate procedural dates.

Yearbook p2002 states City ownership after the exhibition, with operating rights through December 31, 1909. This agrees with the ownership/enjoyment distinction in [article 11 of the original agreement](EIFFEL-CONVENTION-1887.md), and differs from the modern SAEF summary. The yearbook also reports a December 1, 1906 extension; the original amendment is unacquired, so its terms are not independently authenticated here.

## Implication and next test

This narrows financing arguments that equate corporate share capital with construction cash. The reported arrangement is consistent with transferring a concession into an operating company near completion. It does not establish the full financing chain, actual subsidy payment, profitability, material manufacture or inheritance of the tower.
