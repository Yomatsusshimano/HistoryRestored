# A numbered-cave conflict in the bat locality records

2026-10-09. C011/S324; sourced draft without independent review.

The original 2017 study by J. Angel Soto-Centeno, Nancy B. Simmons and David W. Steadman provides a locality spreadsheet and a specimen-list supplement. [Publisher article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0178066). The original spreadsheet was read without editing; main PDF pages6/10 and specimen-list PDF page1 were visually inspected. Other PDF pages were not visually audited.

## Why the numbered cave matters

S1 Table, Sheet1 row31, labels18.209992N,−74.010012W as **Trou Jeremie #1**, with elevation236m. Both coordinates round to the2015paper's18.20999N,−74.01001W. The [executed comparison](../analysis/haiti_bat_locality_crosswalk.py) scans all36locality rows and finds exactly one such match. [Raw cells and results](../data/haiti-bat2017-locality-crosswalk.json) retain every row, including a separate locality west-southwest of the town of Jérémie and Trou Jean Paul.

This is a stronger documentary warning than a generic coordinate discrepancy. S318 identifies the sloth cave as Jérémie#5, while S319's bat locality omits the number. The2017table assigns the matching point to#1. It does not establish which cave actually supplied Beta345518, identify the stage of any error, or license a correction to either source. The236m value is a GoogleEarth estimate under the supplement's stated convention for new sites, not an independently surveyed cave elevation.

The previously published [bat–sloth interval arithmetic](JEREMIE-BAT-COMPARISON.md) remains reproducible, but cannot establish an age mixture within one cave. The original accession/field-label chain must resolve cave identity before a shared-deposit interpretation is attempted. Neither coordinate agreement nor contrasting labels demonstrate geographic upheaval.

## The offered accession list belongs to another cave

S2 Table page1 explicitly covers Trouing Jean Paul. Its Macrotus waterhousii accessions are UF282171 andUF307265. Neither can be assigned to Beta345518 just because the taxon agrees.

Main PDF page6 explains that the six Table3assays dated individual **woodcock bones**, with bat association through collection-grid locations. The bat abbreviations beside those assays are associated taxa, not the dated materials. This distinction prevents importing those ages as six directly dated bats. No dates, specimen identities, or sediment contexts are transferred to Jérémie here.
