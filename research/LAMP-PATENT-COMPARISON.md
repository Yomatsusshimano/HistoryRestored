# Carbon lamps before and within Edison's patent

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C007 remains without independent review.

The [Woodward scan](../sources/originals/US181613.pdf) records an application filed January 4, 1875 and patent 181,613 granted August 29, 1876. All three pages were visually inspected. The [Edison scan](../sources/originals/US223898.pdf), specification pages 2-3 previously inspected, records patent 223,898 issued January 27, 1880 after the November 4, 1879 filing.

## Compare disclosures, not familiar names

| Question | Woodward, 181,613 | Edison, 223,898 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light-producing element | Shaped carbon with electrodes at its ends | High-resistance carbon filament; several carbonizable materials discussed |
| Vessel atmosphere | Air removed and replaced with rarefied gas that does not chemically combine with hot carbon | Exhausted glass receiver; high vacuum described |
| Claimed combination | Carbon, gas-filled vessel and described electrode connection | Four claims addressing filament, exhausted glass receiver, coil arrangement and contact/carbonizing method |
| Supporting drawing | Carbon/vessel configurations and wiring arrangements | Filament and connection/manufacturing arrangements |
| Independently measured performance in this archive | None | None |

Locators: Woodward PDF pages 1-3; Edison PDF pages 2-3. The table summarizes disclosed features; it is not a legal determination of validity, infringement or priority across all earlier work. Woodward's assertion of effectively unlimited lamps from one generator is not adopted as a measured engineering result.

## Credit and transmission

Woodward's specification heading names him and lists assignment recipients Rupert Mearse Wells, Thomas Richard Fuller, Ernest Heimrod and Charles H. Woodward. That is evidence of the printed attribution and assignment notice, not a complete set of transfer instruments. It does not name Edison as recipient there. This absence cannot exclude a later transfer.

No original Woodward-to-Edison transaction has been inspected. A purchase, if documented, would establish a rights transaction; determining technical dependence would still require dated access and development records. Neither an earlier patent nor a transaction alone demonstrates recovery of ancient technology.

## Present finding and limits

The broad idea of an enclosed incandescent carbon lamp was disclosed before this Edison patent. A narrative crediting Edison with every aspect of electric light is too broad for these records. Conversely, treating the two disclosures as identical erases differences that could matter technically. Neither inventor's patent establishes who first achieved every relevant function or whether the described lamp met a specified commercial performance threshold.

The next audit should compare original assignment instruments, dated notebooks, other inventors' disclosures, fabrication methods and measured lifetime/power data. Indexed Rutgers notebook-document PDFs for October 21-22, 1879 returned 404 when opened; they remain unreviewed leads, not supporting notebook evidence.

## Surviving objects: a test beyond patent wording

The assignment search remains unresolved: Rutgers' digital index presented browser verification, while its indexed March 1879 draft-patent PDF returned 404. Neither failure is evidence that a transfer did not occur.

[Friedel and Israel's 1983 park history study](https://npshistory.com/publications/edis/edisons-elec-light.pdf), with contributions by Bernard S. Finn (S51), offers an artifact-based comparison. Its **Artifacts pp. 1–8** (PDF 291–298) and photographic sheets PDF 299–301 were visually inspected. The authors report examining surviving lamps, finding component combinations that do not form a simple chronological sequence. They propose reuse or departures from the expected development sequence; these remain interpretations.

The [artifact ledger](../data/lamp-artifacts.json) records three Smithsonian identifiers for follow-up. SI 181,799 combines an early clamp with a later base; the authors interpret it as experimental. SI 318,653 is explicitly anomalous in their sequence, but its photograph has not yet been inspected here. SI 318,686 appears under a base type dated 1888 and an attachment type dated 1886: those are component-type labels, not two independent dates of the whole object.

Physical variability offers a test of development accounts, but photographs do not independently date manufacture or establish performance. Museum accession files, restoration histories, original measurements and notebook links remain needed. No Woodward-to-Edison transfer instrument or ancient technology lineage has been established.
