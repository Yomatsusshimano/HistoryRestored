# Ozette: buried Makah houses and repeated occupation

2026-10-08. C018, sourced draft; no field inspection or independent review.

## Physical record

The [National Register nomination packet, S62](https://npshistory.com/publications/olym/nr-ozette-indian-village-as.pdf) contains an earlier 1973 form and a 1980 form revised in 1981. The later continuation's item 7 pages 3–5 (PDF pp. 12–14) were visually inspected. This is an excavation-era synthesis citing reports and personal communications, not the original trench notebook or laboratory certificates.

It describes two slide units separated by cultural deposits: I, recent occupation; II, clay slide; III, cultural deposits; IV, another slide containing clay, displaced midden and crushed structural remains; V, cultural deposits. VI and VII were incompletely defined, with uncertainty over whether VI contained or overlay VII. The 1966 excavation narrative gives about half a metre for an upper slide deposit; this is not a site-wide blanket thickness. House 1 contained displaced boards and poles intermixed with artifacts. House 5 lay beneath House 2, and House 2 had subfloor drainage channels for hillside runoff.

These observations make burial by mud a concrete, testable case. Our inference: the described occupation–slide–occupation sequence is adverse to treating all its layers as one instantaneous burial. The exact duration of intervening occupation and correlation of units between trenches still need original plans, sections and artifact contexts.

## Dates must retain their objects

Three reported determinations are transcribed in [the context ledger](../data/ozette-contexts.json): WSU 1777, 810 ± 70 BP, charcoal at a basal clay/beach-sand interface; WSU 1965, 790 ± 80 BP, lower cultural component; and WSU 1779, 440 ± 90 BP, associated with the Unit IV/V houses. The latter two sample materials are not specified in the inspected passage. Error conventions, pretreatment, calibration and old-wood effects remain unresolved. These are not three direct measurements of one slide's calendar date. No subtraction from 1950 is presented as a calibrated event year.

The record also interprets Unit II as predating 1850 from stratigraphic and artifact context. That is a reported bound, not an exact year or a direct link to Cascadia 1700.

## The community and its account

The [Makah Museum's account, S63](https://makahmuseum.com/about/ozette-archaeological-site/) describes oral history of a major slide, cooperation between the Tribe and excavators, six buried longhouses, and over 55,000 recovered artifacts retained on the reservation. It reports 500 ± 50 BP, without assay identifiers or calibration details on that page. We retain this separately from the nomination's sample-specific figures. Its account identifies a living community and its stewardship; this evidence does not require assigning the houses to an unnamed erased civilization. The page is an institutional account of oral history, not an original recorded interview or permission to reproduce the collection.

## Next discriminating tests

The [NPS recovery-program abstract, S64](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1187/upload/1979a_508-2.pdf), printed p. 60 (PDF p. 66), identifies Mauger's 1979 Phase XI report, edited by Daugherty, as **NTIS PB81 102196**, describing 1977 excavations. This page was visually checked; the full excavation report remains uninspected. It assigns rounded ages of 400 BP to Houses 2 and 5 and 800 BP to a deeper cultural deposit. These are not additional identified assays and cannot yet be cross-matched to the nomination's determinations. It reports beach sand used to construct floors. Our inference: sand in a house context requires discrimination between intentional placement and natural deposition; this abstract alone does not resolve the sedimentary mechanism of individual layers.

The [1976 WSU date list, S65](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200002435) was downloaded and all ten pages searched as extracted text. Neither Ozette nor WSU 1777, 1779 or 1965 was found. This is a bounded search, not evidence that the assays do not exist. The list's methods cannot fill the unknown conventions of absent assays. Search scope and report identifiers are retained in [the acquisition ledger](../data/ozette-report-search.json).

Retrieve WSU laboratory records and final excavation reports; map every dated sample to a section and assess whether it dates wood growth, occupation, redeposition or burial. Test whether Unit II and IV have distinct source areas and intervening stable occupation surfaces. Establish event dates before comparing with Cascadia or other buried sites. The Ozette reference chronology sought for C004 was not recovered during this search, and the village's burial dates cannot substitute for it. No common worldwide event or historical fabrication follows from the present record.
