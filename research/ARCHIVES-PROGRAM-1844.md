# Palace specifications and Soubise works belong to different sites

Research draft, 2026-10-11. Expanded S365; C006. No independent review.

The original [1844 Lazare dictionary](https://archive.org/details/dictionnaireadm00lazagoog) was additionally inspected on printed pages **27–31**, PDF pages **42–46**. The complete *Archives du royaume* entry begins near the bottom of p27 and ends in the left column of p30; p31 is an adjacent alphabetical control. This expansion supplies the entry's source declaration and the context on both sides of the quoted palace program. The complete original decree and the underlying works accounts remain unacquired. [Inspection and comparison ledger](../data/archives-program-1844.json).

## What the quoted program requires

On p29 the dictionary reproduces an extract attributed to a decree dated **21 March 1812**, at the Élysée. Its four articles specify:

| Reported specification | Role of the number or provision | What it does not measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left-bank frontage between Iéna and Concorde | Broad intended location | Exact palace parcel or tower pier coordinates |
| 100,000 cubic metres | Intended accommodation stated in the extract | Excavated earth, sediment deposit or completed building volume |
| Quarters made usable successively; garden space reserved for possible doubling | Staged design and expansion requirement | Proof that any quarter was actually completed |
| Stone and iron, excluding wood from construction | Material specification | Delivered batches, actual fabric or age of an excavated object |
| Plans due before 1 May; 200,000 francs from a decree of the sixth of that month assigned to first works | Reported design deadline and initial allocation | Payment, total cost or certified work quantity |

The extract is visibly abbreviated, including an **etc.** and a printed signature attribution. It is a nineteenth-century quotation, not our inspection of a signed decree, complete legal text or authenticated administrative copy. Its 21 March heading should not silently settle differently dated citations elsewhere.

The surrounding account says the fall of the Empire caused abandonment of the project and that the archives remained at the Hôtel de Soubise. It supplies no exact abandonment day, surviving foundation height, excavation volume or list of completed quarters. It cannot be converted into a statement that no preliminary works occurred.

## The later figures concern Soubise

The entry's address and first historical section concern the Hôtel de Soubise in the Marais. Its closing *Constructions nouvelles* passage on p30 reports works beginning in **September 1838**, names **Charles Lelong and Adolphe Gréterin**, says a voted million francs was spent across the **1838–1841** exercises, and identifies **800,000 francs** still needed for several tasks. It describes **1,000 square metres** of executed new construction, stone walls and vaults, iron roof structure and zinc roofing, followed by a prospective **1845** completion statement.

These are reported works, spending and specifications for the existing archive establishment and adjoining property. They are not quantities for the abandoned river-front palace. The money needed includes finishing, storage fittings, restoration and acquisition; adding it to the earlier palace allocation would combine different sites, periods and financial roles. The prospective 1845 sentence is not an inspected acceptance certificate.

## Source dependence and the unresolved works sequence

On p28 the authors explicitly say the following information was extracted **in part** from a brochure by **M. Dessalles**, an archives employee. The inspected statement gives neither the brochure's title nor its date. This is a named dependency to trace, not proof that every sentence derives from the same publication or that Dessalles witnessed the 1812 works. The online Wikisource transcription was used for navigation; it is not another independent witness.

The program's successive-quarter requirement gives a specific staged-construction possibility to test against the [1812 ceremony notice](ARCHIVES-FIRST-STONE-1812.md) and [1813 prospective foundation letter](CYGNES-NAPOLEON-1813-LETTER.md). It does not itself reconcile their chronology. Nor does a match between specified stone/iron and an excavated material identify its source: those materials are not diagnostic of this palace.

Next obtain the complete decree and its administrative context, identify the Dessalles brochure from bibliographic and original-page controls, and retrieve palace execution drawings, contractor accounts and acceptance records. Compare the dated physical footprint with the [undated site plan](ARCHIVES-PALAIS-SITE-PLAN.md), then with later excavation locations. The actual palace work extent, foundation association and deposit chronology remain unknown. No common catastrophe or deliberate rewriting mechanism follows; all twenty objectives remain active.
