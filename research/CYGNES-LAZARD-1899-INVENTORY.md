## What the inventory actually identifies

Printed page86 opens **Carton35**. The Orsay entry begins on87 and continues on88 before Carton36. These are historical inventory locators, not authenticated current item numbers. The earlier [current finding-aid text](ORSAY-LAZARE-1855.md) identifies D1Z88 for the corresponding alphabetical range, but a carton-to-current-item concordance has not been acquired.

| Description in the original inventory | Reported date | Role and limit |

| Letters patent authorizing the City to fill the channel separating the island from Gros-Caillou | 20 June1773 | Precise authorization target; not a completion certificate |
| Letter attributed to Napoleon I, described as completion of the quay | 26 March1813 | Exact correspondence target; description does not reveal whether the letter orders, discusses or certifies completion, or which quay section it covers |

The grant heading on87 reports 21March1722. This differs in date role from the1855entry’s March1721letters and 21March1722registration. The inventory heading alone does not authenticate the instrument’s creation date. Its Orsay stone-setting record is separately dated 6June1705; it cannot date the later island/channel operation.

## Selection and dependence matter

The complete two-page preface says the papers were gathered by Louis and Félix Lazare for their dictionary and donated by Montassier in1895. It expressly distinguishes originals from copies and says the inventory selects interesting dossiers containing complete acts rather than cataloging all approximately3,000–3,500dossiers. For acts before yearIX it supplies analyses; for later acts it lists those absent from a named official compilation. Asterisks mark acts already appearing in that compilation, not original-versus-copy status.

The preface also reports that many revolutionary documents derive from administrative registers destroyed in the archive fire, leaving Lazare copies. This is a general preservation statement. It does not establish that the1773authorization or1786sale is a copy, and does not license treating every missing item as destroyed. The inventory and dictionary share a documentary lineage; their agreement is not independent authentication.

The1786description strengthens the need to test a staged sequence, but supplies neither an actual fill quantity nor a dated sediment sample. The1813letter is a useful retrieval target precisely because its heading leaves execution uncertain. Retrieve these items and their plans, establish current identifiers and copy/original status, and register the parcels to tower foundations before assigning reported bones or masonry to them.
