# Lake Cahuilla: inland water is not automatically a marine passage

Research draft, 2026-10-08. C017; no independent review.

## Physical observations and their limits

[Waters (1983)](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=8973), pp. 375-378, describes alternating fine lake sediment and coarser river deposits near Indio. Figure 4 shows sections A-C with both conformable and erosional contacts. The text identifies freshwater shellfish; paired *Anodonta* shells were selected for dating. These are reported field observations, not specimens inspected here.

The author could trace upper lake units between A and B, but poorer exposure limited lower correlations; one unit was interpreted as eroded at B. Correlation to C was tentative and depended on dates. Consequently, the columns cannot be treated as a perfectly complete, independently dated basin-wide sequence. The original laboratory records, shell identifications and reservoir effects remain unaudited.

The 1983 reconstruction depicts an inland lake separate from the gulf. That drawing is an interpretation supported by the described deposits, not itself a second physical observation. Its 12 m shoreline and the later study's +13 m value need datum, location and survey comparison before any elevation change is inferred.

## Later chronology is partly dependent on historical accounts

[Rockwell et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2022.107456), inspected through the [NTU repository copy](https://dr.ntu.edu.sg/server/api/core/bitstreams/27c09858-8214-416b-8807-1c7c1321a8b1/content), combines radiocarbon information with stratigraphy, historical constraints and hydrological modeling. The abstract gives 423 compiled dates and final models using 151 or 149 dates. Selection and age inheritance require auditing; this is not a simple average of all determinations.

Section 2.2 explicitly describes Kino's account and Anza's later journey as constraints on the latest lake chronology. Section 5.4 discusses soils, oxidation and root casts as evidence relevant to drying, while acknowledging incomplete evidence for full desiccation between every lake. This audit has not reproduced either age model or extracted all specimens. Its findings must not be represented as independent confirmation of Kino's dates.

## Test consequence

Lake sediment and aquatic remains are evidence of water. Their environment, geometry and chronology determine whether they support a proposed marine strait. A lake supplied by a river and an ocean channel make different predictions about fossil assemblages, salinity indicators, sediment sources, outlet elevation and through-going connections.

This new case offers a physical comparison for C005, but no particular island-map strait has yet been geographically specified. It therefore cannot reject every possible former channel. Nor does the presence of these inland deposits establish one. A useful next step is specimen-level auditing of the freshwater attribution and paired shell/charcoal dating, followed by a dated transect across the proposed connection. Historical constraints must be removed or varied when using the age model to test those same histories.
