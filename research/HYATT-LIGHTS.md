# Hyatt lights: function, improvements and date roles

Research draft, 2026-10-08. This follows the **Hyatt Lights** annotation visually inspected on the Pioneer Building first-floor plan, S94. A product-family connection is plausible; an exact patent model, supplier or installed specimen has not been identified.

## What the technical records describe

| Record | Inspected evidence | Scope of the disclosure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [US 4,266, S95](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4266A/en) | PDF pp.2–3, specification and claim | Glass elements admit light through vault covers. Smaller protected lenses address breakage and abrasion of earlier large-glass covers. The claim concerns a particular arrangement of glass, supporting layers and protective projections. |
| [US 21,050, S96](https://patents.google.com/patent/US21050A/en) | PDF p.2, specification, claim and dates | Shaped glass redirects descending light laterally through reflection and refraction. The specification names Thaddeus Hyatt as assignor to Geo. Jackson & Co. |
| [Reissue 9,883, S97](https://patents.google.com/patent/USRE9883E/en) | PDF p.3, drawing sheet 3/Fig.7; PDF p.5, specification opening | A sidewalk can serve as the roof of an underground room. The section depicts a principal story, basement and room beneath the sidewalk, with light paths through the covering. |

The third record directly documents a designed way to create illuminated usable space below a sidewalk. Such a configuration does not require a formerly above-ground story to have been buried. This supplies a concrete competing mechanism for interpreting below-sidewalk spaces; it does not establish the origin of every Seattle areaway or demonstrate installation at the Pioneer Building.

The drawings and specification describe daylight transmission, not an energy source. No electric-generation mechanism appears in the inspected portions. Optical performance, load capacity and waterproofing assertions have not been experimentally reproduced here.

## Preserve dates and editions

S95's specification states a patent date of **November 12, 1845** and reissue **April 3, 1855, No.303**. Its final page also says **“First printed 1914.”** The inspected reproduction is therefore not evidence that this printed sheet physically existed in 1845. The underlying grant and later printing are distinct events; the original manuscript and reissue file remain uninspected.

S96's header gives **July 27, 1858**, while its closing text is dated **December 15, 1857**. The latter is recorded as a date in the specification, not silently converted into a verified filing date.

S97 states reissue **September 27, 1881**, application for reissue **September 5, 1881**, earlier reissue **August 6, 1878, No.8,363**, and original patent **August 27, 1867, No.68,332**. The 1867 record has now been compared on the limited points below; the 1878 version remains unchecked. Do not assume every passage or drawing was already present in 1867. The heading names **Elizabeth A. L. Hyatt, formerly Elizabeth A. Lake**, as assignee. That attribution must be retained, but it does not itself establish who designed or manufactured each component.

## Original patent versus reissue

[US 68,332 (S98)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US68332A/en), retrieved as a seven-page patent reproduction, was visually checked at PDF pages 2, 5 and 7. The header states August 27, 1867. Page 5 already describes the sidewalk as an underground-room roof and conversion of coal-vault space into finished basement accommodation. Drawing B on sheet 2 depicts the principal story, basement and under-sidewalk space with light paths. Thus this core design is present in the earlier record, rather than known solely from a later recital.

The 1867 opening expressly acknowledges prior basement enlargement and glass-covered areaways. Hyatt distinguishes his walkable connection from a raised skylight that obstructed access. His retrospective account also reports work at the New York Herald building in 1850 and a later basement-extension project, including failures to make joints watertight. These are the applicant's historical and performance claims, not independently inspected contracts or site observations. They identify follow-up records to seek; they cannot be counted as several independent witnesses.

This is a selected content comparison, not a full transcription, prosecution-history review or legal-validity assessment. It supports continuity of the core designed arrangement while documenting revision. Neither continuity nor revision supplies evidence of a global chronology break. The digital reproduction's physical printing date has not been established.

## Consequences for the investigation

The early specification explicitly discusses prior glass-covered openings. It cannot support a claim that Hyatt originated all underground daylighting. Conversely, an improvement upon earlier technology is not evidence of recovery from a lost civilization. Establishing that stronger proposition would require an identifiable older artifact and a documented chain of transmission.

For the Pioneer Building, obtain the light-panel detail, purchase or installation record, and a location-matched material survey. Compare glass geometry, support construction and markings with dated product documents. Until then, the plan annotation and these patent disclosures are a functional comparison, not proof of a specific installation or its age. They supply no sediment thickness, burial date or global chronological correction.
