# What the original Sanpoil record says about outlet age

2026-10-11. C008 / S163, compared with S29. Selected source scans and foldouts inspected; no independent field verification or model run.

[Atwater's original report](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1661/report.pdf), printedp37/PDF45, argues that high lake levels with a blocked outlet should produce thinner, finer, less erosive flood beds than a low lake with an open outlet. The lowest known Manila Creek beds, around395–405m in borehole2, are described as thick, coarse and erosive. The author infers that a deeply cut outlet probably preceded the known sequence. This is an interpretation of sediment response, not a direct excavation date.

The original [Plate2](../sources/originals/missoula/sanpoil-1986/plate-2.pdf) supplies the generalized composite; [Plate3](../sources/originals/missoula/sanpoil-1986/plate-3.pdf) supplies detailed columns. Its lower borehole2 section marks a questioned basal unconformity and probable bedrock. Its legend notes estimated elevation control and uncertain exact elevations for partially recovered material. The road reference is466.5m estimated from a1:2400map with1.5m contours. The report uses465m for the present outlet; S29 later uses approximately470m.

These observations and qualifiers are preserved separately in the [structured evidence record](../data/sanpoil-outlet-evidence.json). Plate overview inspection does not constitute a complete bed-by-bed correlation audit. No statistical elevation uncertainty is derived from contour interval.

## What must be tested before adopting the inference

The inference requires a sufficiently diagnostic relationship between lake level and the observed sediment. A numerical test should hold flood forcing, sediment supply and routing explicit while varying the receiving lake and outlet state. Without that comparison, a thick bed cannot independently determine all those variables. This is a required discrimination test, not evidence that any particular alternative occurred.

It also requires the recorded lowermost beds to represent the relevant beginning of the flood history. A questioned basal unconformity and uncertain core recovery keep earlier missing deposition possible; they do not demonstrate a missing flood or license an invented early event. Recover original drilling notes, core photographs and sample identifiers, and check contacts across the mapped sections. The plate is a published field synthesis, not access to the cores themselves.

Finally, an inference that incision preceded known deposition is a relative sequence. Extending it to a particular glacial period depends on the section's chronology and regional correlation. Those are separate requirements. The [existing chronology audit](MISSOULA-STRATIGRAPHY.md) retains detrital-wood, annuality, ash and composite-correlation limits.

The465m and approximately470m values come from different source treatments. Their5m numerical difference is not measured uplift, erosion or a datum conversion. A valid comparison needs authenticated vertical references, locations, epochs and survey precision.

## Consequence for reconstruction

The [outlet-history conflict](MISSOULA-CONTROL-SEMANTICS.md) now has an original, inspectable basis: a sedimentological inference with explicit source limitations. It remains distinct from a hydraulic model's need to restrict an outlet. Neither interpretation may be treated as independently proving the other. Ice blockage of an incised channel remains different from an unretreated rock barrier.

Next test the bed-response prediction and known-section completeness against the original records, then compare those results with authenticated outlet geometries. The exact Babcock noncrossing observation remains missing. No common catastrophe date, executed physical reconstruction or prospective success follows from recovering these plates.
