# Seattle: John Graf's street-grading dispute

Research draft, 2026-10-08 (America/New_York). Case C010; source S21. No independent review. This concerns the northeast corner of Fifth and Terrace, lots 5 and 8 in block 36 of Boren's Addition. It does not establish how the Pioneer Square areaways in C001 were formed.

## Original record and access

Seattle Municipal Archives' [City of Seattle Records transcription project, file 991085](https://fromthepage.com/sma-archives/city-of-seattle-records/991085) supplies twelve page views with facsimiles and indexed transcriptions. The engineer's letter on page 8 and the counsel letter's date on page 9 were visually checked; the petition on pages 11-12 was also visually inspected. Other pages were read as transcriptions, not fully checked against their images. This is one archival packet containing different voices, not twelve independent confirmations.

The [archive catalog target](https://archives.seattle.gov/digital-collections/index.php/Detail/objects/4925) returned 404 during retrieval; its search-indexed metadata identified series 1802-04, box 9, folder 8. Treat that container locator as catalog-level evidence. Direct automated retrieval of FromThePage failed, but the browser displayed its public scans without login. The facsimiles remain linked at their host; this repository does not claim to preserve copies of them.

Gardner's stated arithmetic is internally consistent: **121 + 0.35 - 116 = 5.35 feet**, the floor height above the reported present grade. The difference between the two stated grades is 5 feet. Neither figure is a measured deposit thickness. The historical datum's physical benchmark and relationship to modern vertical datums are unknown here.

Graf's 12-foot allegation and Gardner's figures must remain separate. They refer to differently described positions and quantities. A plan and longitudinal/cross sections are needed before calling them either a contradiction or a resolved agreement.

## Source corrections and unresolved details

- Page 11's indexed transcription omits **nearly** from the scan's description of wrecked buildings. This audit follows the scan and does not infer total destruction.
- The counsel letter on page 9 visibly bears **November 21, 1890**. The search-indexed catalog notes gave October 21. The scan supports November for this letter; the cause of the catalog discrepancy is unknown.
- Page 11 itemizes at least $6,000 for buildings/improvements and $4,000 for land; page 12 visibly types a total of $1,000. That apparent inconsistency is retained, not silently corrected to $10,000. None is an independently appraised or awarded loss.
- The petition's October 28 reference is not a complete date. Packet dates and the engineer/counsel letters supply context, but this audit does not assign the petition an exact creation date.
- [Page 3](https://fromthepage.com/sma-archives/city-of-seattle-records/991085/display/1688871) provides a transcribed ordinance index. Ambiguous identifiers remain leads until checked against the handwriting and actual ordinance books.

## What this changes in the investigation

This is contemporary evidence that a resident and city officers disputed street grading, property levels and resulting damage. It supplies a named resident's account of harm alongside the official response. The complaint challenges any effortless story that urban regrading only improved conditions; the city's assertions still require checking.

The proposed local mechanism is excavation and possible slope failure, including alleged drainage alteration. That differs physically from a depositional mud flood or a deliberately raised roadway. Even if the slide is confirmed, moved earth alone would not identify a worldwide event. Conversely, the packet does not exclude a natural contribution to slope failure and does not settle the city's liability.

No site visit, sediment section, deposit date, map reconstruction or final adjudication has been obtained. This case cannot close C001's missing original fill records or be combined with the later Denny Hill project as one event.

## Discriminating next checks

1. Retrieve the actual grade ordinances, benchmark descriptions, survey books and dated construction records; tie each quoted elevation to a surveyed position.
2. Locate pre/post-grading photographs or plans for these lots and any drainage/retaining-work records. Check whether the requested replacement earth was installed.
3. Trace council minutes and any arbitration/court file to establish what was investigated, accepted or rejected. Preserve Graf's claims even if the final decision went against him.
4. If physical sections survive, compare cut geometry, displaced slope material and later fill with separately dated street work. Do not infer sediment character from the word slide alone.
