## What the marsh passage actually says

Printed p.28 describes rivers draining Shoal-water Bay, prairie lands beside them, highest-winter-tide flooding of lands nearest the bay, salt water, cultivation, grass and hay. It names the Whil-a-pah, Palux and Nasal rivers in the surrounding passage. This is a broad descriptive account rather than a measured transect at an identified modern soil locality.

Comparison with the quotation in S245, printed p.95:

These differences do not establish a cause, deceptive intent or a chronology change. The inspected copy is not necessarily the exact physical copy used by the report's authors. No attribution of the differences to editing, transcription or edition variation is proven.

The winter-flooding description is qualitatively compatible with the report's high-marsh interpretation. Swan supplies no tidal datum, species-level grass inventory, surveyed elevation, exact mapped footprint or start/end measurements for soil formation. Reworking, local land management and ecological change have not been quantified.

Swan does not date a1700 earthquake in this inspected passage. The report's approximately150-year recovery inference combines its own event chronology with Swan's later landscape description and additional maps. Therefore the account is historical evidence of reported marsh conditions, not an independent earthquake date, a150-year minimum, or a growth-rate experiment. A quantitative reconstruction must verify that the historical footprint and relevant buried soils are the same places.

## Adjacent buried-forest description retained

Printed p.27 reports sandy-clay banks containing shell strata and buried ancient forest remains around much of the bay, contrasting a low marshy/sandy western peninsula. It also describes steep banks modified by bay waves. These are relevant reported coastal observations and should not be discarded because the passage is used for marsh recovery.

However, the inspected paragraph gives no sample identifiers, exact exposure coordinates, section drawing, radiometric age, growth-position determination or deposition mechanism. Swan's qualitative age characterization is his interpretation, not measured elapsed time. The high-bank description is not automatically the same Holocene high-marsh sequence mapped in S245. Correlating the two requires identifiable exposures and actual stratigraphy; no global flood or rapid geography change is inferred here.

The adjacent wildlife list is likewise a historical report, not a specimen-verified occurrence dataset. In particular, historical common names cannot establish modern species identities or connect these animals to Arctic fossil samples. No animal coordinates or mortality dates are created from the list.
