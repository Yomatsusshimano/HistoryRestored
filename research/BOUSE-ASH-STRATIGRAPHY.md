# Bouse ash-to-bed correlation audit

2026-10-08. Selected original descriptions and figures inspected; no field verification or independent review.

The Lawlor eruption age does not automatically date every Bouse fossil assemblage. The present evidence chain has four separate links: eruption dating, tephra identification, timing of ash deposition, and correlation from the ash-bearing section to a target fossil bed. This audit locates weaknesses in the last two links without treating uncertainty as evidence for a preferred alternative chronology.

## Amboy: distinguish parts of the ash bed

[Miller et al. 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262874294_Bouse_Formation_in_the_Bristol_basin_near_Amboy_California_USA), S200, p.465, describes interval B as 40–45 cm of ash covering algal mounds. The lowest 5 cm is particularly clean and well sorted; silt and biotite higher in the bed suggest reworking. Their interval C contains diatoms and ostracodes. The Lawlor identification relies on earlier tephrochronology and Harvey's zircon work. Selected author-uploaded full-text portions were read; the scan and figure-to-sample positions remain unverified.

Our inference: a blanket description of the entire ash as either pristine fallout or wholly reworked would exceed this account. Recover which height and sublayer supplied each dated/correlated sample. Sorting and draping are useful observations, but they alone do not quantify a deposition lag. Fossils above the ash are stratigraphically later if the section is intact; the length of that interval requires further evidence.

## Buzzards Peak and Milpitas: identification versus proposed matching

[Dorsey et al. 2016](https://pages.uoregon.edu/rdorsey/Downloads/Dorseyetal2016DS.pdf), S199, pp.149–151 and Figures 6–8, distinguishes:

| Section | Reported ash status | Critical qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BP1 | Identified as Lawlor | Ash base is not exposed |
| BP2 | Likely Lawlor | Undated; correlation proposed |
| C27 | Possible Lawlor, 10–15 cm thick | Discontinuous bed beneath upper limestone |

The link from Buzzards Peak carbonate to the upper limestone is a hypothesis in this account. Figure 8 restores the base of the upper limestone to horizontal, uses variable vertical exaggeration and excludes later deformation. It is a stratigraphic interpretation, not measured former terrain.

The [2018 follow-up](https://pages.uoregon.edu/rdorsey/BasinAnalysis/BasinPapers/Dorsey%20etal%202018.pdf), S201, pp.18–19, retains the undated BP2 and C27 assignments. It proposes correlation using lateral changes in sedimentary character. Weathered, calcite-encrusted residues from Buzzards Peak did not permit microfossil correlation to other Bouse sections. Thus this later paper does not supply the missing fossil-based bridge. These papers share authors, sections and earlier dating inputs; they are not independent replications.

## What would discriminate the alternatives

1. Obtain original Amboy and Buzzards Peak sampling logs. Match each glass/zircon sample to the measured bed, including basal versus reworked ash. Keep uncertain matches unresolved.
2. Find subsequent chemical analyses or dates for C27 and BP2. A published similarity claim is not a completed analytical match. Compare possible alternative tephras using the same criteria applied to BP1.
3. Trace the contact and sedimentary relationships to the exact Hart Mine Wash fossil samples already in the inventory. A common formation name, nominal elevation or similar carbonate texture cannot substitute for that mapping.
4. Evaluate both proposed placements of the ash-bearing carbonate: basal carbonate or upper bioclastic member. Record the observations each explains and the observations each would contradict. Separately test unconformities and possible missing intervals.
5. Seek independent age constraints above and below the target horizon. An ancient clast or underlying eruption supplies an older bound unless primary deposition and a tight temporal connection are demonstrated. A younger bed can contain older material; invoking that possibility requires actual reworking evidence and a bounded lag.

These tests concern the regional record. Neither tentative correlations nor a securely dated regional marine deposit establish the northern waterway depicted on an island-California map. The [map audit](CALIFORNIA-MAP-AUDIT.md) still requires its own route, deformation and shoreline tests.
