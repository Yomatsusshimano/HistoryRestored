# Later sample names and limits on sedimentation ages

2026-10-09. C005/S329. Selected original supplement inspected; no independent review.

The publisher-linked [GSA Data Repository Item2017396](https://doi.org/10.1130/2017396) belongs to [Knott et al.'s Death Valley study](https://doi.org/10.1130/B31690.1). The institutional metadata dates the journal issue to2018; the supplement's reference uses2017. These are publication-stage labels, not geological dates. The main article was not acquired as a scan. The original23page supplement and companion workbook were downloaded normally and matched publisher MD5 values. Only PDF2/4/19 were rendered and visually inspected; the workbook was acquired but not analyzed.

JRK-DV-39 and104 are the same printed identifiers grouped as lower Nomlaki in the [2001 table audit](ARTISTS-DRIVE-NOMLAKI.md). Their later labels establish documentary continuity to the tuff of Artists Drive. They do not supply new independent dates. The source heading concerns similar or correlative layers: adjacency in this table is not an authenticated correlation of every row. In particular, the two core descriptions do not specify the exact2008extrapolation calculation or make their depths interchangeable with section heights.

The selected rows do not resolve the mixed FLV-119-WW populations or establish a numerical rare-earth comparison. The precise Knott et al.2008 predecessor to retrieve is *Reconstructing late Pliocene to middle Pleistocene Death Valley lakes and river systems as a test of pupfish (Cyprinodontidae) dispersal hypotheses*, DOI10.1130/2008.2439(01); its citation was text-read on supplement PDF5, not visually inspected here. This is distinct from House et al.'s2008chapter already audited.

## How the authors make correlations

Methods PDF2 report normalized microprobe oxide comparisons against the USGS database. Their general similarity calculation uses SiO2, Al2O3, Fe2O3, CaO and TiO2; selected other units use MnO instead of TiO2. Correlations also use stratigraphic position, magnetic evidence and argon ages. This describes the authors' procedure; it does not reproduce particular coefficients, uncertainty or acceptance decisions. A chemically similar named sample is not enough to transfer a regional age.

## Approximate ages are not Gaussian measurements

The sedimentation-rate discussion on PDF4 expressly warns that deposition in fans and playas is episodic and that magnetic boundaries are inferred between available samples. It illustrates the limitation with a different unit, the tuff of Curry canyon: reported18.4cm/ka rate within a3.596-3.330Ma magnetozone and a sampling-related age range3.57-3.51Ma, summarized as approximately3.54Ma. The authors reject expressing this as3.54±0.03Ma because it would overstate precision.

That example is not the age of lower Nomlaki and not a reproduced interpolation. Its spread is not treated as one standard deviation or a confidence interval. Nor is the warning adopted as a universal rule against estimating uncertainty: an explicit model of sampling, hiatuses, accumulation variability and dating error could quantify uncertainty, but no such model is executed here. It cannot replace the different2008personal-communication error recipe.

Next inspect the2008predecessor and recover quantitative trace-element data and the exact Artists Drive age-depth inputs. Retain the later names when retrieving records, but keep all dated minerals, correlated beds, core depths and final depositional events separate. No historical event date or global reconstruction follows from this documentary continuity.
