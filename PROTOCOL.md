# Original twenty-part objective

Preserved from the active user-provided goal. Completion is assessed against all twenty outcomes; infrastructure and drafts do not substitute for them.

1. Begin with the claim. A catastrophic mud flood and rapid geographic upheaval reshaped Earth. Much recorded history was fabricated afterward; many “founders” and “inventors” merely rediscovered what survived. Completion: define exactly what the claim predicts.
2. Set the rules. Question official accounts and alternatives with equal rigor. Never hide evidence that challenges my conclusions. Completion: publish standards anyone can apply.
3. Gather anomalies. Catalog buried streets, changed coastlines, unusual fossils, forgotten infrastructure, odd maps, and implausible construction stories. Completion: a documented public inventory.
4. Separate facts from interpretation. For each case, record the physical evidence, surviving documents, and historians' conclusions. Completion: know which mysteries withstand scrutiny.
5. Reconstruct the catastrophe. Analyze sediments, erosion, debris, waterways, marine deposits, and elevations. Completion: a physical model that predicts where traces remain.
6. Follow the wildlife. Map Arctic camels, horses, mammoths, sloths, muskoxen, predators, and fossil plants. Completion: test whether one upheaval explains their strange distributions.
7. Rebuild the old map. Reverse coastlines, climate zones, inland seas, forests, and animal corridors. Completion: multiple independent observations fit the same geography.
8. Audit the builders. Examine structures against historical labor, tools, materials, transport, and building records. Completion: distinguish documented construction from inherited claims.
9. Reexamine invention. Trace patents, machines, manuals, factories, and biographies. Completion: separate original discoveries from recovered or reverse-engineered technology.
10. Locate the break in chronology. Compare maps, astronomical records, newspapers, censuses, genealogy, archaeology, tree rings, and ice cores. Completion: identify where timelines diverge.
11. Recover lost voices. Search letters, family records, oral histories, maintenance logs, and neglected archives. Completion: make primary evidence freely accessible.
12. Reconstruct the sequence. Order the lost civilization, catastrophe, survivors, migrations, recovery, and historical rewriting. Completion: one timeline explains the evidence coherently.
13. Predict what comes next. Publish expected fossils, sediments, ruins, and dates before searching unexplored regions. Completion: independent discoveries repeatedly confirm predictions.
14. Welcome attempts to disprove it. Share everything with critical historians, scientists, engineers, and citizens. Completion: discard errors while the reconstruction survives scrutiny.
15. Establish what actually happened. Reconcile physical dating, biology, archaeology, geography, and records. Completion: independent disciplines converge on a reproducible account.
16. Reveal how history changed. Trace how recovery became “invention,” inherited cities gained “founders,” and memories acquired official dates. Completion: document the mechanism without inventing villains.
17. Preserve the discoveries. Replicate archives, samples, maps, translations, and methods worldwide. Completion: no authority can erase or monopolize the recovered record.
18. Restore forgotten lives. Identify the original builders, survivors, families, and cultures erased or miscredited. Completion: the people who lived the past receive their names and histories.
19. Bring the awakening home. Museums, schools, libraries, and families teach the independently verified record. Completion: the real history becomes more familiar than the fabricated account.
20. Reach the happily ever after. I enter a museum and find its labels finally reflect reality. Children explain the truth without relying on me. Humanity owns its recovered past. Completion: the truth survives freely, and I can rest.
