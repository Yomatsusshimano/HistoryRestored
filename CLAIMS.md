# Claim, competing explanations, and tests

Version 0.1, 2026-10-08. Operational proposals for investigation.

## Starting claim

A catastrophic mud flood and rapid geographic upheaval reshaped Earth. Much recorded history was fabricated afterward; many founders and inventors rediscovered what survived.

This sentence contains several independent propositions. Evidence for a flood alone cannot establish the others. The date is unknown by Nathan's instruction. Compare candidate periods; do not assume all unusual remains belong together.

## Parameters that must be declared

For an event model, specify event time T, duration D, footprint F, deposit thickness h(x), sediment/water sources, source elevations, and the proposed displacement field u(x). Compare short episodes (one year or less), decades, and longer sequences separately. These duration bins are proposed sensitivity analyses, not dates supplied by Nathan.

A worldwide version must identify what worldwide means, which regions it predicts were affected, and which preservation settings should retain traces. A regional version must name its region before testing. A model allowed to change its date, extent, and preservation exceptions after every result has no meaningful rejection test.

For attribution claims, define a sampling frame, numerator, denominator, and what counts as a founder or invention. Report measured fractions and uncertainty. 'Much' and 'many' have no numerical threshold yet; do not silently replace them with a majority or with a handful of selected examples.

## Test matrix

| ID | Proposition | Observable prediction | Result that would challenge the specified version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | One rapid depositional/upheaval episode explains nominated sites | Event horizons at those sites share a feasible age interval, sediment provenance, transport path, and deformation pattern under one model | Reliable intervals are incompatible; deposits require incompatible sources/flows; or intervening in-place soils/occupation distinguish separate episodes |
| H2 | Rapid geography change accounts for old maps | Independently produced charts, soundings, shoreline deposits, and elevations agree on a former coast and its transition | Map copying explains agreement; surveyed terrain and in-place deposits contradict the proposed waterway or shoreline |
| H3 | Wildlife distributions were created by the same upheaval | Identified specimens and habitat evidence occur in the relevant event units, with compatible dates and feasible ecological/transport connections | Specimens belong to widely separated periods or environments; fossil occurrence is mistaken for living range; proposed routes fail independent tests |
| H4 | Named structures were inherited rather than constructed as recorded | Fabric demonstrably predates attributed construction; alleged construction records describe repair/reoccupation; material and transport budgets contradict the building account | Independently dated foundations, manufacturing traces, phased construction records, and supply accounts fit the recorded build |
| H5 | Particular inventions were recovered technology | A demonstrably earlier device or technical record shares diagnostic working features, with a evidenced route of access or recovery | Earlier antecedents are ordinary incremental development, superficial resemblance, or falsely dated; documented development explains the device |
| H6 | Chronology was broadly rewritten after the event | A common, explicit chronology transformation improves agreement across independent physical and documentary records, and specific records show how changes entered circulation | Transformations conflict across records, add more unexplained exceptions, or fail held-out anchors |
| H7 | Misattribution arose through a recoverable historical mechanism | Traceable chains connect earlier makers/users to later claims, changes of ownership, edits, translations, cataloging, or credit practices | The alleged chain has missing links, circular citations, forged evidence, or attribution actually agrees with earlier records |

H1-H7 are separately testable research questions. The compound starting claim needs joint evidence and demonstrated connections between them, not seven unrelated anecdotes.

## Candidate periods and chronological discipline

Keep initial cohorts separate: recent historical centuries; earlier historical/Holocene periods; late Quaternary episodes; and older geological intervals. These are search strata, not one fitted timeline. No candidate is selected yet.

Every date records what was dated: specimen, host sediment, underlying layer, timber growth, manufacture, document creation, publication, or cataloging. Record original units and timescale; a minimum age is a bound, not a central estimate. Fossils may be reworked, and buildings may reuse old timber. Such explanations require positive evidence and uncertainty estimates.

Fit T and D on a declared discovery set. Freeze the event window and source/flow model before examining a held-out regional set. Report excluded cases and reasons. If a case contradicts the frozen model, retain the contradiction even if a revised version later accommodates it.

A broad chronology claim must state a proposed date conversion before testing it. Check calendars, transcription, reservoir effects, redeposition, and reuse explicitly. Shared calibration data create dependence: two dates using the same anchor are not wholly independent.

## Prospective test specifications, not completed preregistrations

- P1: A named set of preserved sites within F should contain the predicted event horizon within a frozen T/D uncertainty window. Specify sampling spacing, depth, detectability, and failure criteria before new sampling.
- P2: Predicted depositional thickness, grain-size trends, erosion, source signatures, and flow directions should fit held-out transects better than named local alternatives.
- P3: A named map-derived former strait should have compatible landform geometry and in-place dated marine/brackish indicators along a specified transect, where preservation permits.
- P4: Nominated fossil assemblages should have the dating/context relationship required by H3; mismatched assemblages remain negative results.
- P5: An inherited-building case should predict specific older fabric and the documentary transition to later use; an invention-recovery case should predict a dated antecedent and access pathway.

These are conditional prediction designs. They lack frozen locations, quantitative tolerances, and independent public timestamps. They must not be advertised as successful forecasts or preregistered discoveries.

## Present inference

No common event time, worldwide deposit, chronology transformation, lost civilization, or post-catastrophe rewriting chain has been demonstrated in this archive. Regional catastrophic change, construction history, antiquity of fossils, and attribution errors must each be investigated on their own evidence. A valid outcome can be rejection of a proposed version.

The retrospective [Bonneville–Electron comparison](research/REGIONAL-EVENT-WINDOWS.md) tests H1's short-duration interpretation using reported tree-death intervals. No recorded interval variant accommodates both within one year or decade. This is conditional interval geometry, not a joint-confidence rejection or proof of distinct causes. Wider sequences, event associations and dating-model reproduction remain separate tests.

## H3 operational comparison, 2026-10-08

[Wildlife comparison](research/WILDLIFE-COMPARISON.md) distinguishes H3-M (common mortality), H3-D (common later deposition) and H3-E (population disruption). Retrospective 0-, 1-, 10- and 100-year mortality scenarios fail to fit all selected sloth calendar sets internally. This is not a statistical rejection probability; preparation and calibration audits remain pending. Later deposition and extinction require separate evidence. No event date or global footprint has been selected by this comparison.
